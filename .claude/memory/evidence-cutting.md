## Cutting without losing muscle
| Topic | Evidence | Strength |
|---|---|---|
| Deficit size | Murphy & Koehler 2022 (Scand J Med Sci Sports, meta-regression of RCTs) [V]: a deficit blunts lean-mass gain (ES −0.57) but not strength; deficits **> ~500 kcal/day** were associated with no lean gain. Treat 500 as a soft guide | strong |
| Rate | Garthe 2011 RCT, PMID 21558571 [V 10-28] (n=24 elite athletes, 4 lifts/wk, DEXA): 0.7 %/wk **gained** LBM +2.1 % vs −0.2 % at 1.4 %/wk, fat loss 31 vs 21 % (`tiny n`, athletes). Helms 2014, PMID 24864135 [V] (narrative review, natural bodybuilders): 0.5–1 %/wk, slower as you get leaner (expert opinion). **Superseded for his caps by [[evidence-rapid-loss-harms]]** (0.7 %/wk, 0.5 % ≤84 kg) | moderate |
| Protein | Morton 2018 BJSM, PMID 28698222 [V 10-28] (MA 49 RCTs, n=1,863, lifting ≥6 wk, FFM): break point **1.62 g/kg/day, CI 1.03–2.20**; protein adds only +0.30 kg FFM; mostly non-deficit trials, so a floor, not a ceiling (authors: ~2.2 "prudent"); `industry` (dairy grants). Helms 2014 IJSNEM, PMID 24092765 [V 10-28] (SR 6 studies, trained lean adults, men ≤23 % BF, in a deficit): **2.3–3.1 g/kg FFM**, higher the leaner and the harder the cut. Total daily intake matters most; 3–5 feedings. Trommelen 2023 [V]: 100 g meal still used, so no hard per-meal cap | strong |
| Fat | Whittaker & Wu 2021 J Steroid Biochem Mol Biol, PMID 33741447 [V 10-28] (MA 6 intervention studies, n=206 men, serum hormones): low-fat vs high-fat → total T **SMD −0.38 (−0.75 to −0.01)**, free T −0.37; LH/SHBG unchanged; `tiny n`, `surrogate`, mostly Western non-athletes; 2026 erratum not read. **The floor number (~0.5–1 g/kg / 20–30 % energy, his 0.7 g/kg) is NOT in this source**: it is a conservative judgement, not a tested threshold | weak–moderate |
| Diet breaks | MATADOR (Byrne 2018, obese men) [V]: 2 wk on / 2 wk maintenance → more loss, smaller REE drop. Peos 2021 ICECAP, PMID 33587549 [V 10-28] (RCT n=61 resistance-trained adults at ~25 % BF, not lean; 12 wk: fat mass 15.3 vs 18.0 kg, FFM 56.7 vs 56.7 kg, both ns; breaks lowered hunger) and Poon 2025 meta-analysis of 12 RCTs [V]: **no body-composition advantage**. Breaks help adherence, mood and hunger, not muscle | moderate |
| Adaptation | RMR, leptin, T3 and testosterone fall in long cuts, most in lean men; contest-prep men recover T in ~1–3 months of eating more (Frontiers Nutr 2024 review [V]). Detail in [[evidence-metabolic-adaptation]] | moderate |
| Low energy availability | IOC 2023 REDs consensus, Mountjoy 2023 BJSM, PMID 37752011: the **<30 kcal/kg FFM/day** figure and "uncertain for men" are **still unverified** (10-28: abstract has no number, full text paywalled). Male data: Koehler 2016, PMID 26852783 [V] (crossover n=6, 4 d): 15 vs 40 kcal/kg FFM → leptin −53 %, insulin −34 %, T3/testosterone/IGF-1 unchanged; `tiny n`, `short`. EA = (intake − exercise kcal) ÷ FFM, not intake ÷ FFM. His floor (25 for men) is in [[evidence-rapid-loss-harms]] | strong (consensus) |
| Cardio | Schumann 2022 meta-analysis (43 studies) [V]: concurrent training does not reduce hypertrophy or max strength; small hit to explosive strength. Walking is the lowest-cost option | strong |
| Sleep | Nedeltcheva 2010 Ann Intern Med, PMID 20921542 [V 10-28] (randomised crossover n=10, 7 men, age 41, BMI 27, 14 d deficit): 5.5 h vs 8.5 h → fat lost 0.6 vs 1.4 kg (**−55 %**), FFM lost 2.4 vs 1.5 kg (**+60 %**); `tiny n`, `short`, non-lifters. Wang 2018 *Sleep* RCT (n=36, 8 wk, PMID 29438540) [V]: ~1 h less on 5 nights/wk → same total loss, smaller fat share. Sleep loss does not lower metabolic rate (Markwald 2013, PMID 23818627, EE +5 % but intake up more [M]); its cost is fat-vs-lean partition and appetite | moderate |
| When to stop | Stop signs: strength down across several lifts, persistent fatigue, worse sleep, mood, illness, or target reached. **Libido is not a signal for him** ([[feel-checkin]]) | expert |
