Darren asked (2026-10-20) whether body fat affects muscle growth, wanting recent (≈2023–26) experimental data.
**[V]** = checked online (abstract or reliable summary), **[M]** = model memory. Direct 2023–26 trials on
"BF level as a moderator of hypertrophy" are **thin**; most evidence is indirect.

## The claim and where it comes from
"Fatter people build less muscle / should cut before bulking" rests on the **P-ratio** (share of a weight change
that is lean tissue). Forbes 1987/2000 (sedentary people, cross-sectional) [V via summary]: higher fat mass → a
smaller lean share of any gain or loss. Hall 2007 Br J Nutr [V] extended it to large changes. **No lifting in that
data.** The partitioning effect is about *where a surplus goes*, not how well muscle responds to training.

## Evidence
| Question | Evidence | Strength |
|---|---|---|
| Does higher BF blunt hypertrophy from training? | Lopez 2022 MSSE meta-regression, 85 interventions, 2,537 overweight/obese adults [V via SBS summary]: mean +0.8 kg lean; **baseline BMI not a significant moderator** (slope −0.11 univariable p=0.11, +0.22 adjusted p=0.11). Bigger caloric restriction and age → smaller lean gain | moderate |
| Obese men can still grow | Bagheri 2024 MSSE RCT, 60 overweight/obese men, 12 wk, 3×/wk [V abstract]: muscle mass, FFM, strength, power up; fat %, myostatin, CRP, TNF-α down; follistatin, adiponectin up | moderate |
| Acute muscle protein synthesis in obesity | Beals 2018 J Physiol [V abstract]: lifting + feeding raised myofibrillar MPS **less** in obese adults (blunted p70S6K/4E-BP1). Hulston 2018 [V]: active young obese vs lean similar after lifting. 2019 middle-aged men: no basal/fed difference [V]. Reviews (2019/2021) [V]: obesity anabolic resistance driven by insulin resistance, intramuscular lipid, inflammation, and worse when **inactive** | low–moderate, mixed |
| Muscle quality | Intramuscular fat (myosteatosis) tracks insulin resistance and lower strength per kg; training lowers it (2026-dated narrative review, [V partial]) | low |
| Surplus size decides fat gain | Helms 2023 Sports Med Open [V]: trained lifters, maintenance vs +5 % vs +15 % for 8 wk → no hypertrophy/strength difference, more fat at +15 %. Smith/Tinsley 2021 [V]: trained men (~14 % BF), 6 wk overfeeding, ~0.55 %/wk gain ≈ all FFM on average, huge individual spread (R² 0.18–0.40) | moderate |
| Extra energy still adds protein (untrained) | Hatamoto 2024 Clin Nutr RCT, 23 young men, 6 wk [V partial abstract]: +40 % surplus raised body protein 0.44 kg (+3.7 %); +10 % protein-only arm smaller. Not lifters | low transfer |
| Recomp at higher BF | Vargas-Molina 2026 Eur J Appl Physiol RCT, trained, BMI ~26, 10 wk, protein 2.5 g/kg [V abstract]: maintenance **and** ~−250 kcal both lost fat and kept/gained FFM vs habitual diet. Murphy & Koehler 2022 [V, see [[evidence-cut-and-training]]]: deficit >~500 kcal kills lean gain | moderate |
| Hormones | Biernikiewicz 2025 J Sex Med meta-analysis (28 studies, overweight/obese men) [V abstract]: diet weight loss → large testosterone rise (d 1.39). 2022 CR meta-analysis [V via summary]: T effect of CR depends on BMI (rises in obese, can fall in lean). Effect is mostly an obesity-range story | moderate |

## Verdict (Opus, 2026-10-20)
- **In the range he lives in (~15–25 % BF, active, insulin-sensitive), body fat itself does not measurably slow
  muscle growth.** Training (volume, effort), protein and energy balance decide it. The "blunting" evidence is for
  obesity with insulin resistance and inactivity, and even there trained obese men still grow.
- **What BF really changes is the fat cost of a surplus**: higher BF + big surplus → more of the gain is fat. That
  is a reason to keep a lean bulk small (+5–10 %, ≤0.25–0.5 %/wk), not a hard "must be lean first" rule.
- **Higher BF makes recomposition easier**: a modest deficit (≤~250–500 kcal) or maintenance with high protein and
  hard training can add muscle while losing fat. So his current gentle cut and the 12-14 → 01-22 maintenance block
  are real muscle-building windows if he trains hard, not just "holding" phases.
- **The plan stays as is** ([[cutting-goal-2026]]): finish at 15–18 % then lean-bulk. The reason is aesthetics
  (proportion, [[performance-first]]) and less fat to cut later, not that fat blocks growth.
- Don't over-read Forbes for him: it is sedentary data and gives no hard BF cutoff.
