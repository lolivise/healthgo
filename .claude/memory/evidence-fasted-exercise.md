Darren asked (2026-10-16) whether fasted cardio is better for fat loss. Answered in chat; this is the distilled base.
**[V]** = abstract/full text checked online, **[S]** = secondary report only, primary not opened.

## Verdict
**Half true.** Fasted exercise burns more fat *during* the session, but 24 h fat oxidation and long-term fat loss
are no different from fed exercise. The daily/weekly energy deficit decides fat loss. All studies are small
(tens of people), mostly young, ≤ a few months. The direction of the evidence is consistent.

## Where the claim comes from
- Overnight fast → low insulin, low glycogen → more fat used as fuel. Real mechanism.
- Vieira 2016 (Br J Nutr, meta-analysis, 27 studies) [V]: fasted aerobic exercise burns ≈ +3 g fat per session
  (≈27 kcal), significant only below ~70 % VO₂max. Acute outcome only; the authors warn against extrapolating.
- The body compensates later in the day (more fat during, more carbohydrate after), so 24 h totals even out.

## Key studies
| Study | Design | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Kazeminasab 2025, Clin Nutr ESPEN [V abstract] | meta-analysis, 28 RCTs, 302 healthy adults, acute | higher fat oxidation during exercise ≠ higher 24 h fat oxidation or fat loss; not metabolically better |
| Hofstätter 2025, Physiol Rep [V] | 8 wk RCT, n=36, 3×60 min Fatmax cycling: 16 h fasted vs carb snack vs normal diet | fat oxidation improved in all groups, no group difference; fat mass ↓ in all, no difference; fasted group weight +0.46 kg |
| Vieira 2025, IJSNEM [S] | 12 wk RCT, n≈28, resistance training, fasted 10–12 h vs carb meal 1–2 h before, diet controlled | strength and power gains equal |
| 2025 resistance-training meta-analysis (J Bodywork Mov Ther, S1360859225003043) [S] | fasted vs fed RT | reportedly no difference in FFM, fat mass, hypertrophy, strength. Unverified |
| Schoenfeld 2014 [V]; Hackett & Hagstrom 2017 (5 studies, 96 people) [S] | body composition | no advantage for fasted |
| Aird 2018, Scand J Med Sci Sports (46 studies) [S] | performance | eating before helps prolonged (≳60 min) aerobic performance; little effect on short sessions |
| Edinburgh/Gonzalez 2019, JCEM (Bath) [S] | 6 wk, overweight men | fasted group had better insulin sensitivity; same weight loss |
| Slater 2024 (MMU) [S] | appetite | morning fasted exercise usually doesn't raise later intake |
| NCT07487090 (ongoing, 16 wk) | fasted vs fed aerobic | hypothesis: fasted causes more energy compensation. Check results later |

## Pros / cons / who should avoid
- **Pros:** possibly better insulin sensitivity (overweight men); convenience and gut comfort; usually no compensatory eating.
- **Cons:** worse long/hard session performance; lifting quality may drop on a given day (dizzy, weak, fewer reps);
  dizziness or hypoglycaemia symptoms, worse in a deficit, after poor sleep or during caffeine withdrawal;
  slightly higher protein breakdown in theory (irrelevant if daily protein is adequate).
- **Avoid / ask a doctor:** diabetes on insulin or sulfonylureas; history of fainting, hypotension or hypoglycaemia;
  pregnancy; eating-disorder history; long endurance or HIIT where performance matters; adolescents.
- **Hep B:** no studies found either way. Controlled Hep B is not a contraindication; mention it to the doctor if convenient.

## How it applies to Darren
His ~30 min moderate morning treadmill is in the "doesn't matter" zone. Choose whichever feels better. Lifting
quality matters more than fuel source during the cut, so a half scoop of whey or some oats before training is fine.
It counts toward the daily total, so it isn't "wasted". Don't train fasted on dizzy days.
