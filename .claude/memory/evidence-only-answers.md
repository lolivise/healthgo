**Every claim in an answer must rest on research I have actually checked.** Darren, 2026-10-27: "anything you answer,
it needs to be backed by a scientific research. It can't just be something like common sense or inside your model."
He wants to know where each statement comes from, how the experiment was done and how the number was obtained.

**Why:** he acts on these answers for years. "If any misleading information come in and I just follow it ... for like
2-3 years, even a decade ... I'm damaging my body." Time lost can't be claimed back and recovery gets harder with age.
Building correct longevity knowledge is the point of the project ([[research-purpose]], [[ask-myth-buster]]).

**How to apply:**
- Each factual statement carries its source: author, year, journal/PMID, design (RCT, meta-analysis, cohort, model),
  n and population, how it was measured (DXA, doubly labelled water, calorimetry…), the effect size.
- **Verify before citing.** A PMID or number recalled from training is not evidence until checked against the
  abstract/full text this session (or an `evidence-*` memory that recorded it as verified). Unverified → mark it so
  or leave it out.
- Say how strong it is: RCT vs cohort (association only), animal/cell, tiny n, surrogate markers, population unlike
  him (older, obese, women only), industry funding.
- No evidence or conflicting evidence → say so plainly ("❓ 證據不足") rather than filling the gap with plausible
  reasoning. Reasoning that links findings to his plan is labelled as inference, not fact.
- Applies to /review verdicts, /ask, /knowledge-review and every ad-hoc question; bounded by [[safety-limits]].
