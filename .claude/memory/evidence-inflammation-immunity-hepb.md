## Chronic low-grade inflammation
- "Inflammaging" is mostly an industrialised-lifestyle product: absent in Tsimane / Orang Asli (Franck, *Nature Aging*
  2025 [V]). At 36, age adds little; fat, activity, sleep, diet and alcohol drive it. ACC 2025 statement puts
  inflammation beside LDL as a CV risk factor [V via TCTMD].
- **Measure: hs-CRP only** (GP test). Bands <1 / 1–3 / >3 mg/L, ≥10 = acute, redo [U: AHA/CDC 2003]. Within-person CV
  ≈44 % (2024 meta-analysis [V]) → **2 draws ~2 wk apart**, ≥2 wk after any cold, 48–72 h after hard training.
  Ferritin is useless as an inflammation marker for him (liver disease raises it); CRP is made in the liver, read it
  with ALT. **Garmin HRV/RHR are not validated inflammation proxies** (RMSSD showed no link [V]): a low HRV means look at
  sleep, load, illness.
- **What lowers it, by strength:**
  1. Weight loss: ≈ −0.13 mg/L CRP per kg (Selvin 2007, weak r = 0.30 for lifestyle); STEP trials −39–48 %.
     His −14 kg is already his biggest lever.
  2. Moderate CR itself: CALERIE 2 (~12 % CR, 2 y) lowered CRP and TNF-α independent of weight [V]. Only extreme
     deficits (50 % of needs) raise cortisol/immune stress [V]. His deficit is not in that zone.
  3. Exercise: 38 RCTs ES −0.18 on CRP [V]; RT ~3×/wk ≥6 wk lowers CRP. No evidence his volume raises it.
  4. Sleep: CRP/IL-6 rise after several nights at ~4.3 h (Ballesio 2025 [V]); one short night no effect. Poor quality
     links more than 6–7 h duration. Sleep matters more for immunity than for CRP.
  5. Diet: Mediterranean (33 RCTs) lowers hs-CRP and IL-6; olive-oil-rich versions most (CRP −0.51, IL-6 −0.74 SMD)
     [V]. Red meat +0.23 mg/L only in diseased groups. UPF, coffee: observational. **Linoleic acid does not raise CRP**
     (RCTs) so the walnuts are fine. Fish oil plateaus at ~1.2 g EPA+DHA (he is there; no effect in overweight groups).
     Plant diversity ("30/wk") and fermented foods: weak / not replicated (2025 preprint).
  6. Mindfulness: effect mainly in stressed people, floor effect in healthy ones.
  7. Alcohol: no protective dose (Zhao 2023, 107 cohorts [V]); a binge raises endotoxin.
- His diet already matches most of it. Gaps: olive oil only ~4 g, little fruit, no legumes/fish (optional, weak).

## Immunity / viral infection
- **Sleep is the strongest lever:** <6 h → 4.2× cold risk vs >7 h (Prather 2015, landmark); objectively short sleep
  weakens vaccine antibodies, **strongly in men** (ES 0.93; incl. hep A/B vaccines; Spiegel 2023 [V]). Garmin sleep is
  exactly the objective measure. His weekly average: 5.8–6.6 h ISO W38–W42, 7.15 h W43 (first week ≥7 h).
- Moderate deficit does not impair immunity (CALERIE 2: thymus function improved [V summary]); harm = deep/long
  deficit + heavy load + low carb (IOC REDs 2023).
- Regular exercise: COVID hospitalisation RR 0.58, plateau ~150–300 min/wk (Ezzatvar 2022). The "open window" after one
  hard session is contested since 2018. Real risk = stacked stressors (long-haul flight, crowds, broken sleep → the
  Taiwan trip).

## Hepatitis B
- **Flare triggers with evidence:** stopping nucleos(t)ide antivirals (tenofovir stop → 70 % virological relapse in
  12 wk; severe flare 1.2 %, 3.6 % with cirrhosis; EASL 2025 [V]) and immunosuppression (steroids, rituximab). No
  evidence colds, exercise or a deficit trigger flares. ✅ **He is on entecavir** (told 2026-10-25, details in [[health-conditions]]), so never stopping it is his #1 flare rule.
- **Normal ALT for men = ≤30 U/L** (EASL 2025, WHO 2024), not the lab's 40. A rise during the cut can be the transient
  diet bump or a flare; only HBV DNA tells them apart.
- Monitoring (EASL 2025): inactive pattern → ALT + HBV DNA every 12 months, fibrosis check every 2–3 y; higher DNA → 6-monthly.
  HCC surveillance (MJA 2025, Australia): Asian men with CHB **consider 6-monthly ultrasound ± AFP from 40**, earlier
  with family history. He turns 40 in ~4 years.
- Metabolic dysfunction (obesity, diabetes, BP, **weight gain**) worsens CHB mortality dose-dependently (EASL 2025;
  MASLD + CHB HCC 4.8 vs 1.2 % at ~5 y, HR ≈4). Simple steatosis alone is not the problem. → **The cut's value for his
  liver depends on not regaining after the trip.**
- **Alcohol: no safe level for CHB.** +12 g/day → cirrhosis +6.2 %, HCC +11.5 % (Wu 2024, 45 studies [V]); ACG says
  avoid. ALDH2 flushers carry extra HCC risk. Australia's ≤10 drinks/wk is for healthy adults, not him.
- Coffee: linked to less fibrosis/HCC (EASL 2025, not RCT). Exercise 1,000–1,500 MET-min/wk ↔ lower HCC on
  antivirals (Korean cohort, mixed). Aflatoxin: discard mouldy nuts/grains.
- Vaccines (Australian Handbook): **hep A for any chronic liver disease if not immune**; chronic liver disease is a
  pneumococcal risk condition (GP decides for a non-cirrhotic carrier). Flu/COVID wording not re-checked [U].
- Transmission: shared food/utensils fine; no shared razors/toothbrushes; non-immune household/sexual contacts vaccinated.
- Supplements, "not for you": turmeric/curcumin (TGA 2023 liver-injury warning; avoid with liver disease),
  ashwagandha (3/5 with chronic liver disease died of ACLF, 2023 series), green tea extract ≥800 mg EGCG, Taiwanese
  herbal/"liver tonic" products. Korean ginseng in his current multi: **no liver-safety evidence found either way** →
  ask the hepatologist ([[supplements]]).
