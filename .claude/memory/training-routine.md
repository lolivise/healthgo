  About 100–110 working sets/week.

  posture and muscle feeling over load: lighter weights, slower tempo. The row dropping from 60×10 to 30×12

**Training history (told 2026-10-12):** lifting 3–4 years but on/off: blocks of 3–4 months at 6 days/wk, then 1–2
months off or only 1–2 days/wk. The 2026-07 cut is his first long consistent block. Treat him as **late novice /
early intermediate** (effective training age ~1.5–2 yr; bench e1RM ~0.75× bodyweight). He still has fast-gain
potential, and his lifts rising during the cut (pulldown 67 → 78 e1RM) fit that. **Consistency is his biggest lever.**

**Why he used to stop (told 2026-10-12):** overtraining, **36 sets/day × 6 days (~216/wk)** (he first said 12, corrected), no clear goal and no
plan, chronic tiredness, and **all-or-nothing breaks**: once he rests, the break runs 1–2 months. Current volume is
about half that (~108–112 logged sets/wk, ~22/session, W37–W40, may include warm-ups) plus ~220–270 treadmill
min/wk, in a deficit, on ~6.2 h sleep. Markers are fine so far; the guard is against creeping back up.
**He keeps the current split as is (2026-10-12: "the training plan I have is doable")**: don't propose a rewrite;

**How to apply:** a load drop with good recovery is **intentional**, not muscle loss. Don't call it "strength
dropping". There is no InBody any more ([[data-sources]]), so the muscle signal is strength: use e1RM
only for lifts whose load he hasn't deliberately reduced, and use reps at the same load. Otherwise use
the reference levels above as the baseline for "is strength dropping". Training advice stays as
targeted tweaks to this split ([[review-preferences]]); watch shoulder loads ([[health-conditions]]).
