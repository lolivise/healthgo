**Answer:** both sayings are half right. Muscle is cheap at rest and expensive at work.

- **Tissue rates at rest** (Elia 1992, validated by MRI + calorimetry, Wang 2012 PMID 21836642), kcal/kg/day:
  muscle 13, fat 4.5, liver 200, brain 240, heart/kidney 440. +1 kg muscle ≈ +13 kcal/day. The gym claim
  "1 lb muscle burns 50 kcal" is **hype** (≈ 9× too high).
- **Lifting raises RMR a bit more than the tissue math:** meta-analysis RT +96 kcal/day vs controls (95 % CI 45–147;
  high risk of bias; aerobic n.s.) — MacKenzie-Shalders 2020, PMID 32397898. 9 months RT: RMR +73 kcal/day (~5 %),
  DXA tissue model predicted +52, wide individual spread, thyroxine part of it — Aristizabal 2015, PMID 25293431.
- **Why exercise raises energy use:** (1) the work itself, by far the biggest; (2) EPOC, small, prolonged (3–24 h)
  only after ≥50 min at ≥70 % VO2max or hard intervals — LaForgia 2006, PMID 17101527; (3) after hard lifting, REE stays
  up 24–72 h (repair, protein turnover) — Hackney 2008, PMID 18714225, n=16.
- **But TEE is constrained:** ~28 % of extra activity kcal is offset by lower BEE (n=1,754 DLW; more in higher body
  fat) — Careau 2021, PMID 34453886; TEE plateaus at high activity — Pontzer 2016, PMID 26832439. **Age:** TEE adjusted for fat-free mass is flat 20–60 y, −0.7 %/y after 60 (DLW, ~6,400 people, 29 countries, cross-sectional — Pontzer 2021, PMID 34385400); mid-life "slow metabolism" is lost lean mass and activity, not age.
- **Muscle's real payoffs:** keeps RMR from falling during a cut, more work capacity, glucose disposal (muscle takes
  most insulin-stimulated glucose — DeFronzo 1981, PMID 7030826), strength with age.

- **Repair cost fades with training (10-26 follow-up):** muscle damage and the protein-synthesis spike are largest in
  week 1, lower by week 3, minimal by week 10; hypertrophy tracks MyoPS only after damage fades — Damas 2016, PMID 27219125.
  Trained muscle: synthesis spike back to rest by 28 h vs still up in untrained — Tang 2008, PMID 18032468 (repeated bout
  effect). So the "repair burns kcal" part shrinks to near the floor; the work itself and the muscle kept do not.
- **Why build muscle anyway:** strength training −10–17 % all-cause mortality, CVD, cancer, diabetes, peak at
  ~30–60 min/week (cohorts) — Momma 2022, PMID 35228201; grip strength predicts death better than blood pressure — Leong
  2015 PURE, PMID 25982160.

**For him:** lean ≈68 kg; the +3–7 kg goal ([[cutting-goal-2026]]) ≈ +40–90 kcal/day at rest (+ maybe a little from
training) — about half a 100 g rice pack, not a diet lever. Lift to keep and build muscle ([[performance-first]]), never
eat back "muscle kcal". TDEE from the weight trend already includes all of this. Compensation means Garmin activity kcal
overstate the net burn, which makes the EA check in [[evidence-rapid-loss-harms]] conservative (safe side). No change.

**His stated reason for training (10-26):** to raise daily burn so eating out is not a burden. That is served by the
**sessions themselves** (Garmin, last 4 wk: lifting ~305 kcal / 47 min, treadmill ~356 kcal / 38 min, ~616 kcal per
active day, before ~28 % compensation), not by muscle "afterburn". Asked whether a trained lifter can train fewer days:
yes for **maintenance** (~⅓ volume, ≥1–2 sessions/wk, see [[evidence-hypertrophy-training]]), but not now: in a
deficit and still +3–7 kg short of the goal. Decided: keep the 5-day split through the cut; revisit days only once at
maintenance with no lean-mass goal pending.

**"Exercise is only ~5 % of TEE" (asked 10-27, re-verified):** **no primary paper found that states 5 %** (not
verified; the earlier "BMR 60–70 % + TEF 10 %" split was model memory, also unverified). Measured: DLW PAL (TEE ÷ REE)
averages 1.7–1.8 in adults, range ~1.1–2.5 (Westerterp 2013, PMID 23637685), so all activity ≈ 30–45 % of TEE;
spontaneous activity varies 100–800 kcal/d between people (chamber, n=177, Ravussin 1986, PMID 3782471). Wang 2012
tissue rates = n=80 young women, MRI organ mass + calorimetry, regression-fitted. His case: Garmin 09-28→10-24 avg TEE 2,925, BMR 2,243, active 682;
logged sessions 586/day ≈ 20 % gross (≈ 15 % net of the BMR inside them, ≈ 11 % after ~28 % compensation). No conflict
with the muscle point: muscle *at rest* is cheap; the kcal come from *doing the work*. No change.
