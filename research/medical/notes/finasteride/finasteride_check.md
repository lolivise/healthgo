# Finasteride 1.25 mg/day (restart 2026-11-19): evidence check

Compiled 2026-11-19. Research notes only, no advice. Every item was read from the source text in this session (PubMed abstract via E-utilities, or the DailyMed label text). Anything not read is marked UNVERIFIED.

## 1. Efficacy in male androgenetic alopecia

| Source | Design / n / duration | Outcome and measurement | Number |

| Kaufman 1998, [PMID 9777765](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9777765/) | Two RCTs, finasteride 1 mg vs placebo, n=1553 men 18-41 y, 1 y; 1215 continued to year 2 | Hair count in a 1-inch (5.1 cm2) circle of balding vertex, baseline 876 hairs; plus patient, investigator, expert photo panel | +107 hairs vs placebo at 1 y, +138 at 2 y (P<.001); placebo lost hair progressively |

| Kaufman 2008, [PMID 18573712](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18573712/) | Photo data, 1553 men, up to 5 y | Global photographic assessment | 93% lower 5-y likelihood of further visible hair loss vs placebo (95% CI 89-97%) |
| Mella 2010, [PMID 20956649](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20956649/) | Systematic review, 12 RCTs, 3927 men (finasteride vs placebo) | Self-assessment, hair count, investigator, photos | Patient-perceived improvement RR 1.81 short term (<=12 mo), RR 1.71 long term (>=24 mo); NNT 5.6 and 3.4. Hair count mean difference vs placebo 9.42% (short) and 24.3% (long). Evidence graded moderate quality |

Cochrane review of finasteride for male AGA: not found in PubMed search (the Cochrane item found was for female pattern hair loss, PMID 27225981, not relevant). UNVERIFIED that none exists.

Time to effect and when to judge (label wording, PROPECIA section 2): "In general, daily use for three months or more is necessary before benefit is observed. Continued use is recommended to sustain benefit, which should be re-evaluated periodically." Self-assessment benefit vs placebo seen "as early as 3 months (p<0.05)"; investigator assessment as early as 3 months (p<0.001); hair-count significance at 6 and 12 months. Maximum hair count vs baseline reached in the first 2 years.

## 2. Dose: is about 1.25 mg equivalent to 1 mg?

| Source | Design | Number |
|---|---|---|
| Drake 1999, [PMID 10495374](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10495374/) | RCT, n=249 men with AGA, scalp biopsies before/after 42 days of 0.01, 0.05, 0.2, 1, 5 mg/day or placebo. Outcome: scalp skin and serum DHT | Scalp DHT fell 13.0% placebo; 14.9%, 61.6%, 56.5%, 64.1%, 69.4% at 0.01, 0.05, 0.2, 1, 5 mg. Serum DHT fell 49.5%, 68.6%, 71.4%, 72.2% at 0.05, 0.2, 1, 5 mg. Authors: "doses as low as 0.2 mg per day maximally decreased both scalp skin and serum DHT" |
| Roberts 1999, [PMID 10495375](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10495375/) | Two RCTs, men 18-36 y, moderate vertex loss, 5, 1, 0.2, 0.01 mg/day or placebo; hair counts, self-assessment, investigator, photos | Efficacy for all endpoints at 0.2 mg or higher; 1 and 5 mg "similar efficacy that was superior to lower doses"; 0.01 mg similar to placebo. Abstract gives no hair-count numbers or n (UNVERIFIED: n and hair-count values) |

Reading of the data: 1.25 mg sits between the tested 1 mg and 5 mg arms, both on the plateau of the dose-response for hair and DHT. No trial tested 1.25 mg specifically. Dose actually delivered by a quartered 5 mg tablet (uniformity of split film-coated tablets): UNVERIFIED, no PubMed hit for finasteride tablet splitting; no manufacturer data found. Taiwan generic (列腺威) label / TFDA text: UNVERIFIED, not read.

## 3. Telogen effluvium, and an early shedding phase

- Finasteride for telogen effluvium (non-androgenetic shedding): no trial or case series found in PubMed (searches "finasteride telogen" returned nothing relevant). UNVERIFIED / no evidence found. The label indication is male pattern hair loss only (section 1).
- Daunton 2023, [PMID 37052778](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37052778/): systematic review of chronic telogen effluvium (18 studies, 1628 cases, 97.5% female); says many cases labelled CTE are likely early female pattern hair loss or incipient secondary TE. Not about finasteride; no treatment efficacy data. Background only.
- Initial shedding phase on finasteride: neither the PROPECIA label nor the abstracts read mention an early transient shed. Label data show placebo-to-finasteride switchers had a hair-count decrease during the placebo year, then increases (section 11). Whether a dermatology source documents an initial shed: UNVERIFIED.

## 4. Liver

| Item | Source | Finding |
|---|---|---|
| Metabolism | PROPECIA label 12.3 | "extensively metabolized in the liver, primarily via the cytochrome P450 3A4 enzyme subfamily"; two metabolites with no more than 20% of 5-alpha-reductase inhibitory activity. Excretion: 39% urine as metabolites, 57% feces; half-life 5-6 h (18-60 y) |
| Hepatic impairment | PROPECIA 8.6; PROSCAR 8.6 and 12.3 | "Caution should be exercised in the administration of PROPECIA in those patients with liver function abnormalities, as finasteride is metabolized extensively in the liver." PROSCAR: "The effect of hepatic impairment on finasteride pharmacokinetics has not been studied." No dose adjustment figure given |
| CYP effects | PROPECIA 7.1 | "Finasteride does not appear to affect the cytochrome P450-linked drug-metabolizing enzyme system" (antipyrine, digoxin, propranolol, theophylline, warfarin tested). No interactions of clinical importance identified |
| LiverTox | NBK548319 | Direct page blocked by NCBI reCAPTCHA. A web-search summary reports likelihood category E (unlikely to cause clinically apparent liver injury), aminotransferase elevations no higher than placebo, no clinically apparent cases. UNVERIFIED (not read at source) |
| Chronic hepatitis B, entecavir | PubMed search "finasteride AND hepatitis B/HBV/entecavir" | No relevant study. UNVERIFIED / no data |

Entecavir interaction (BARACLUDE label): "Entecavir is not a substrate, inhibitor, or inducer of the cytochrome P450 (CYP450) enzyme system." Elimination: "predominantly eliminated by the kidney", urinary recovery of unchanged drug 62-73%, glomerular filtration plus net tubular secretion. Section 7: coadministration with drugs that "reduce renal function or compete for active tubular secretion" may raise concentrations; effects with other renally eliminated drugs not evaluated. Finasteride is hepatically (CYP3A4) cleared, so no shared pathway is evident on the labels; no finasteride-entecavir study exists in what was searched. Also from the entecavir label: severe acute exacerbations of hepatitis B have been reported on discontinuing anti-HBV therapy; empty stomach dosing (at least 2 h after, 2 h before a meal); no dose adjustment for hepatic impairment (entecavir PK).

## 5. Adverse effects vs placebo

| Source | Design | Number |
|---|---|---|
| PROPECIA label 6.1 | Three 12-month RCTs, finasteride 1 mg n=945 vs placebo n=934 | Decreased libido 1.8% vs 1.3%; erectile dysfunction 1.3% vs 0.7%; ejaculation disorder 1.2% vs 0.7%. Any of these 3.8% (36/945) vs 2.1% (20/934), p=0.04. Discontinuation for drug-related sexual AEs 1.2% vs 0.9%. Resolution on stopping, and in most who continued; each fell to <=0.3% by year 5. Questionnaire (vertex trials), month 12: significantly worse than placebo in 3 of 4 domains (sexual interest, erections, perception of sexual problems), no difference in overall satisfaction |
| Mella 2010, PMID 20956649 | Meta-analysis | Erectile dysfunction RR 2.22 (1.03-4.78), NNH 82.1; any sexual disturbance RR 1.39 (0.99-1.95); stopping due to sexual AEs RR 0.88 (0.51-1.49). Moderate quality |
| Złotowska 2026, [PMID 42396141](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42396141/) | Systematic review, 41 studies | In placebo-controlled RCTs of oral finasteride 1 mg, sexual AEs 1.9-6.7% treated vs 0.9-3.9% placebo, mostly mild and reversible |

| EMA PRAC 5-8 May 2025 ([highlights](https://www.ema.europa.eu/en/news/meeting-highlights-pharmacovigilance-risk-assessment-committee-prac-5-8-may-2025)) | Regulatory review | "PRAC has confirmed suicidal ideation (suicidal thoughts) as a side effect of finasteride 1 and 5 mg tablets"; benefit-risk still favorable; frequency unknown; patients with mood changes to seek advice and, on 1 mg, stop; patient card in 1 mg packs |

Persistent post-finasteride symptoms, quality of evidence:
- Jędrzejczyk 2026, [PMID 42074750](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42074750/): cross-sectional, n=129 symptomatic ex-users, no control group; greater cumulative exposure and older age tied to worse scores. Cannot estimate incidence.
- Till 2026, [PMID 42628582](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42628582/): PCPT (finasteride 5 mg vs placebo) linked to Medicare, 14,239 men, 267,983 person-years, 22 PFS-type conditions; none significantly associated with arm. Population older men on 5 mg, claims-based outcomes.
- Gupta 2026, [PMID 41524216](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41524216/): FAERS disproportionality, reporting rose after 2012; authors attribute to nocebo. Signal-detection only.
- Overall: persistent symptoms rest on case series, surveys and spontaneous reports; no RCT is designed to detect them. The causal question is unsettled; the label carries the postmarketing warning. Bhambhvani 2026, [PMID 42830721](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42830721/) (propensity-matched cohort, low-dose finasteride, young men): abstract text was not retrievable, results UNVERIFIED.

## 6. PSA

PROPECIA label 5.2: in men 18-41 y, mean serum PSA fell from 0.7 ng/mL at baseline to 0.5 ng/mL at month 12. PROSCAR 5 mg in older men with BPH: PSA decreased by about 50%. "Any confirmed increase from the lowest PSA value while on PROPECIA may signal the presence of prostate cancer and should be evaluated, even if PSA levels are still within the normal range for men not taking a 5α-reductase inhibitor." Non-compliance can also affect PSA results. Label 5.3: in the 7-year PCPT (5 mg, men 55+, n=18,882) Gleason 8-10 cancer 1.8% finasteride vs 1.1% placebo. The label's "doubling" rule of thumb for 5 mg is not stated for the 1 mg product; the numeric adjustment factor for 1.25 mg: UNVERIFIED.

## 7. Handling and pregnancy (exact wording, PROPECIA label 5.1)

"Women should not handle crushed or broken PROPECIA tablets when they are pregnant or may potentially be pregnant because of the possibility of absorption of finasteride and the subsequent potential risk to a male fetus. PROPECIA tablets are coated and will prevent contact with the active ingredient during normal handling, provided that the tablets have not been broken or crushed."

Section 8.1: "Women could be exposed to finasteride through contact with crushed or broken PROPECIA tablets or semen from a male partner taking PROPECIA." "If a pregnant woman comes in contact with crushed or broken PROPECIA tablets, the contact area should be washed immediately with soap and water." Semen study (12.3): 35 men on 1 mg/day for 6 weeks, finasteride undetectable (<0.2 ng/mL) in 60% of samples, mean 0.26 ng/mL, highest 1.52 ng/mL; worst-case vaginal exposure 7.6 ng/day, "650-fold less than the dose of finasteride (5 µg) that had no effect on circulating DHT levels in men." PROSCAR 5 mg label carries the same crushed/broken warning ("Females should not handle crushed or broken PROSCAR tablets when they are pregnant or may potentially be pregnant"). Semen exposure at 5 mg-derived dose: UNVERIFIED (study was at 1 mg).

## 8. Muscle and strength

No RCT of finasteride (or any 5-ARI) on resistance-training gains in healthy men was found (searches in PubMed returned none). UNVERIFIED / not found. Indirect, testosterone-supplementation trials only:
- Bhasin 2012, [PMID 22396515](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22396515/): RCT, 139 healthy men 18-50 y (102 completed), testosterone enanthate 50/125/300/600 mg/wk plus dutasteride 2.5 mg/d (type 1 and 2 inhibitor) vs placebo, 20 weeks. Fat-free mass gain (primary): e.g. 5.8 vs 5.7 kg at 300 mg/wk; difference between dutasteride and placebo groups not significant (P=.18); muscle strength, fat mass, hematocrit did not differ. Men were not training and testosterone was supraphysiologic or suppressed baseline.
- Borst 2014, [PMID 24326421](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24326421/): RCT, 60 hypogonadal men aged 60+, testosterone enanthate 125 mg/wk plus finasteride 5 mg/day vs placebo, 52 weeks, 2x2. Finasteride did not alter testosterone-induced gains in strength (8-14%), fat-free mass (+4.04 kg) or BMD; it prevented prostate volume growth.
These test DHT's role in testosterone-driven gains in other populations, not finasteride's effect on trained men with normal testosterone.

## 9. Interactions: creatine, ginseng, calorie deficit

- Creatine and DHT: van der Merwe 2009, [PMID 19741313](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19741313/): double-blind placebo crossover, n=20 college rugby players, 25 g/d x7 d then 5 g/d x14 d. Testosterone unchanged; DHT +56% at day 7, +40% at day 21; DHT:T ratio +36% then +22%. Small, one study, not replicated in anything found here; hair outcomes not measured. The 2010 letter (Green, [PMID 20445368](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20445368/)) is a comment, no data. Whether DHT rise from creatine matters for hair or interacts with finasteride: UNVERIFIED.
- Ginseng and finasteride, or ginseng and 5-alpha-reductase in men: nothing in humans found; only a preclinical title (Murata 2012, PMID 21538628, ginseng rhizome and ginsenoside Ro on 5-alpha-reductase and hair regrowth, animal/in vitro model) whose abstract was not read. UNVERIFIED.
- Finasteride label interaction section 7.2 lists many co-used drug classes without significant interactions; supplements not addressed.
- Weight loss / calorie deficit and finasteride, or deficit effect on DHT in men: no relevant study retrieved. UNVERIFIED.

## 10. Discontinuation: what happens after stopping

- PROPECIA label section 2: "Withdrawal of treatment leads to reversal of effect within 12 months."
- Label section 14 (from the Kaufman/Finasteride Study Group trials): "A change of treatment from PROPECIA to placebo (n=48) at the end of the initial 12 months resulted in reversal of the increase in hair count 12 months later, at 24 months." (Hair counts by photographic enlargement; figure not retrievable as text, so intermediate time points like 6 months are UNVERIFIED.)
- Biology: PROSCAR label 12.1: in healthy men treated 14 days, stopping returned DHT to pretreatment levels in about 2 weeks. So the drug effect on DHT is gone within weeks; the hair reversal lags because of the hair cycle.
- Untreated trajectory (placebo arms, Kaufman 2008, PMID 18573713): -239 hairs (26.3%) over 5 years; 75% worse by photos. Without drug, progression resumes at this approximate rate.
- Could stopping about 6 months ago explain thinning noticed now? The label timeline (reversal complete within 12 months of withdrawal) makes loss of finasteride-derived hair a documented possibility at 6+ months, as does continued untreated progression. The trials do not give a number at 6 months off drug. Telogen effluvium after weight loss (Jul-Sep 2026) is not tested against this in any source read. Both remain hypotheses; evidence cannot separate them.

## 11. Restarting after a break

- Controlled data on restart after a break: none found. UNVERIFIED.
- Closest data (label section 14, same trials): 65 men got finasteride for 12 months then placebo for the first extension year; some were switched back to finasteride, 32 entering year 5. The label gives no hair-count result for those switch-back men. A different group, placebo for 12 months then PROPECIA (n=425 switched), had a hair-count decrease during placebo, then an increase after 1 year of treatment: 56 hairs above original baseline, versus 91 above baseline in men treated from the start. "A higher absolute hair count was achieved in patients who were started on treatment with PROPECIA in the initial study. This advantage was maintained through the remaining 3 years." So delayed starts regained a smaller improvement (relative to original baseline), although the gain relative to the count at therapy start was "comparable" between the groups.
- Whether gains from the first course return fully on restart: UNVERIFIED (no source).
