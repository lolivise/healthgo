# Chronic low-grade inflammation (inflammaging / meta-inflammation): mechanisms, markers, drivers

Context: male, 36, 177 cm, ~88 kg (from 102 kg since 2026-07-15, losing ~0.75–1 %/week), ~22 % body fat, strength training 5 d/wk plus daily treadmill, sleep often ~6–7 h, chronic hepatitis B (controlled, normal bloodwork), Garmin wearer.

Verification legend: items with a link were found online in this session (abstract, PubMed/PMC page or search-result snippet). Items marked **[not verified this session]** come from well-known literature but were not opened. Treat them as leads to check, not as confirmed.

## 1. Definition and biology: what inflammaging / meta-inflammation is, and does it matter at 36?

### Takeaway
"Inflammaging" is a chronic, low-grade, sterile rise in inflammatory signalling (IL-6, TNF-α, CRP and others) that tracks age in industrialized populations. A 2025 Nature Aging study suggests it is largely a product of industrialized lifestyle (adiposity, inactivity, diet), not an inevitable result of age. At 36, age-driven inflammaging is minor. The modifiable lifestyle and adiposity component (meta-inflammation) is what matters, and it can be present at any age.

### Cited Findings
- Franck et al., "Nonuniversality of inflammaging across human populations," *Nature Aging* 2025. The authors tested a 19-cytokine inflammaging axis first derived in Italy's InCHIANTI cohort. It replicated in a Singapore aging cohort but not in two non-industrialized groups, the Tsimane (Bolivia) and the Orang Asli (Malaysia). In those groups the axis showed little or no link with age and none with age-related disease. — [IAST summary](https://www.iast.fr/node/104820); [author PDF (UCSB)](https://gurven.anth.ucsb.edu/sites/default/files/sitefiles/papers/Franck%20et%20al.%20Nature%20Aging%202025%20inflammaging.pdf); [TSE record](https://www.tse-fr.eu/articles/nonuniversality-inflammaging-across-human-populations)
  - Conclusion: inflammaging as measured appears to be "largely a product of industrialized lifestyles." In the non-industrialized groups, inflammation tracked infection burden, not age: about 66 % of Tsimane had an intestinal parasite and more than 70 % of Orang Asli had a current infection. — [Technology Networks](https://www.technologynetworks.com/immunology/news/inflammation-tied-to-lifestyle-not-just-aging-401685); [IAST](https://www.iast.fr/node/104820)
  - Caveat: this is a short Letter reanalysing existing cytokine data. Sources disagree on the citation date (June vs August 2025; one lists vol. 5, pp. 1471–1480). — [Pharmazeutische Zeitung](https://www.pharmazeutische-zeitung.de/entzuendungen-typisch-beim-altern-aber-nicht-bei-jedem-157099/); [TSE](https://www.tse-fr.eu/articles/nonuniversality-inflammaging-across-human-populations)
- 2025 ACC Scientific Statement (Mensah et al., *JACC*, online 29 Sep 2025, doi 10.1016/j.jacc.2025.08.047): it frames chronic low-grade inflammation as a central, measurable cardiovascular risk factor alongside LDL. — [TCTMD](https://www.tctmd.com/news/acc-its-time-clinicians-address-inflammation-cvd); [ACC ACCEL summary](https://www.acc.org/latest-in-cardiology/articles/2026/05/12/13/22/accel-lite-12may2026)
- Mechanisms (background, **[not verified this session]**):
  - The term "inflammaging" was coined by Franceschi et al., 2000 (Ann N Y Acad Sci).
  - Furman et al., "Chronic inflammation in the etiology of disease across the life span," *Nat Med* 2019 (landmark review), lists the drivers as chronic infection, physical inactivity, visceral obesity, gut dysbiosis/permeability, diet, social isolation, psychological stress, disturbed sleep and xenobiotics.
  - Pathways named across this literature:
    - NLRP3 inflammasome activation by metabolic danger signals (saturated fatty acids, cholesterol crystals, uric acid) drives IL-1β and then IL-6, which induces hepatic CRP.
    - Senescent cells secrete the SASP (senescence-associated secretory phenotype).
    - Adipose-tissue macrophages in expanding visceral fat secrete TNF-α and IL-6. This is "meta-inflammation."
    - Gut-barrier leak (LPS translocation) adds to the load.
  - These are well-established concepts, but no specific 2023–2026 paper on them was opened here.

### Inferences
- At 36, the "aging" component is small. His inflammatory load is mostly set by the lifestyle and metabolic factors the Franck study points to: adiposity (especially visceral), sleep, activity and diet. Those are exactly the ones he is changing.
- The IL-6 to CRP pathway runs through the liver. CRP is made by hepatocytes in response to IL-6, which matters for interpreting CRP in someone with chronic hepatitis B (see section 2).

### Gaps
- No 2023–2026 quantitative data were retrieved on how much IL-6 or CRP rise per decade between the 30s and later life in healthy industrialized adults.
- No 2023–2026 primary source was opened for NLRP3, SASP or gut-permeability mechanisms. These rest on background knowledge.

## 2. Markers: hs-CRP cut-offs, IL-6, NLR, GlycA, ferritin; variability; wearable proxies

### Takeaway
hs-CRP is the only inflammation marker that is practical, cheap, standardized and guideline-endorsed for a routine GP blood test. The 2025 ACC statement now backs near-universal hs-CRP screening. A single value is noisy (within-person CV about 44–46 %), so judge the average of two measurements taken about 2 weeks apart while healthy. Discard and repeat any value ≥10 mg/L. HRV is inversely associated with CRP and IL-6 at the population level, but it is not a validated individual proxy.

### Cited Findings
**Guidelines and cut-offs**
- 2025 ACC Scientific Statement: it calls for universal (or near-universal) hs-CRP screening in primary and secondary prevention, on the reasoning that "clinicians are unlikely to treat what they do not routinely measure."
  - Residual inflammation measured by hs-CRP predicts recurrent events even in statin-treated patients.
  - In primary prevention, hs-CRP can identify people who would benefit from statins despite normal LDL.
  - Colchicine is cited as the first FDA-approved anti-inflammatory for CV event reduction. It is contested: CLEAR SYNERGY (OASIS 9) was neutral after MI with PCI.
  - Sources: [TCTMD](https://www.tctmd.com/news/acc-its-time-clinicians-address-inflammation-cvd); [ACC](https://www.acc.org/latest-in-cardiology/articles/2026/05/12/13/22/accel-lite-12may2026); [TribeMD summary](https://tribemd.com/us/contents/inflammation-as-a-central-target-in-cardiovascular-disease:-2025-acc-scientific-statement). The exact thresholds in the JACC full text were not opened.
- Classic AHA/CDC 2003 categories (Pearson et al., *Circulation* 2003;107:499, landmark and still the commonly used reference): <1 mg/L low, 1–3 mg/L average, >3 mg/L high CV risk; >10 mg/L means look for an acute or other cause and repeat. **[not verified this session]**
- 2018/2019 ACC/AHA prevention guidelines list hs-CRP ≥2.0 mg/L as a "risk-enhancing factor." **[not verified this session]**

**Within-person variability**
- 2024 systematic review and meta-analysis (*PLoS ONE*): for hsCRP the median within-subject CV was 0.44 (range 0.27–0.76) and the pooled ICC 0.62 (95 % CI 0.58–0.67). Many included studies were small or had few repeats. — [PMC11530069](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11530069/); [Birmingham record](https://research.birmingham.ac.uk/en/publications/within-subject-variation-of-c-reactive-protein-and-high-sensitivi/)
- NHANES repeat-exam substudy (n = 541, mean interval 18.9 days): ICC 0.77 (0.69–0.84), Spearman r 0.65, within-person CV 46.2 %. — search snippet only; candidate PMC record [PMC3613132](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3613132) (the exact paper-to-link match was not confirmed)
- Bogaty et al. 2013 (stable CAD and non-CAD subjects): median within-subject SD was 0.07 mg/L within a day, 0.19 within a week, 0.36 between weeks and 0.63 between months. — search snippet; candidate [PMC3620269](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3620269) / [PMC3392358](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3392358) (exact match not confirmed)
- *Clinical Chemistry* 2009: one or two measurements may not establish a person's usual level. Repeat about 2 weeks apart and discard/repeat any value ≥10 mg/L. — [Helmholtz Munich PDF](https://push-zb.helmholtz-munich.de/deliver.php?id=13189)
- Older work (Campbell 2002) found intra-individual variation large enough to misclassify risk category in many patients. — [Macquarie record](https://researchers.mq.edu.au/en/publications/limited-clinical-utility-of-high-sensitivity-plasma-c-reactive-pr/)

**Wearable proxy (HRV, RHR)**
- Williams et al., "Heart rate variability and inflammation: a meta-analysis of human studies," *Brain Behav Immun* 2019 (landmark; no newer equivalent found).
  - 2,283 records screened, 159 eligible, 51 meta-analysed.
  - Overall inverse association: higher HRV goes with lower inflammatory markers. SDNN and HF-HRV gave the most consistent associations.
  - Primary findings were mixed, with some positive associations.
  - — [PubMed 30872091](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30872091/)
- MIDUS (US national sample, n = 836): HF-HRV was inversely associated with IL-6, CRP and fibrinogen, and LF-HRV with IL-6 and CRP. — [MIDUS PDF](https://midus.wisc.edu/findings/pdfs/2343.pdf)
- In stable CHD patients, RMSSD (the vagal index closest to what consumer wearables report overnight) was not significantly associated with any inflammatory measure. — [PMC3207966](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3207966)

**Other markers (background, [not verified this session])**
- IL-6: research-grade, not standardized for routine clinical use, and not on a standard GP panel.
- NLR (neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratio): can be computed for free from a routine full blood count. It is associated with CV and all-cause mortality, but it is non-specific and has no agreed cut-off for low-grade inflammation.
- GlycA: an NMR composite of glycosylated acute-phase proteins. It is more stable than hs-CRP but only available via specialist labs.
- Ferritin: an acute-phase reactant and an iron-store marker, and it is raised in liver disease and metabolic dysfunction-associated fatty liver. That makes it a poor inflammation marker, especially with hepatitis B.

### Inferences
- **Practical recommendation for him:** ask the GP to add hs-CRP to his next routine bloods, with the full blood count giving NLR for free.
  - Draw when free of colds or infections and injuries for at least 2 weeks.
  - Avoid drawing within about 48–72 h of an unusually hard or novel session (hard exercise acutely raises IL-6, then CRP; this is background knowledge, not verified here).
  - If the result is >2–3 mg/L, repeat in about 2 weeks and use the mean. If ≥10, treat it as acute and repeat.
  - Ideally take one reading now, mid-cut, and another after reaching his goal, to show the trend.
- **Hepatitis B:** CRP is synthesized in the liver. In controlled chronic HBV with normal LFTs, hs-CRP should still be interpretable, but any rise should be read alongside ALT and the liver panel and raised with his doctor, not self-interpreted. Ferritin is especially confounded by liver disease.
- **Garmin:** his overnight HRV and RHR are useful as personal recovery and stress trend signals. The evidence only supports a weak, group-level inverse association with CRP and IL-6, and the RMSSD-type metric is the least consistent. A falling HRV trend cannot be read as "inflammation is up." It should prompt a look at sleep, training load and illness.

### Gaps
- No study was found validating consumer-wearable (Garmin) HRV or RHR against hs-CRP or IL-6 in healthy individuals. No 2023–2026 meta-analysis updating Williams 2019 was found.
- The exact hs-CRP thresholds in the 2025 ACC statement (whether ≥2 mg/L is used) were not verified from the full text.
- No source was retrieved quantifying how long after strength training hs-CRP stays elevated in trained men.

## 3. Drivers with effect sizes

### Takeaway
For someone like him, the dominant driver is excess and visceral adiposity, which falls as weight is lost. The landmark slope is about −0.13 mg/L CRP per kg lost, strongest with large losses. Sleep loss raises IL-6 and CRP only with sustained, severe restriction (about 4.3 h for several nights), not at 6–7 h. Regular exercise modestly lowers CRP (SMD about −0.18). Evidence that a moderate deficit itself raises inflammation is weak. Aggressive deficits in lean athletes raise cortisol and immune-cell stress more consistently than IL-6.

### Cited Findings
**Adiposity and weight loss**
- Selvin et al., *Arch Intern Med* 2007 (landmark; still the standard per-kg estimate), 33 weight-loss studies:
  - mean CRP change was −0.13 mg/L per 1 kg lost (weighted r = 0.85) across lifestyle and surgical interventions
  - for lifestyle interventions alone the correlation was much weaker (r = 0.30)
  - the relationship was roughly linear
  - — [Nutrition Evidence summary](https://nutrition-evidence.com/article/96950/the-effect-of-weight-loss-on-c-reactive-protein-a-systematic-review); [Metabolicum summary](https://www.metabolicum.com/learn/research/selvin-2007-weight-loss-crp). The secondary site's dose table (about 1.3–1.5 mg/L per 10 kg) is not verified against the original.
- Semaglutide STEP 1–3 pooled analysis (*EClinicalMedicine*, Jan 2023), week 68:
  - CRP fell vs placebo by about 44 % (STEP 1), 39 % (STEP 2) and 48 % (STEP 3), all p < 0.05
  - reductions tracked body-weight loss and held regardless of baseline BMI or glycaemic status
  - Novo Nordisk-funded, post hoc
  - — [DOAJ record](https://doaj.org/article/60feb89664bd496d9129a6ab9fd90750); [UCL Discovery](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/10167483)
- STEP-HFpEF (*NEJM* 2023): mean CRP change was −43.5 % with semaglutide vs −7.3 % with placebo. A pooled secondary analysis found the CRP fall was similar regardless of the amount of weight lost, suggesting a partly weight-independent drug effect. — [Epocrates summary](https://epocrates.com/online/article/trial-evaluates-semaglutide-in-patients-with-hfpef-and-obesity); [Primary Care Notebook](https://primarycarenotebook.com/pages/cardiovascular-medicine/step-hfpef-trial-semaglutide-in-patients-with-heart-failure-with-preserved-ejection-fraction-hfpef-and-obesity)
- A 2023 meta-analysis: adding exercise to a weight-loss diet did not lower hs-CRP more than diet alone, though TNF-α fell more in trials longer than 16 weeks. — [Ingenta (Crit Rev Food Sci Nutr 2023)](https://www.ingentaconnect.com/content/tandf/bfsn/2023/00000063/00000019/art00049)

**Sleep**
- Ballesio, Fiori & Lombardo, "Effects of experimental sleep deprivation on peripheral inflammation: an updated meta-analysis," *J Sleep Res* 2025 (35 studies, 887 healthy, mostly young adults). Cohen's d:
  - **multiple nights of partial deprivation** (about 4–4.6 h for about 7–9 nights): IL-6 d = 0.42 (0.11–0.73) and CRP d = 0.76 (0.09–1.43), both significant
  - a single night of total or partial deprivation: no significant change in CRP or IL-6
  - TNF-α: no significant effect
  - limitations: 71 % non-randomised, results sensitive to post hoc outlier removal, only morning-after sampling
  - — [PMC12856123](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12856123/) (PMID 40474574, doi 10.1111/jsr.70099)
- Irwin, Olmstead & Carroll, *Biol Psychiatry* 2016 (landmark, 72 studies):
  - sleep disturbance and long sleep were associated with higher CRP and IL-6
  - short sleep was not associated in categorical analyses, though it was linked to higher CRP when analysed as a continuous variable
  - experimental restriction was not associated with CRP, IL-6 or TNF in that earlier pooling
  - — [PMC4666828](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4666828/); [UCLA PDF](https://teams.semel.ucla.edu/sites/default/files/publications/Mirwin%20Sleep%20disturbance%20sleep%20duration....pdf)
- Mendelian-randomization and sleep-trait review: a U/J-shaped relationship of CRP and IL-6 with sleep duration. Genetically, CRP appeared causal for sleep duration rather than the reverse, and IL-6 was associated with long sleep. — [PMC10950822](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10950822) (publication year not confirmed)

**Psychological stress**
- Steptoe et al. 2007 meta-analysis of acute lab stress (landmark): IL-6 r = 0.19 (p = 0.001), IL-1β r = 0.58 (p < 0.001), CRP marginal (r = 0.12, p = 0.088). A later update confirmed reliable rises in IL-6, IL-1β, IL-10 and TNF-α. — [JCI reference listing](https://www.jci.org/references/scholar/34237/B47); [PMC5553449](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5553449)
- Chronic or early-life stress (childhood maltreatment, trauma): a small meta-analysis of 11 studies found higher adult CRP and IL-6. Chronic-stress findings in other samples were mixed or null. — [PMC5553449](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5553449); [Guardino et al. PDF](https://cds.psych.ucla.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/48/2020/10/Guardino-et-al-Chronic-stress-and-c-reactive-protein.pdf)

**Exercise and sedentary time**
- 2023 meta-analysis of 38 RCTs (n = 2,557 healthy adults): long-term exercise training reduced CRP (SMD −0.18). Effects were larger with more than 12 weeks of training and moderate intensity. — [PMC10499556](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10499556/)
- 2024 meta-analysis (*Arch Gerontol Geriatr*, 12 studies, n = 388, middle-aged and older adults): resistance training reduced CRP and IL-6. Effect sizes were not retrieved. — [PDF](https://fisiologiadelejercicio.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/Effect-of-resistance-training-on-inflammatory-markers-in-middle.pdf)
- Overtraining and excessive exercise: no 2023–2026 source was retrieved. See Gaps.

**Diet: ultra-processed food (UPF)**
- Scoping review, *Nutrients* 2025 (24 studies; CRP assessed in 21):
  - adults: 11 of 17 analyses found higher CRP/hs-CRP with more UPF, 5 found no association and 1 found it in women only
  - IL-6, TNF-α and IL-1β were generally not associated
  - mostly observational, no pooled effect size
  - — [PubMed 41010537](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41010537/); [PMC12472508](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12472508/)

**Aggressive dieting and low energy availability (LEA)**
- 2024 *Redox Biology* trial: 12 female endurance athletes ate about 50 % of their energy needs for 14 days. Results:
  - cortisol rose markedly and immune-cell stress increased markedly
  - performance fell and was not restored by 3 days of refeeding
  - IL-6 was not reported in the summaries seen
  - — [University of Copenhagen news](https://news.ku.dk/all_news/2024/07/widespread-practice-among-athletes-harms-both-performance-and-health/); [DOAJ record](https://doaj.org/article/de52a7cd34ca4cdfbeb6e45fa7fb31a6)
- Elite race walkers (*Med Sci Sports Exerc*, about 2021–2022): 6 days of LEA alone did not change the iron or immune markers. Exercise-induced IL-6 and cortisol rose with a low-carbohydrate, high-fat diet instead. — [PubMed 34690285](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34690285/)
- 2021 review: athletes with low energy balance or availability showed lower LH and estradiol but not altered IL-6. — [Dialnet PDF](https://dialnet.unirioja.es/descarga/articulo/8102560.pdf)
- 2026 systematic review of experimental LEA (13 studies, 145 participants) includes inflammation outcomes. Its IL-6 findings were not retrieved. — [PMC12967264](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12967264/)
- *Frontiers in Immunology* 2019: prolonged hard training combined with LEA in normal-weight people may suppress immune function, and the route of weight loss (diet vs exercise) shapes the immune response differently. — [Frontiers PDF](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fimmu.2019.00907/pdf)
- Tomiyama et al., *Psychosom Med* 2010 (landmark): calorie counting and restriction raised cortisol in women. **[not verified this session]**

**Smoking and alcohol**
- No source was retrieved in this session. Both are established drivers of raised CRP (background knowledge); see Gaps.

### Inferences
- **Weight loss:** applying the Selvin slope to his ~14 kg loss suggests roughly 1.8 mg/L lower CRP if he started elevated. Treat this only as a ceiling: the lifestyle-only correlation is weak, and people starting near 1 mg/L cannot fall that far. The biggest anti-inflammatory lever he has is already being pulled.
- **Rate of loss:** ~0.75–1 %/week with high protein and strength training is a moderate deficit, not the about 50 %-of-needs LEA used in athlete studies. No retrieved evidence shows a deficit of his size raising CRP or IL-6. Cortisol and immune stress are the more plausible costs of extreme deficits.
- **Sleep:** 6–7 h is well above the about 4.3 h restriction that raised IL-6 and CRP in the 2025 meta-analysis. Short sleep alone is therefore unlikely to raise his inflammatory markers much. Sleep disturbance (fragmentation, poor quality) has a more consistent observational link in Irwin 2016. Sleep is more likely to matter through appetite, recovery and lean-mass retention than through CRP.
- **Training:** 5 d/wk strength plus daily treadmill is in the range associated with lower CRP. The risk is acute post-exercise elevation confounding a blood test, not chronic inflammation, unless signs of non-functional overreaching appear (falling HRV trend, rising RHR, stalled lifts, poor sleep).

### Gaps
- No 2023–2026 meta-analysis with a per-kg or per-% weight-loss CRP slope for lifestyle interventions was found. Selvin 2007 remains the reference.
- No retrieved study tested whether a moderate deficit at ~0.75–1 %/week (as opposed to athlete LEA) raises cortisol, IL-6 or CRP in men with overweight.
- Overtraining and inflammation, smoking, alcohol (dose-response, J-curve controversy) and sedentary time: no sources were retrieved in this session's budget.
- No chronic-stress meta-analysis with CRP and IL-6 as outcomes was found. Acute-stress evidence is solid but less relevant.

## 4. Does a moderate calorie deficit lower inflammation? (CALERIE 2)

### Takeaway
Yes, in non-obese healthy adults. In CALERIE 2, 2 years of calorie restriction, achieved at only about 12 % below baseline intake (~300 kcal/day), lowered CRP significantly vs control, and the effect held after adjusting for weight loss. TNF-α also fell, as a secondary outcome.

### Cited Findings
- CALERIE 2 design: healthy non-obese adults aged 21–50 (BMI 22–27.9) were randomised 2:1 to 25 % CR or ad libitum diet for 2 years. Achieved CR averaged about 12 % (≈297 kcal/day), with 18 % dropout. — [PubMed 31303390](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31303390/); [BMJ DTB summary](https://dtb.bmj.com/content/58/9/135)
- Kraus et al., *Lancet Diabetes Endocrinol* 2019;7(9):673–683: CR significantly improved CRP at 2 years vs control (p = 0.012), along with the insulin sensitivity index and metabolic-syndrome score. Results were robust after controlling for relative weight loss. — [PubMed 31303390](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31303390/); [NIH news release](https://www.nih.gov/news-events/news-releases/moderate-calorie-restriction-young-middle-aged-adults-significantly-reduces-heart-metabolic-risk-factors-independent-weight-loss)
- CALERIE 2 summary: no change in the primary outcomes (resting metabolic rate, core temperature), but the secondary outcomes T3 and **TNF-α decreased**. — [PMC5366044](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5366044)
- Meydani et al., *Aging (Albany NY)* 2016, "Long-term moderate calorie restriction inhibits inflammation without impairing cell-mediated immunity": 2-year changes in CRP, TNF-α, ICAM-1 and leptin. — [Impact Aging figure](https://static-site-aging-prod.impactaging.com/figure/100994/F3). The magnitudes often quoted (CRP about −47 %, TNF-α about −25 %) are **[not verified this session]**.

### Inferences
- CALERIE participants were already non-obese and cut only about 12 %, yet CRP improved independent of weight loss. A moderate, sustained deficit is anti-inflammatory, not pro-inflammatory. His deficit is larger than CALERIE's and he carries more fat, so if anything the expected direction is a bigger reduction.

### Gaps
- No 2023–2026 CALERIE secondary analyses on CRP or cytokines were retrieved. Spadaro et al., *Science* 2022 on thymic function and PLA2G7 is known but was not opened.

## 5. Health consequences of chronically raised inflammation (brief)

### Takeaway
Persistently raised hs-CRP independently predicts cardiovascular events, even when LDL is controlled. It is linked to insulin resistance, depression, liver disease progression and sarcopenia, though causality outside CVD is less clear.

### Cited Findings
- Residual inflammation (hs-CRP) strongly predicts recurrent CV events in statin-treated patients and identifies primary-prevention candidates with normal LDL. — [TCTMD on the 2025 ACC statement](https://www.tctmd.com/news/acc-its-time-clinicians-address-inflammation-cvd); [Gigazine summary of Ridker data](https://wbgsv0a.gigazine.net/gsc_news/en/20251002-inflammation-and-heart-health)
- Raised CRP and IL-6 are reported in depression and PTSD. — [PMC8490301 table](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8490301/table/T5)
- Background **[not verified this session]**:
  - Furman et al., *Nat Med* 2019, links systemic chronic inflammation to CVD, type 2 diabetes, NAFLD/MASLD, cancer, depression, neurodegeneration and sarcopenia.
  - Ridker et al., *Lancet* 2023, a pooled analysis of 31,245 statin-treated patients, found hs-CRP a stronger predictor of future events than LDL.
  - CANTOS (*NEJM* 2017) showed IL-1β inhibition reduced CV events.

### Inferences
- For a 36-year-old with a falling body weight, active training and controlled HBV, the main relevance is long-term CV and metabolic risk and, given HBV, liver health. A baseline hs-CRP gives an objective answer to "is this a risk for me?" in place of inference.

### Gaps
- Ridker *Lancet* 2023 and the CANTOS data were not opened this session.
- No source was retrieved on whether systemic low-grade inflammation accelerates HBV-related fibrosis in controlled chronic HBV.
