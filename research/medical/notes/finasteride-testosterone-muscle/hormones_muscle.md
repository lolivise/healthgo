# Hormones and muscle: testosterone, DHT, 5-ARI, energy deficit (research notes)

Compiled 2026-11-19. Notes only, no advice. Every item below was read as a PubMed abstract (E-utilities efetch) in this session; PMIDs given. Anything not read is marked UNVERIFIED. Related: ../finasteride/finasteride_check.md (Bhasin 2012 PMID 22396515, Borst 2014 PMID 24326421, label T +10-20 %, DHT -70 %; not redone).

## Q1. Testosterone dose-response for muscle

| Source | Design | Numbers |
|---|---|---|
| Bhasin 1996, PMID 8637535 (NEJM) | RCT, n=43 normal men, 2x2 (T enanthate 600 mg/wk vs placebo; lifting 3x/wk vs none), 10 wk. FFM by underwater weighing, muscle size by MRI, bench-press/squat strength | No exercise: T vs placebo triceps area +424 vs -81 mm2, quadriceps +607 vs -131 mm2, bench +9 vs -1 kg, squat +16 vs +3 kg (all P<0.05). T + exercise: FFM +6.1 +/- 0.6 kg, triceps +501 mm2, quadriceps +1174 mm2, bench +22 kg, squat +38 kg; greater than either no-exercise group. Dose was supraphysiologic (single dose, no dose-response). Abstract gives no placebo+exercise FFM figure (UNVERIFIED) |
| Bhasin 2001, PMID 11701431 (Am J Physiol Endocrinol Metab) | RCT, n=61 eugonadal men 18-35 y, GnRH agonist (suppress endogenous T) + T enanthate 25/50/125/300/600 mg/wk, 20 wk, energy and protein standardized. DXA/body comp, leg-press strength, thigh MRI volumes | Nadir/achieved T 253, 306, 542, 1,345, 2,370 ng/dl. FFM +3.4 (125 mg), +5.2 (300), +7.9 kg (600); FFM change correlated with log T r=0.73. Leg-press strength, leg power, thigh and quadriceps volume rose with T concentration; fat mass fell. Authors: single linear dose-response. FFM changes for the 25 and 50 mg groups not given in the abstract (UNVERIFIED; the abstract says increases were seen at 125+ mg) |

Reading: the dose-response is across a suppressed-to-supraphysiologic range (253 to 2,370 ng/dl); it does not test variation inside the normal range in men with intact axes.

## Q2. Does own resting T within the normal range predict training gains? Do acute hormone spikes matter?

- Morton 2016, PMID 27174923 (J Appl Physiol): 49 resistance-trained men (23 y), 12 wk whole-body RT (two rep ranges), DXA, biopsies, strength. "No significant correlations between the acute postexercise rise in any purported anabolic hormone and the change in strength or hypertrophy." (This abstract addresses post-exercise rises, not resting baseline.)
- Morton 2018, PMID 30356739 (Front Physiol): same cohort, n=49, 12 wk RET. Backward elimination and principal component regression of circulating T, free T, DHEA, DHT, IGF-1, free IGF-1, LH, GH (pre, post, pre-/post-intervention) against change in type 1 CSA, type 2 CSA, lean mass: "No hormone ... was consistently significant". Highest (n=10) vs lowest (n=10) responders: no blood or intramuscular hormone difference. Androgen receptor (AR) content higher at all times in high responders and linearly related to change in LBM (P<0.01), type 1 CSA (P<0.05), type 2 CSA (P<0.01). Best direct answer to "does own T predict gains": no, in trained young men; AR content did.
- West 2012, PMID 22105707 (Eur J Appl Physiol): n=56 young men, 12 wk RT, acute post-exercise GH, free T, IGF-1, cortisol AUC at midpoint vs gains. No correlation of GH, fT or IGF-1 with LBM or leg-press strength; cortisol AUC r=0.29 with LBM change, 0.35 with type II fibre CSA; GH AUC r=0.36/0.28 with fibre area, not lean mass or strength.
- West 2010, PMID 19910330 (J Appl Physiol): n=12 young men, 15 wk, within-subject: one arm trained alone (basal hormones), other arm followed by leg exercise raising GH, IGF-1, T. CSA +12 % vs +10 %, no difference (P=0.25); strength gains no different. Conclusion: acute hormone elevation enhances neither hypertrophy nor strength.
- Mobley 2018, PMID 29621305 (PLoS One): n=67 untrained men, 12 wk RT; clusters by vastus lateralis thickness change (low +0.11, moderate +0.40, high +0.69 cm). Biomarkers explained only ~8-11 % of variance; abstract does not report resting serum T as a predictor (UNVERIFIED whether measured).
- A meta-analysis of baseline resting T vs hypertrophy: none found in my PubMed searches. UNVERIFIED / not found.
- Caveat: these cohorts were young men with presumably normal T; none tested men with T near the low end. Limited generalisation to hypogonadal men (see Q1: below the normal range, gains are T-dependent).

## Q3. DHT in muscle; 5-ARI human data

- Morton 2018 (PMID 30356739) measured human vastus lateralis (immunoblot/immunoassay): 5-alpha-reductase protein and intramuscular DHT and free T were detectable and measured; 5-alpha-reductase increased after RET in high responders (P<0.01) but not low (P=0.32); intramuscular DHT/free T/5-alpha-reductase had no relation to hypertrophy, unlike AR content.
- Yarrow 2012, PMID 21988936 (Med Sci Sports Exerc): review (17 reviews + 58 studies): skeletal muscle expresses androgen-synthesising enzymes including 5-alpha-reductase; can make T and DHT from DHEA; exercise raises expression of some; "exact role ... requires further elucidation".
- Borst 2007, PMID 17488806: orchiectomized rats, T +/- 5-alpha-reductase inhibitor MK-434, 56 d: T's effects on muscle, bone, hemoglobin were not blocked by the inhibitor; prostate effects were. Animal only.
- Bhasin 2012 and Borst 2014 (human, T-replaced/supraphysiologic): see above file; gains not blunted.
- Amory 2007 PMID 17299062: RCT n=99 healthy men 18-55 y (dutasteride 0.5 mg n=33, finasteride 5 mg n=34, placebo n=32), 1 y: serum DHT -94 % (D), -73 % (F); serum T "transiently increased" (magnitude not in abstract, UNVERIFIED). Sperm count -28.6 % / -34.3 % at 26 wk, recovered partially by 52 wk. No muscle outcome.
- Amory 2008 PMID 18423697: same trial: DHT suppression did not affect BMD, bone markers, lipoproteins, hemoglobin; PSA and sexual function fell slightly, returned after stopping. No muscle/strength/lean mass endpoint in the abstract. Authors: circulating DHT has no clinically significant role in bone, hematopoiesis, lipids in normal men.
- Wilborn 2010, PMID 21116018: n=30 resistance-trained men, 500 mg/d "purported aromatase and 5-alpha-reductase inhibitor" supplement vs placebo, 8 wk, 4 d/wk RT. Supplement not a drug-grade 5-ARI; DHT unchanged (no significant change among groups); lean mass and 1RM rose over time in both groups. Not evidence about finasteride.
- AR binding affinity of T vs DHT in muscle (e.g., relative affinity, dissociation rate): not retrieved from a verified source. UNVERIFIED.
- BPH trials reporting body composition/strength with finasteride or dutasteride: PubMed searches ("dutasteride lean body mass BPH", "finasteride skeletal muscle mass strength men") returned nothing relevant. UNVERIFIED / not found.

## Q4. Finasteride or dutasteride in resistance-training men

No trial found in PubMed (searches on 5-ARI/finasteride/dutasteride with resistance training, strength, lean mass, bodybuilders, athletes). Only nearest hit was Wilborn 2010 (supplement, not a drug). UNVERIFIED / no study found.

## Q5. Energy deficit and T in lifters; recovery; surplus

| Source | Design | Number |
|---|---|---|
| Rossow 2013, PMID 23412685 | Case study n=1, drug-free bodybuilder, 6 mo pre and 6 mo post competition | Testosterone 9.22 to 2.27 ng/mL during prep (-75 %), back to 9.91 ng/mL after competition (6 mo recovery window; exact time of return not in abstract). Body fat 14.8 to 4.5 %. Strength fell and did not fully recover by 6 mo |
| Pardue 2017, PMID 28770669 | Case study n=1, drug-free bodybuilder, 8 mo prep + 5 mo recovery | T 623 to 173 ng/dL (M1 to M8), intake 3,860 to 1,724 kcal/d, lost 9.1 kg; BodPod fat 13.4 to 9.6 %, "largely, but not entirely, reversed" by M13 (body fat 14.9 %). RMR 107 to 81 % of predicted. T value at M13 not in abstract (UNVERIFIED) |
| Mäestu 2010, PMID 20300017 | n=14 male bodybuilders, 11-wk energy restriction (n=7) vs control (n=7) | T fell only from wk 11 to wk 5 before the show: 20.3 +/- 6.0 to 18.0 +/- 6.8 nmol/L (about -11 %); IGF-1 and insulin fell significantly. Final (3 days pre) T not given in abstract |
| Kistler 2014, PMID 24901578 | Case study n=1, 26 wk prep, 88.6 to 73.3 kg | Abstract reports no testosterone data (cardiovascular, body composition). Not usable for T |
| Smith 2022, PMID 34613412 (Nutr Rev) | Systematic review, 7 RCTs of calorie restriction (CR) in men | CR raised total T in 3/4 studies in overweight/obese men; lowered total T in 2/3 studies in normal-weight healthy men; SHBG up in 4/4. Effect depends on BMI. Pooled effect size not given in abstract |

- A meta-analysis specifically in resistance-trained men during energy deficit: none found. UNVERIFIED.
- Recovery speed: only the two single-case studies above (months), no controlled data. How quickly T returns at maintenance after a moderate cut: UNVERIFIED.
- Surplus: Sato 2014, PMID 24792219: 20 healthy men overfed +5,200 kJ/day (45 % fat) for 28 d; SHBG and DHT fell (P<0.05); serum T fell more in men with family history of T2DM; muscle 3beta-HSD, 17beta-HSD mRNA down. So an overfeeding study found no T rise (sedentary healthy men, 28 d, high-fat). No RCT of a lean-bulk surplus in lifters found. UNVERIFIED.
- Note: Hulmi/Karila-type physique-athlete cohort studies were not found by my searches; not used.

## Q6. Diet composition and T

- Whittaker 2021, PMID 33741447: meta-analysis of 6 intervention studies, n=206, low-fat vs high-fat diets: total T SMD -0.38 (95 % CI -0.75 to -0.01), free T -0.37 (-0.63 to -0.11), DHT -0.30 (-0.56 to -0.03); LH, SHBG no difference. Authors say more RCTs needed. Fat percentages per study not in abstract (UNVERIFIED).
- Whittaker 2022, PMID 35254136: 27 studies, n=309, low-carbohydrate (<=35 % energy): moderate-protein (<35 %) no consistent effect on resting total T; high-protein (>=35 %) low-carb: resting total T SMD -1.08 (-1.67 to -0.48), about -5.23 nmol/L. Whittaker 2023, PMID 36266956 (commentary): the fall relates to >3.4 g/kg/day; 1.25-3.4 g/kg/day do not decrease T.
- Alcohol: Santi 2024, PMID 37705506: meta-analysis, 21 studies/30 trials, n=10,199: chronic alcohol use total T MD -4.02 (-6.30 to -1.73) (units not stated in abstract, UNVERIFIED), free T MD -0.17, E2 +7.65; seen in healthy chronic consumers, not after acute intake. Moosazadeh 2024, PMID 39867254: 17 observational studies: overall T SMD 0.03 (-0.11 to 0.16), not significant; low and moderate intake groups slightly higher T vs non-drinkers (SMD 0.19 and 0.22). The two meta-analyses disagree; intake thresholds in drinks per day: UNVERIFIED.

## Q7. Does resistance training raise chronic resting T?

Potter 2021, PMID 35134000 (JSCR): meta-analysis, 11 RCTs, n=421 insufficiently active healthy men 19-75 y, aerobic/resistance/combined, median 12 wk: SMD 0.00 (95 % CI -0.20 to 0.20); no effect of mode, age, BMI, or T measure. Population was untrained men, not lifters; a meta-analysis in trained lifters was not found. UNVERIFIED for trained men.

## Q8. Body fat and T

Corona 2013, PMID 23482592 (Eur J Endocrinol): meta-analysis of 24 studies (diet or bariatric surgery) in obese men. Total T rise: low-calorie diet +2.87 nmol/L (95 % CI 1.68-4.07), bariatric surgery +8.73 (6.51-10.95), both P<0.0001; T change greatest with larger weight loss (B=2.50 +/- 0.98, P=0.029), younger age, non-diabetic, greater obesity; estradiol fell, gonadotropins rose. (Units nmol/L assumed from the paper's usual reporting; abstract does not state units, UNVERIFIED.) Applies to men with obesity; Smith 2022 shows the reverse direction in normal-weight men.
