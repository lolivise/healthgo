# Hormones, libido and erections: evidence notes (research only, no advice)

Compiled 2026-11-19. Sources read this session: PubMed abstracts via E-utilities (PMID given), PMC full text for Finkelstein 2013 (PMC4142768) and Leproult 2011 (PMC4445839, PubMed has no abstract), and the DailyMed PROPECIA (Oct 2024) and PROSCAR (Mar 2026) labels. Anything not read is marked UNVERIFIED. Context: man, 36, 88 kg, lifts 5 d/wk, ended a 4-month cut (102 to 88 kg) now at maintenance; restarted finasteride 1.25 mg/d on 2026-11-19. Existing finasteride facts are in `../finasteride/finasteride_check.md` and not repeated.

Unit conversion used: testosterone ng/dL = nmol/L x 28.8 (e.g. 11 nmol/L = about 317 ng/dL); ng/mL x 100 = ng/dL.

## 1. Which hormone drives libido and erections: T, DHT or estradiol?

**Finkelstein 2013, [PMID 24024838](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24024838/), NEJM.** Randomised, placebo-controlled, 16 weeks, 400 healthy men aged 20-50. All got goserelin (suppresses endogenous T and estradiol). Cohort 1 (n=198) got placebo gel or testosterone gel 1.25, 2.5, 5, 10 g/day. Cohort 2 (n=202) got the same plus anastrozole (aromatase inhibitor, blocks T to estradiol). Sexual function was a secondary outcome: desire rated against pre-study sex drive on a 5-point scale (-2 much less to +2 much more); erectile function from a 3-item monthly self-report (difficulty aroused, difficulty with erection, difficulty reaching orgasm; 1 to 5). Analysed: desire n=184 (cohort 1) and 174 (cohort 2).
- Mean T by dose, cohort 1: placebo 44 +/- 13, 1.25 g 191 +/- 78, 2.5 g 337 +/- 173, 5 g 470 +/- 201, 10 g 805 +/- 355 ng/dL. Estradiol 3.6, 7.9, 11.9, 18.2, 33.3 pg/mL.
- Abstract: "In general, sexual desire declined as the testosterone dose was reduced." Both androgen and estrogen deficiency "contributed to the decline in sexual function".
- Full text, cohort 1 (estradiol intact): desire fell progressively with falling dose from 10 g to 0 g; all dose groups differed significantly from each other except 2.5 g vs 5 g. Erectile function worsened with placebo versus any T dose and declined more at 1.25 g (about 191 ng/dL) than at the three highest doses.
- Cohort 2 (estradiol blocked): desire fell significantly on placebo versus the three highest doses and more at 1.25 g than the two highest. Erectile function fell more on placebo than on T.
- Estradiol effect, men who got T: blocking aromatisation (cohort 2 vs 1) significantly decreased desire (P<0.001) and erectile function (P=0.022); cohort x dose interaction P=0.045 (desire) and P=0.032 (erections). Authors: estradiol had an independent effect.
- Authors' discussion: desire loss "routinely develops at higher mean testosterone levels" than lean-mass/strength loss (those preserved until T <= 200 ng/dL); erectile function was reduced at about 200 ng/dL. The 300-350 ng/dL figure in the paper refers to onset of fat gain, not desire.
- Limits: self-rated, young healthy men, T made abruptly low by goserelin (not a chronic state), gel T also raises DHT and DHT was not measured or separated in this design (the full text has no DHT analysis).

**Bhasin 2012, [PMID 22396515](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22396515/), JAMA.** RCT, 139 healthy men 18-50 (102 completed), GnRH-suppressed, T enanthate 50/125/300/600 mg/wk plus placebo or dutasteride 2.5 mg/d (blocks type 1 and 2 5-alpha-reductase, DHT largely lowered), 20 weeks. Sexual function was a secondary outcome; abstract: "Changes in fat mass, muscle strength, sexual function, prostate volume, sebum production, and hematocrit and lipid levels did not differ between groups." So with T supplied, blocking DHT conversion did not change sexual function over 20 weeks. Caveat: sexual-function instrument and numbers not in abstract (UNVERIFIED); T was replaced by injection, which is not the same as finasteride with intact T.

**Other controlled data on DHT and erections.**
- Cunningham 1995, [PMID 7775644](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7775644/): double-blind RCT, n=20 sexually active men 41-64, finasteride 5 mg/d vs placebo, 12 weeks. DHT fell to 28-31% of control. Sleep-related erections by polysomnography (before and week 12): no consistent suppression; no group x session effect for any best-night measure or self-reported sexual activity. Authors note finasteride mostly inhibits type 2 and type 1 is the main CNS enzyme, so DHT role in libido "is not excluded".
- Amory 2008, [PMID 18423697](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18423697/): double-blind RCT, n=99 healthy men 18-55, dutasteride 0.5 mg (n=33), finasteride 5 mg (n=34) or placebo (n=32) for 1 year, validated questionnaire, 24 weeks off-drug follow-up. "Self-assessed sexual function decreased slightly during treatment with both 5-alpha-reductase inhibitors but returned to baseline during follow-up." Authors: "minimal, reversible effect on ... sexual function in normal men". Numbers not in abstract (UNVERIFIED).
- Isidori 2005, [PMID 16181230](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16181230/): meta-analysis, 17 RCTs, 656 subjects, median 3 months. In men with mean baseline T below 12 nmol/L, T improved nocturnal erections, sexual thoughts and motivation, intercourse, erectile scores; no effect on erectile function in eugonadal men. Meta-regression: effect on erectile function, but not libido, inversely related to baseline T.
- Corona 2014, [PMID 24697970](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24697970/): meta-analysis, 41 RCTs. T improves erectile function and other aspects in hypogonadal men; role "uncertain in men who are not clearly hypogonadal"; publication bias detected.

Summary of what is shown: testosterone (largely via androgen action) and estradiol both contribute to desire and erections in a controlled human dose-response. A specific role for DHT is not isolated: dutasteride on top of T did not change sexual function (Bhasin 2012), finasteride 5 mg did not consistently change sleep erections (Cunningham 1995, n=20) and gave a slight, reversible fall in self-rated function (Amory 2008).

## 2. Dose-response and thresholds

- Finkelstein 2013 (above): desire declined progressively across the dose range; all dose groups differed except 2.5 g vs 5 g (about 337 vs 470 ng/dL), so the lower groups (placebo 44, 1.25 g 191 ng/dL) differed clearly from the rest. Individual thresholds varied widely ("varied widely in men"). Per-man hormone-to-symptom curve for a man with normal T: not available.
- Bhasin 2001, [PMID 11701431](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11701431/): 61 eugonadal men 18-35, GnRH agonist plus weekly T enanthate 25/50/125/300/600 mg, 20 weeks. Nadir T 253, 306, 542, 1,345, 2,370 ng/dL. "Sexual function, visual-spatial cognition and mood, and PSA levels did not change significantly at any dose." Even the lowest group (about 253 ng/dL) kept stable sexual function by their measure (instrument not in abstract, UNVERIFIED). Contrasts with Finkelstein's finding at similar T (191 and 337 ng/dL); different instruments and designs.
- Wu 2010 EMAS, [PMID 20554979](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20554979/), NEJM: random population sample n=3369 men 40-79, 8 European centres, questionnaires, morning total T by mass spectrometry, free T by Vermeulen. Poor morning erection, low sexual desire and ED were related to T; increased probability of these three appeared across total T 8.0-13.0 nmol/L (about 230-375 ng/dL) and free T 160-280 pmol/L. Late-onset hypogonadism defined as at least 3 sexual symptoms plus total T below 11 nmol/L (3.2 ng/mL, about 317 ng/dL) and free T below 220 pmol/L. Observational, ages 40-79 (he is 36).
- Marberger 2006, [PMID 16434455](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16434455/): 4,254 BPH men from dutasteride trials, questionnaires; sexual dysfunction frequency rose only below baseline T 225 ng/dL. Older men with BPH.
- Answer to "within the normal range?": the cross-sectional data (Wu, Marberger) show symptoms emerge in the low-normal to low range (roughly 225-375 ng/dL), not a smooth gradient across 500-900 ng/dL. Finkelstein shows dose-related desire differences down to about 337 vs 470 ng/dL in an experimental drop. No study found here tests libido against T inside a healthy man's own normal range over time (UNVERIFIED that none exists).

## 3. Why finasteride might lower libido or erections when serum T rises

Label facts (PROPECIA 12.2): DHT down about 65% within 24 h; "Mean circulating levels of testosterone and estradiol were increased by approximately 15% ... remained within the physiologic range"; no affinity for the androgen receptor; no change in LH, FSH, prolactin; HPT axis unaffected; "Finasteride has been found to cross the blood-brain barrier."

Proposed mechanisms, with level of evidence:
| Mechanism | Evidence | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Less DHT in penile tissue and loss of smooth-muscle integrity | Zhang 2013, [PMID 23876578](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23876578/): 30 aged (16-month) rats, 5-ARI 16 weeks; plasma DHT -52%, corpus cavernosum weight -22%, erectile response to nerve stimulation fell (P<.001), eNOS and smooth muscle down, more apoptosis | animal only, aged rats |
| Nitric oxide pathway (eNOS reduced as above) | Same rat study | animal only; human penile NO data on finasteride not found (UNVERIFIED) |
| Central type 1 5-alpha-reductase and DHT in libido | Cunningham 1995 (reasoning in the abstract, no CNS measurement) | hypothesis, not tested in humans |
| Neurosteroid changes (pregnenolone, progesterone, dihydroprogesterone, allopregnanolone-type metabolites) | Melcangi 2017, [PMID 28408350](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28408350/): 16 post-finasteride-syndrome (PFS) patients; CSF: lower pregnenolone, progesterone, dihydroprogesterone, DHT, 17beta-estradiol; higher DHEA, T, 3alpha-diol; all 16 had ED; 8 had major depression; abnormal pudendal-nerve evoked potentials in severe ED | human, but case series of symptomatic ex-users, no control group in the abstract, cannot show cause; and these are persistent-symptom patients, not typical users |
| Allopregnanolone effects on sexual behaviour in rodents | Not retrieved (UNVERIFIED) | not verified |
| Androgen receptor changes or SRD5A2 methylation | Melcangi 2019, [PMID 31272082](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31272082/), CSF methylation pilot: title only read | UNVERIFIED (abstract not read) |
| Reviews of persistent effects | Traish 2011, [PMID 21176115](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21176115/): narrative review, subset with persistent diminished libido/ED/depression, mechanisms "possible" | review, no new data |

Also relevant: the label figure that sexual AEs are small in absolute terms (3.8% vs 2.1%, in existing note) shows only a minority is affected. No human study found here that measures libido against DHT level within finasteride users (UNVERIFIED that none exists).

## 4. Nocebo: Mondaini 2007

[PMID 17655657](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17655657/), J Sex Med. Design: randomised, n=120 sexually active BPH patients with IIEF-EF >= 25 (normal erectile function), finasteride 5 mg/day for 1 year, disguised as "compound X of proven efficacy for BPH". Group 1 not told of sexual side effects; group 2 told ("it may cause erectile dysfunction, decreased libido, problems of ejaculation but these are uncommon"). Side effects assessed at 6 and 12 months with the MSF-4 questionnaire and a self-administered questionnaire. 107 completed (group 1 n=52, group 2 n=55).
- One or more sexual side effects: 43.6% informed vs 15.3% uninformed (P=0.03).
- ED 9.6% vs 30.9% (P=0.02); decreased libido 7.7% vs 23.6% (P=0.04); ejaculation disorders 5.7% vs 16.3% (P=0.06, not significant).
- Caveats: older BPH men on 5 mg, not young men on 1 mg; the trial has no placebo arm, so it cannot say how much of 15.3% is drug and the nocebo estimate is the difference between two active-drug arms.
- Replication: none found in PubMed (searches for finasteride nocebo informed uninformed returned only commentary and pharmacovigilance papers). Gupta 2026 ([PMID 41524216](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41524216/), FAERS) and Trüeb 2019 ([PMID 31559258](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31559258/), opinion piece) argue a nocebo role but are not trials. Other-drug nocebo trials: not retrieved (UNVERIFIED).

## 5. Dose and time course of sexual AEs

- Dose comparison: no head-to-head 1 mg vs 5 mg trial of sexual AEs with numbers was read. Roberts 1999 and Drake 1999 (existing note) compare efficacy and DHT, not sexual AEs (UNVERIFIED for sexual AEs). Label numbers come from different populations and cannot be compared directly:
  - PROPECIA 1 mg, 12 months, men with hair loss: decreased libido 1.8% vs 1.3%; ED 1.3% vs 0.7%; ejaculation disorder 1.2% vs 0.7%; any 3.8% (36/945) vs 2.1% (20/934), P=0.04.
  - PROSCAR 5 mg (PLESS, BPH, n=1524 vs 1516), year 1: impotence 8.1% vs 3.7%; decreased libido 6.4% vs 3.4%; ejaculation disorder 0.8% vs 0.1%. Years 2-4 combined: impotence 5.1% vs 5.1%; libido 2.6% vs 2.6%.
- Hirshburg 2016, [PMID 27672412](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27672412/): systematic review of 5-ARIs; sexual effects "as many as 3.4 to 15.8 percent". Low-quality summary.
- Amory 2008 (above): finasteride 5 mg and dutasteride 0.5 mg both lowered self-rated sexual function slightly in healthy men over 1 year; back to baseline 24 weeks after stopping.
- Time course, label wording (PROPECIA 6.1): "Resolution occurred in men who discontinued therapy ... due to these side effects and in most of those who continued therapy. The incidence of each of the above adverse experiences decreased to <=0.3% by the fifth year of treatment." PROSCAR: "There is no evidence of increased sexual adverse experiences with increased duration of treatment ... New reports of drug-related sexual adverse experiences decreased with duration of therapy." Numbers of men who resolved on continuing: not given in the label (UNVERIFIED). Postmarketing: sexual dysfunction that "continued after discontinuation of treatment" reported, frequency not estimable.

## 6. Energy deficit, T and libido; recovery after a cut

| Source | Design / n / duration | Number |
|---|---|---|
| Friedl 2000, [PMID 10797147](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10797147/) | US Army Ranger course, 8 weeks, group 1 n=49 and group 2 n=48, energy deficit about 1,200 and 1,000 kcal/day with sleep loss, workload, cold | Group 1 T "approached castrate levels" (4.5 +/- 3.9 nmol/L, about 130 ng/dL); T3 78 ng/dL; IGF-I halved. Refeeding "produced prompt recovery of T, T3 and IGF-I" even with other stressors continuing. Higher intake attenuated the changes. Serum hormones by assay (not stated in abstract). Multistressor, very lean, not comparable to a 4-month moderate cut |
| Rossow 2013, [PMID 23412685](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23412685/) | One drug-free bodybuilder, age 26-27, 6 mo before and 6 mo after competition | Body fat 14.8% to 4.5%; T 9.22 to 2.27 ng/mL (922 to 227 ng/dL), back to 9.91 ng/mL after competition; mood disturbance 6 to 43 and back to 4 at 6 mo; strength did not fully recover in 6 mo. Libido not reported in abstract |
| Pardue 2017, [PMID 28770669](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28770669/) | One drug-free male bodybuilder, 8 mo prep plus 5 mo recovery (13 mo) | T 623 to 173 ng/dL (M1 to M8); intake 3,860 to 1,724 kcal/day; lost 9.1 kg; RMR 107% to 81% of predicted; by M13 "largely, but not entirely, reversed" (body fat DXA 13.8% start, 5.1% peak lean, 13.8% at M13). Libido not reported in abstract |
| Mäestu 2010, [PMID 20300017](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20300017/) | 14 male bodybuilders (7 dieting, 7 control), 11 weeks | T fell only from week 11 to 5 before competition: 20.3 +/- 6.0 to 18.0 +/- 6.8 nmol/L (about 585 to 520 ng/dL, still normal); IGF-1 and insulin fell significantly |
| Schoenfeld 2023, [PMID 37218855](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37218855/) | Systematic review of 11 case studies, 15 drug-free athletes (8 male) | "Marked alterations" in hormones with high individual variability; numbers not in abstract |
| Corona 2013, [PMID 23482592](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23482592/) | Meta-analysis, 24 studies, obese men | Weight loss raised T (low-calorie diet +2.87 nmol/L); effect larger with more weight loss. Obese, not lean |
| Khoo 2011, [PMID 21819545](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21819545/) | Obese diabetic men, low-energy vs high-protein diets | Abstract text not fully read for numbers; UNVERIFIED |

- Size of drop: ranges from none to small (Mäestu, 11-week prep in already high-T athletes) to 60-75% in extreme single-case preps (Rossow -75%, Pardue -72%) and near castrate in the Ranger course. Where body fat went to 4-5%, T fell most.
- Recovery: Friedl: prompt on refeeding (time not given in abstract). Rossow: back to baseline measured at the post-competition point within the 6-month recovery window (exact week not in abstract, UNVERIFIED). Pardue: largely reversed by 5 months. The Minnesota Starvation Experiment (Keys 1950, libido loss): not on PubMed with abstract, not read, UNVERIFIED.
- Libido in these studies: not reported in the abstracts. Nothing here measures libido against T during a moderate cut in a man at about 15-20% body fat. A cut from 102 to 88 kg over 4 months is far less extreme than the case-study preps; his own T was not measured in these sources.

## 7. Resistance training, resting T and libido

- Potter 2021, [PMID 35134000](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35134000/): systematic review/meta-analysis of 11 RCTs, 421 insufficiently active, apparently healthy men 19-75, aerobic, resistance or combined, median 12 weeks, resting total T. Standardised mean difference 0.00 (95% CI -0.20 to 0.20); no difference by mode, age, body-mass status. "Exercise training does not seem to affect resting total or free testosterone concentration in insufficiently active, eugonadal men." Limits: untrained men, short; says nothing about 5 d/wk lifters.
- Acute spikes: Vingren 2010, [PMID 21058750](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21058750/): review; T generally rises directly after heavy resistance exercise in men. West 2010, [PMID 19910330](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19910330/): 12 men, 15 weeks, arm training with vs without a leg session to spike T/GH/IGF-1; CSA +12% vs +10%, strength gains same: spikes did not add growth. West 2012, [PMID 22105707](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22105707/): n=56, acute free-T response not correlated with gains in lean mass or strength. Morton 2016, [PMID 27174923](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27174923/): n=49 resistance-trained men, 12 weeks, no correlation between post-exercise hormone rises and hypertrophy or strength. Meaning: acute post-session rises are transient and are not a marker of anabolism or of a change in resting T.
- Libido: no RCT found on resistance training and libido in healthy men. Gerbild 2018, [PMID 29661646](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29661646/): systematic review (10 studies), aerobic exercise (about 160 min/week for 6 months) reduced ED in men with inactivity, obesity, hypertension, metabolic syndrome or CVD; not healthy trained men. Libido in trained men: UNVERIFIED.

## 8. Sleep and T

Leproult 2011, [PMID 21632481](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21632481/), JAMA research letter (data read from PMC4445839): 10 healthy men, mean age 24.3, BMI 23.5 (28 consented, 10 passed screening and completed). Lab protocol: 3 nights of 10 h in bed then 8 nights of 5 h in bed (12:30-5:30). Blood every 15-30 min for 24 h after the second 10-h night and the seventh 5-h night; total testosterone by immunochemiluminescent assay. Total sleep fell from 8 h 55 min to 4 h 48 min. Daytime T (08:00-22:00): 16.5 +/- 2.8 vs 18.4 +/- 3.8 nmol/L (about 475 vs 530 ng/dL), P=0.049; 14:00-22:00: 15.5 vs 17.9 nmol/L, P=0.02. Authors: decreased 10-15%. Vigor scores fell from 28 to 19 (P=0.002). Cortisol unchanged. Libido not measured. Limits: n=10, convenience sample, immunoassay, one week; their own words "small convenience sample".
- Penev 2007, [PMID 17520786](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17520786/): 12 men aged 64-74, observational; polysomnographic sleep time predicted morning total T (Beta 0.792, P=0.017) and free T (Beta 0.741, P=0.029). Older, tiny.
- Relevance to about 6.4 h/night: the only experiment found restricted to 5 h for 8 nights (Leproult). Dose-response between 5 and 8 h, and effect at 6.4 h in a man of 36: not found (UNVERIFIED).
