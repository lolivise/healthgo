# Freezing raw chicken breast in marinade, thawing method, and food safety (home cook, Perth AU)

Evidence grades used on every bullet: **[peer-reviewed]**, **[official guidance]**, **[expert-tested]** (America's Test Kitchen / Cook's Illustrated), **[anecdotal]**, **[secondary]** (a summary of a primary source I could not open myself; treat as unverified). Numbers are copied from the sources and nothing is invented. Several primary pages (FSIS, PMC, NSW Food Authority) blocked automated fetching (HTTP 403, timeout or bot-check); where the wording came via a secondary page, that is stated.

## 1. How much does one freeze-thaw cycle add to drip loss and cooking loss, and does it make chicken breast tougher? (freezing rate, storage time, temperature fluctuation)

### Takeaway
One freeze-thaw cycle measurably increases thaw/drip loss (roughly 3 points of thaw loss plus about 2.5 points more drip in the one study I could read in full), but in that same study freezing lowered shear force (more tender, not tougher). The one-cycle rise in cooking loss was small and not significant, and it grew clearly only from cycle 2 onward. Freezing alone does not explain a "tough/chewy" result. Dryness is consistent with extra water loss, but toughness points to other causes (overcooking, woody-breast fillets, see Inferences).

### Cited Findings
- **One cycle vs fresh, chicken breast (Ali et al. 2022, J. Anim. Plant Sci. 32(5):1554-1560).** 200 g breast pieces, individually bagged, frozen to core -18 C +/-2 for 4 days per cycle. C0 = fresh control, C1 = one freeze-thaw cycle, up to C4. Table 2 values (mean): **thawing loss** C1 2.98%, C2 5.86%, C3 8.28%, C4 10%; **drip loss** C0 4.14%, C1 6.61%, C2 7.81%, C3 7.70%, C4 8.41%; **cooking loss** C0 14.99%, C1 16.03% (not significantly different from C0), C2 20.77% (also not flagged significant), C3 22.32%, C4 24.09%. **Shear force** fell from 34.75 N (fresh) to 18.01 N at C4, i.e. progressively more tender. Authors attribute the water loss to ice-crystal damage and lipid/protein oxidation. Small-sample Pakistani university study; carcasses sampled 2 h post-slaughter, which is fresher than supermarket meat. **[peer-reviewed, lower-tier journal]** — [PDF](https://thejaps.org.pk/manuscript/as-20-0126.pdf)
- **Freezing/thawing usually tenderises.** The Ali paper cites earlier work that multiple cycles reduce shear force and increase tenderness (Farouk 2004, Lagerstedt 2008), attributing it to protein/membrane denaturation. A search summary of other work notes ice-crystal damage can also lower juiciness even where shear drops. **[peer-reviewed, secondary for the cited works]** — [Ali et al. 2022](https://thejaps.org.pk/manuscript/as-20-0126.pdf); [search summary](https://www.redalyc.org/pdf/1797/179742203006.pdf)
- **Conflicting direction on thaw loss across cycles.** Search-engine summaries of a six-cycle breast study and an LF-NMR breast/thigh study report drip/thaw loss *decreasing* with cycles while free-water proportion falls. I did not read those papers, so I cannot reconcile them with Ali et al. **[secondary; unverified]** — [repository.ias.ac.in/130365](https://repository.ias.ac.in/130365) (page returned 404 when fetched; do not rely on it)
- **Freezing rate, household vs blast freezer (Iowa State honors project, Gregg 2020).** Paired breast fillet halves frozen at -18 C (household) vs -40 C (blast), then all stored 4 days in household freezer, cooked, cooled. Household-frozen fillets lost more moisture during freezing (P=0.005); a puncture-probe texture test found no difference by freezing condition. Cook loss varied with wooden-breast severity (P=0.039), not freezing method. Student project, small (n=6 per severity group); search-summary access only. **[peer-reviewed-adjacent / secondary]** — [Iowa State](https://dr.lib.iastate.edu/entities/publication/0b80af8e-7f20-474d-bff1-c1c3ab6c7a43)
- **Freezing rate, ostrich (not chicken; mechanism only).** Thaw loss rose with slower freezing: 1 h 2.57%, 2 h 3.00%, 4 h 3.93%, 8 h 5.26%, 24 h 6.24%. The fastest freeze (1 h) was also the toughest (65-57 N). Thawing rate (1.5 to 21 h) had no significant effect. Shows speed/toughness trade-off exists, but species differs. **[peer-reviewed, different species]** — [Leygonie & Hoffman 2020, Foods 9(11):1624](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7694958)
- **Storage time at -18 C.** Search summaries report drip and cooking loss rising gradually over 45 days (vacuum-packed broiler cut-ups at about -21.5 C; breast showed highest cooking loss, 37.36%) and WHC falling through up to 12 months. These were not read in full and don't match a 0-2 week window. **[secondary]** — [foods 09 00741](https://mdpi-res.com/d_attachment/foods/foods-09-00741/article_deploy/foods-09-00741.pdf); [UNESP](https://repositorio.unesp.br/handle/11449/170745)
- **Temperature fluctuation.** Ali et al. list storage temperature, temperature fluctuation and freezing/thawing rate as factors accelerating non-microbial damage (citing Xia et al. 2009). No quantified effect of defrost-cycling freezers found. **[peer-reviewed, qualitative]** — [Ali et al. 2022](https://thejaps.org.pk/manuscript/as-20-0126.pdf)

### Inferences
- A single freeze-thaw cycle is a small-to-moderate contributor to dryness (a few percentage points of water lost at thaw) and is not a plausible cause of toughness. If the chicken is dry AND chewy, the likelier culprits are final cooking temperature/time and fillet quality (woody-breast myopathy fillets are significantly firmer raw and cooked per a cited Atlas of Science summary: [source](https://atlasofscience.org/the-woody-breast-condition-affects-texture-characteristics-of-both-raw-and-cooked-chicken-breast-meat/?format=pdf), read only via search snippet). These sit outside this note's scope but the writer may want to cross-check with the cooking-side researcher.
- Because the batches are frozen only up to ~2 weeks, storage-duration effects are probably minor next to the freeze-thaw event itself and the thaw method.

### Gaps
- No study found measuring one freeze-thaw cycle on *marinated* breast with a fresh-marinated control in a home-freezer setting with cooking loss and sensory juiciness together.
- No quantified effect of home-freezer freezing time (hours to freeze a ~1 kg bag of pieces) on chicken breast drip loss. The only freezing-rate numbers I could verify are ostrich.
- Cooking-loss rise from fresh to C1 in Ali et al. is stated as non-significant; the sensory (juiciness) effect of that 1-point change was not measured.

## 2. Does freezing IN a marinade change this? (salt, phosphate, bicarbonate, acid, marinating during frozen storage)

### Takeaway
Salt (and especially salt plus phosphate) applied before freezing reduces thaw and cooking loss; Cook's Illustrated's test had salted-before-freezing chicken lose 11% vs 22% for untreated frozen. At his levels (0.4% and 0.8% salt-equivalent) there is no direct evidence of benefit; most studies use much stronger brines. Acid-plus-freeze "mushy" claims are anecdotal; I found no controlled study showing acidic marinade plus freezing worsens breast texture.

### Cited Findings
- **Salt before freezing beats after thawing (Cook's Illustrated test).** Nine boneless skinless breasts frozen 1 week, cooked sous vide, moisture loss measured: **salted before freezing 11%; brined before freezing 15%; salted after thawing 16%; brined after thawing 20%; untreated frozen 22%.** Salting = rested 1 h; brine = 45 min. Explanation given: treating before freezing gives the salt more time to work, and dry salt makes a more concentrated surface brine. Caveat from the testers: very high salt can hurt moisture retention. n=3 per condition; salt quantity not stated on the page; no fresh (never-frozen) control reported. **[expert-tested]** — [ATK/Cook's Illustrated](https://www.americastestkitchen.com/how_tos/9716-when-to-treat-chicken-before-or-after-freezing)
- **Brine-then-freeze two weeks (ATK).** Brined breasts frozen two weeks, defrosted, cooked vs frozen-plain-then-brined and freshly brined: "slight textural differences, but all three were moist and juicy"; verdict "brine and freeze. Or freeze, then brine." **[expert-tested, no numbers]** — [ATK](https://www.americastestkitchen.com/how_tos/6017-freezing-brined-chicken-breasts)
- **Salt + phosphate in frozen breast (Food Sci. Anim. Resour. 2023, 43(2):245-268).** Broiler breast fillets injected with brine (all with 1.5% NaCl), frozen -18 C 7 days, thawed overnight 4 C. Thawing loss at 1 day post-thaw: salt-only control 9.40%, salt + 0.3% STPP 4.43%; at 3 days 14.45% vs 7.87%. Cooking loss (1 d): 22.63% vs 19.86%. Shear force (kgf, 1 d): 1.16 vs 0.90. Alternatives: yeast-and-lemon extract thaw loss 4.69%, cooking loss 19.59%; prune juice did worse than STPP. Injection brine, not a home soak, and a commercial phosphate: he is not using one. **[peer-reviewed]** — [PMC9998199](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9998199)
- **Salt vs pH as the water-retention driver.** Kim et al. 2014 (Poultry Science 93(3):680-686) on vacuum-tumbled breast: acidic marinades lowered pH and raised collagen solubility vs a 4% salt solution, lowering shear force, but water-holding capacity and cooking yield were driven mainly by salt content rather than pH. Only a trade-news summary was available. **[peer-reviewed, secondary]** — [thepoultrysite summary](https://www.thepoultrysite.com/news/2014/04/soy-sauce-marination-makes-chicken-tender)
- **Acid marinades on fresh breast.** Apple cider vinegar (up to 100%) gave the lowest shear force vs acetic acid and water in a 48 h marination (Unal et al. 2020); lactic acid and sodium bicarbonate both improved spent-hen breast tenderness via water-holding changes (Koeipudsa et al. 2019: bicarbonate raised pH, lactic acid lowered it). Fresh meat, not frozen, and at stronger acid than a mayo/mustard marinade. **[peer-reviewed, secondary snippets]** — [acikerisim.selcuk.edu.tr](https://acikerisim.selcuk.edu.tr/items/71bb0537-5413-4a9a-852c-2b527ce8592f/full); [doi.nrct.go.th](https://doi.nrct.go.th/ListDoi/listDetail/10.14456%2Fapst.2019.36)
- **Yoghurt-based acidic marinade on chicken (Karageorgou et al. 2023, Foods 12:2360).** Yoghurt acid whey, pH 4.5, 4 C, 10-15 h: chicken marinade uptake about 5% (4.97-5.22%), shear force and cooking loss **not affected**. Frozen storage (-20 C, 30 and 60 days) was measured only for lipid oxidation (MDA), which was lower in marinated chicken. No texture result for frozen chicken. **[peer-reviewed]** — [MDPI PDF](https://mdpi-res.com/d_attachment/foods/foods-12-02360/article_deploy/foods-12-02360.pdf)
- **Does marinating continue while frozen?** Food-media claims: freezing pauses marinating, and the marinade only penetrates during thaw in the fridge; also that a mayo emulsion can split in the cold. No controlled study found. **[anecdotal]** — [Tasting Table](https://www.tastingtable.com/1845632/why-season-chicken-after-freezing); [Chowhound](https://www.chowhound.com/1860361/marinate-chicken-before-freezing)
- **"Acidic marinade plus freezing = mushy".** Claim that ice crystals plus acid contact make meat mushier: **[anecdotal]** — [The Takeout](https://thetakeout.com/1712233/mistake-over-marinate-chicken). A Food52 reply says mushiness comes from enzymatic tenderisers (pineapple, papaya), not mild acids like citrus/acetic: **[anecdotal]** — [Food52](https://food52.com/hotline/33227)
- **Too much brine backfires.** A consumer-oriented review notes heavily injected muscle swells and turns soft or spongy. **[secondary]** — [earthwormexpress](https://earthwormexpress.com/the-meat-factory/meat-science-research/the-hidden-water-injection-tumble-marination-and-cavity-filling-in-whole-frozen-poultry-a-consumer-expose-with-scientific-commentary/)

### Inferences
- His 0.4% salt-equivalent mayo marinade is probably below the level that gives a meaningful water-binding or cryoprotective benefit; the yoghurt marinade at 0.8% is closer but still low. For reference only: 1.5% NaCl brine (injected, as % of brine) in the phosphate study and an unspecified dry-salt dose in ATK's test. A direct comparison of dose is not possible from these sources.
- The sourced evidence suggests the marinade's acid is unlikely to damage texture within the ~2-week frozen window and a few hours of thaw, but the evidence is indirect (fresh meat, other acids, other doses).
- Bicarbonate-based water-binding is documented for fresh spent-hen meat but I found no frozen-marinade study using bicarbonate; no baking-soda dose can be recommended from these sources.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed study of home-style mayo/mustard/Worcestershire or Greek-yoghurt marinades frozen with the meat.
- No data on honey/sugar content effect on freeze-thaw drip loss.
- ATK's salt dose for the "salted" group was not shown on the page I could read.
- Unverified whether Kim 2014 effect sizes apply at 0.4-0.8% salt.

## 3. Marinate-then-freeze, freeze plain then marinate after thawing, or cook-then-freeze?

### Takeaway
In the only quantitative head-to-head found (ATK, salt/brine, 1 week frozen), treating before freezing beat treating after thawing (11% vs 16% loss for salt; 15% vs 20% for brine). ATK's separate two-week test found "brine and freeze" and "freeze, then brine" both moist and juicy. I found no controlled study comparing cook-then-freeze with raw-freeze for chicken breast texture.

### Cited Findings
- Salt/brine before freezing lost less moisture than the same treatment after thawing: 11% vs 16% (salt), 15% vs 20% (brine); untreated 22%. **[expert-tested]** — [ATK/Cook's Illustrated](https://www.americastestkitchen.com/how_tos/9716-when-to-treat-chicken-before-or-after-freezing)
- Two-week frozen brined chicken vs freeze-then-brine: both "moist and juicy", slight textural differences, "Go for it." **[expert-tested]** — [ATK](https://www.americastestkitchen.com/how_tos/6017-freezing-brined-chicken-breasts)
- Food-media view that seasoning after freezing gives better marinade penetration: **[anecdotal]** — [Tasting Table](https://www.tastingtable.com/1845632/why-season-chicken-after-freezing)
- Cooked-then-frozen route: CSIRO and Safe Food Qld advise cooking excess defrosted meat, portioning, and freezing "once it has stopped steaming" as a safe way to re-freeze, but no texture data. **[official guidance, no texture data]** — [CSIRO](https://research.csiro.au/foodsafety/five-food-safety-myths-busted); [Safe Food Qld](https://www.safefood.qld.gov.au/?p=18400)
- Precooked breast subjected to three freeze-thaw cycles: a cultured sugar/vinegar blend (3%) improved tenderness (shear force) in frozen and refrigerated ready-to-eat breast. Treats vinegar as texture-improving, not damaging. **[peer-reviewed]** — [Park et al. 2014, Poultry Science 93(3):719-727](https://academic.oup.com/ps/article/93/3/719/1557298)

### Inferences
- Salting/marinating before freezing is supported by the available expert test; the marinade only has to deliver salt (and fat, as in mayo) for the benefit, and a short salt exposure is enough in ATK's tests.
- Cook-then-freeze means two heat events and a reheat on already-cooked chicken; with no data I would not expect it to fix dryness, since reheating cooked breast is a common dryness cause (inference, no source).

### Gaps
- No quantitative comparison of cook-then-freeze vs raw freeze for chicken breast texture found.
- No taster-panel sensory scores (juiciness/chewiness) from either ATK test were published on the pages I could read.

## 4. Thawing methods: drip loss, texture, time for ~1 kg bags, safety, and official guidance

### Takeaway
Fridge or cold-water thawing gave the lowest drip loss in the studies found; microwave thawing gave the highest. Official Australian guidance prefers the fridge (at or below 5 C), allows microwave (cook immediately) and cold water as a quicker option, and warns against the bench. USDA permits cooking from frozen (about 50% longer) and says cold-water and microwave-thawed raw poultry must be cooked before refreezing; Australian guidance is silent on cold water refreezing.

### Cited Findings
- **Five thawing methods, frozen chicken half breasts (Oliveira et al. 2015, Braz. J. Poultry Sci. 17(2)).** About 250 g breasts, fast-frozen to -36 C in 2 h, stored at -18 C; thawed until core reached 10 C. Drip loss (mean): **cold water bag at about 10 C (2 h 15 min) 1.27%; oven at about 40 C (2 h 15 min) 3.55%; refrigerator at about 7 C (22 h) 3.60%; room temperature about 17 C (2 h 20 min) 4.84%; microwave (6 min) 7.65%.** Shear force (kgf/cm2, cooked to 72 C): cold water 1.84, fridge 2.60, room temp 2.58, oven 2.69, microwave 3.20 (highest). Authors credit cold water with the least cell-structure damage. Cooking loss was not reported. Note the fridge here was about 7 C, above the Australian 5 C target. **[peer-reviewed]** — [SciELO](https://www.scielo.br/j/rbca/a/fC7R45ZBLQfVfzWHSkGKQCs/?lang=en)
- **Home thawing practices (Benli 2016, Asian-Australas. J. Anim. Sci. 29(1):100-108).** Microwave drip loss 3.47% (highest), refrigerator 0.62% (lowest). Cooking loss: microwave 18.29%, refrigerator 18.53% (lowest of the home methods). No significant differences in textural parameters of defrosted-then-cooked samples. Counter, warm water, microwave, tap water were the other methods; their values weren't visible on the abstract page. **[peer-reviewed]** — [Avesis record](https://avesis.cu.edu.tr/yayin/5bb0169d-a7b7-43fe-b594-337fdc6b3fc4/consumer-attitudes-toward-storing-and-thawing-chicken-and-effects-of-the-common-thawing-practices-on-some-quality-characteristics-of-frozen-chicken)
- **Thawing rate (ostrich).** No significant quality effect across 1.5-21 h thaw. **[peer-reviewed, different species]** — [Leygonie & Hoffman 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7694958)
- **Thaw method rankings elsewhere.** Search summaries: fridge preserved fibre structure better than microwave; warm water or 25 C raised drip loss and bacterial counts (Egyptian study); microwave and room temperature lost the most protein in exudate. **[secondary]** — [PMC4698676](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4698676); [ejah.journals.ekb.eg](https://ejah.journals.ekb.eg/article_416823.html)
- **Fridge thaw time.** USDA via Texas A&M handout: "24 hours per 5 pounds" for large frozen items, i.e. about 10 h per kg; after fridge thawing, cook or refreeze poultry within 1-2 days. A Canadian chicken-industry page estimates about 10 h per kg (secondary snippet). In Oliveira et al., 250 g breasts needed 22 h at about 7 C. **[official guidance (US) via university handout]** — [Texas A&M PDF](https://foodsafety.tamu.edu/files/2020/06/Understanding-Thawing_-3-Easy-Ways-to-Safely-Defrost-Frozen-Food.pdf); [chicken.ca](https://www.chicken.ca/cooking-tips/storage-and-thawing-guide)
- **Cold water (USDA via same handout).** Leak-proof bag fully submerged in cold water, replace water every 30 minutes; "1 pound: 1 hour or less; 4-12 pounds: 2-6 hours" (no figure is given for 1 kg / 2.2 lb; that falls between the two tiers); must cook immediately after. **[official guidance (US)]** — [Texas A&M PDF](https://foodsafety.tamu.edu/files/2020/06/Understanding-Thawing_-3-Easy-Ways-to-Safely-Defrost-Frozen-Food.pdf)
- **Microwave.** USDA: cook immediately because parts may warm into the danger zone. Safe Food Queensland: use auto-defrost "but cook it immediately afterwards", only if urgent. **[official guidance]** — [Texas A&M PDF](https://foodsafety.tamu.edu/files/2020/06/Understanding-Thawing_-3-Easy-Ways-to-Safely-Defrost-Frozen-Food.pdf); [Safe Food Qld](https://www.safefood.qld.gov.au/?p=18400)
- **Cook from frozen.** USDA: safe to cook without thawing; takes about 50% longer. **[official guidance (US)]** — [Texas A&M PDF](https://foodsafety.tamu.edu/files/2020/06/Understanding-Thawing_-3-Easy-Ways-to-Safely-Defrost-Frozen-Food.pdf). I found no peer-reviewed or expert-tested air-fryer comparison of cooking from frozen vs thawed; the one marinated-from-frozen angle is untested.
- **Australian guidance on method.** Fridge at or below 5 C is the safest, up to 24 hours: Safe Food Production Queensland (relaying FSIC). **[official guidance]** — [Safe Food Qld](https://www.safefood.qld.gov.au/newsroom/refreezing-chicken-meat/). WA local government (Kwinana, Dept of Health WA wording): thaw in the bottom of the fridge; for speed, microwave, or "as a last resort, under cool running water"; never on a bench. **[official guidance, local government]** — [Shire of Kwinana](https://www.kwinana.wa.gov.au/community/health,-safety-and-security/food-safety/thawing,-cooking,-cooling-and-reheating-food-safel). Foodwise, citing NSW Food Authority: fridge, microwave on defrost, or in water in a watertight pack, changing water every 30 min; never on the bench. **[secondary for NSW]** — [Foodwise](https://www.foodwise.com.au/?p=5740)
- **USDA vs Australian difference on cold water.** USDA gives explicit cold-water instructions (30 min changes). Safe Food Qld and CSIRO pages I could read do not cover cold water. The Kwinana page treats running water as a last resort. **[official guidance]**.

### Inferences
- For ~1 kg bags of cut pieces: fridge thaw time depends on piece size and bag thickness. A flat, single-layer bag should thaw much faster than a block, plausibly overnight (about 8-12 h), but no source gives a time for 1 kg of pieces. The USDA figure of about 10 h/kg is for solid mass.
- Given the 24 h limit in Australian fridge-thaw wording, an overnight fridge thaw (about 12-18 h) fits comfortably, and thawing a bag at the start of the day before cooking is safe so long as the fridge is at or below 5 C.
- Fridge or cold-water thaw is the best-supported route for minimising drip; microwave defrost should be avoided for texture.
- Since marinated chicken is discarded of marinade after thaw, the thaw drip (which carries protein and water) is lost either way. Draining and patting dry is likely to help air-fryer browning, but that is cooking-side advice and unsourced here.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed comparison of air-frying marinated chicken pieces from frozen vs thawed.
- No primary source found for fridge thaw time for ~1 kg of cut chicken pieces.
- FSANZ's own consumer wording on thawing was not found; NSW Food Authority's page wasn't retrievable (relied on Foodwise secondary).
- FSIS primary pages blocked (HTTP 403); USDA numbers are via a Texas A&M extension handout and search summaries of FSIS.

## 5. Freezer packing tips that help texture

### Takeaway
Direct poultry-specific evidence is thin. Mechanistic evidence says faster freezing means less thaw loss (ostrich) and the household freezer loses more moisture than a blast freezer (Iowa State project), so thinner flat packs freezing quickly should help; air removal is for freezer burn.

### Cited Findings
- Faster freezing lowered thaw loss: 2.57% at 1 h vs 6.24% at 24 h (ostrich). **[peer-reviewed, different species]** — [Leygonie & Hoffman 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7694958)
- Household freezer (-18 C) vs blast (-40 C): more moisture lost during freezing at -18 C (P=0.005). **[peer-reviewed-adjacent]** — [Iowa State](https://dr.lib.iastate.edu/entities/publication/0b80af8e-7f20-474d-bff1-c1c3ab6c7a43)
- Flat freezing: press food to about 1-2 cm, leave about 2 cm at the bag's open end, freeze on a tray so it stays flat; flat packs freeze and thaw faster; pressing out air protects against freezer burn; portioning stops a single giant clump. UK/recipe-site tips, not tested. **[anecdotal]** — [loavesanddishes](https://www.loavesanddishes.net/wprm_print/how-to-freeze-chicken); [Food Republic](https://www.foodrepublic.com/1427426/flatten-frozen-foods-best-freezer-storage)
- Safe Food Qld: if not using fresh chicken within 2-3 days, "divide large pieces into usable portions and freeze immediately". **[official guidance]** — [Safe Food Qld](https://www.safefood.qld.gov.au/?p=18400)
- Ali et al. 2022 individually bagged each piece before freezing, one cycle per bag; the C1 result applies to that format. **[peer-reviewed]**

### Inferences
- Portion by cook-day (one bag per planned meal) so no bag is thawed, part-used and refrozen. That avoids a second cycle, which Ali et al. associate with compounding loss (thaw loss 2.98% at C1 rising to 5.86% at C2).
- Chilling the marinaded pieces in the fridge first, then freezing in a thin single layer on a tray before bagging, would plausibly speed freezing; not directly tested on chicken.

### Gaps
- No chicken-specific data on bag thickness, vacuum vs zip-bag, or portion size vs drip loss.
- Freezer-door and fluctuation effects not quantified.

## 6. Food safety: discarding used marinade, cooked-chicken fridge life (AU), refreezing thawed raw chicken

### Takeaway
Discarding the used raw-chicken marinade is correct; USDA's alternative is to boil it first. Australian guidance: raw chicken 2-3 days in the fridge; cooked leftovers: official sources I could read vary (NSW flyer says eat or freeze within 3 days; CSIRO microbiologist in media says up to 4). Thawed raw chicken may be refrozen if fridge-thawed at or below 5 C and not thawed for more than 24 h (FSIC via Safe Food Qld); USDA also allows refreezing fridge-thawed raw chicken but requires cooking first if thawed by cold water or microwave.

### Cited Findings
- **Marinade discard.** USDA: marinade used on raw meat/poultry must be boiled for a few minutes before use as a sauce or dip; safest approach is to set aside unused marinade before adding raw meat and discard anything that touched raw meat. Marinate in the refrigerator, never at room temperature. **[official guidance (US) via Consumer Reports, and a USDA search summary]** — [Consumer Reports](https://www.consumerreports.org/food-safety/how-to-marinate-meat-safely); [usda.gov](https://www.usda.gov/node/64541) (not retrievable). I did not find an Australian government page specifically on used marinade; NSW/FSANZ marinade wording not located.
- **Marinade time limits conflict.** HealthDay citing USDA says poultry can marinate up to 2 days; Illinois Extension says no more than 24 h; Consumer Reports gives poultry 2-24 h. Not verified against FSIS directly. **[secondary; conflicting]** — [HealthDay](https://www.healthday.com/health-news/infectious-disease/health-tip-marinating-meat-678167.html); [Illinois Extension](https://extension.illinois.edu/blogs/be-smart-eat-well-get-healthy/2017-04-21-should-you-wash-raw-meat-cooking)
- **Fridge temperature and raw life.** Fridge at or below 5 C; raw chicken within 2-3 days; portion and freeze immediately if not. **[official guidance (AU)]** — [Safe Food Qld](https://www.safefood.qld.gov.au/?p=18400); Foodwise (NSW Food Authority context): "up to 2-3 days" — [Foodwise](https://www.foodwise.com.au/?p=5740)
- **Refreezing thawed raw chicken (AU).** "You may re-freeze thawed chicken meat provided the chicken meat was defrosted in the fridge below 5 C and ... wasn't defrosting for longer than 24 hours at this temperature"; repeated freezing "may result in a deterioration in the eating quality but will not affect the safety"; never refreeze benchtop-thawed. FSIC research cited: 76% of Australians wrongly believe refreezing safely thawed raw chicken is unsafe. **[official guidance (AU, FSIC via Safe Food Qld)]** — [Safe Food Qld newsroom](https://www.safefood.qld.gov.au/newsroom/refreezing-chicken-meat/). CSIRO agrees (fridge at 5 C or below; some quality loss as "cells break down a little"; or cook then refreeze once steaming has stopped). **[official guidance (AU)]** — [CSIRO](https://research.csiro.au/foodsafety/five-food-safety-myths-busted)
- **Refreezing (USDA).** Fridge-thawed raw poultry can be refrozen without cooking (some quality loss); cold-water or microwave-thawed food must be cooked before refreezing. **[official guidance (US) via university handouts / search summary]** — [Texas A&M PDF](https://foodsafety.tamu.edu/files/2020/06/Understanding-Thawing_-3-Easy-Ways-to-Safely-Defrost-Frozen-Food.pdf); [FSIS Big Thaw](https://www.fsis.usda.gov/food-safety/safe-food-handling-and-preparation/food-safety-basics/big-thaw-safe-defrosting-methods) (403 when fetched). Singapore Food Agency says do not refreeze thawed food at all (not AU; reported for contrast).
- **Cooked chicken in the fridge (AU).** NSW Food Authority summer flyer (as extracted by search; fetch timed out): "Refrigerate leftover food within 2 hours of cooking. Eat or freeze within 3 days. Reheat leftovers to steaming hot (75 C)." **[official guidance (AU), via search extract]** — [NSW Food Authority flyer](https://www.foodauthority.nsw.gov.au/sites/default/files/2020-12/NSWFA_Summer_2020_Food_safety_FINAL.pdf). An SBS article quoting FSIC says ideally eat cooked food within a day, and a CSIRO food microbiologist puts the limit at four days. **[secondary, conflicting]** — [SBS](https://phoenix.chrisfo.edsqa01.aws.sbs.com.au/news/article/when-do-christmas-leftovers-become-a-food-poisoning-risk/odcfhvrj6). USDA/FoodSafety.gov: 3-4 days (secondary). **[official guidance (US), via secondary]** — [AOL summary](https://www.aol.com/articles/long-cooked-chicken-lasts-fridge-155900595.html)
- **Cooling and reheating.** CSIRO: refrigerate once steaming stops, "within about 30 minutes", in shallow containers; large containers can take nearly 24 h to reach 5 C. WA local government: refrigerate or freeze within 2 hours, reheat to at least 60 C steaming hot; the 2-hour/4-hour rule is cumulative time between 5 and 60 C. NSW flyer says 75 C. **[official guidance (AU)]** — [CSIRO](https://research.csiro.au/foodsafety/five-food-safety-myths-busted); [Shire of Kwinana](https://www.kwinana.wa.gov.au/community/health,-safety-and-security/food-safety/thawing,-cooking,-cooling-and-reheating-food-safel); [NSW Food Authority flyer](https://www.foodauthority.nsw.gov.au/sites/default/files/2020-12/NSWFA_Summer_2020_Food_safety_FINAL.pdf)
- **Leftover chicken dries quickly.** Foodwise: cooked chicken "can dry-out quickly when overcooked or stored in the fridge" (no days given). **[anecdotal / secondary]** — [Foodwise](https://www.foodwise.com.au/?p=5740)

### Inferences
- His process (fridge thaw, discard marinade, cook, eat or freeze within about 3 days) is aligned with Australian guidance. The main watch-points: fridge at or below 5 C, thaw time not exceeding 24 h before cooking (otherwise do not refreeze raw), and keep raw drips from touching ready-to-eat food (bottom shelf, leak-proof container; the "bottom shelf" tip is from a general consumer site, not an AU regulator).
- Marinated chicken that sits in the fridge thawing for up to 24 h plus the marinade is discarded after thaw, so there is no raw-marinade reuse hazard as long as it's binned, not used for basting without boiling.
- Whether the Australian Dept of Health WA / FSANZ gives a single national number for cooked chicken shelf life is unresolved: sources range from "within 3 days" (NSW) to "up to 4 days" (CSIRO via media). A conservative 3 days is the safest reading.
