CLAIM: Tendons/bones/soft tissue adapt 3-6x slower than the cardiovascular system; novices get hurt around weeks 4-6 when fitness outpaces tissue; full adaptation 2-3 to 6+ months. (SoLo大乾哥, 17:21)
VERDICT: partly/exaggerated · strength: moderate (direction true; "3-6x" and "weeks 4-6" unsourced)
ORIGIN: not found. No paper gives a "3-6x" ratio; the video cites none for it. The qualitative idea (tendon/bone remodel slower than aerobic capacity) is standard physiology, reviewed in Magnusson & Kjaer 2008 (PMID 17855761) and 2019 (PMID 29920664), both narrative reviews [V, abstracts only, no time numbers in abstracts].
WHAT IT REALLY SHOWED:
- Bohm 2015, Sports Med Open, PMID 27747846 [V]: SR+MA, 27 studies/37 interventions, 264 healthy adults 18-50, interventions >=8 wk (so no data on shorter), mostly resistance training. Tendon stiffness SMD 0.70 (0.51-0.88), Young's modulus 0.69 (0.36-1.03), CSA 0.24 (0.07-0.42). Stiffness depends on loading intensity (high > low); SMD "higher, not significantly" for >=12 wk. Shows tendons DO adapt within 8-12 wk of heavy loading; does not give a time course vs cardio.
- Wiesinger-type 2022 MA, Sports Med, PMID 35657492 [V]: 61 studies, n=763, stiffness SMD 0.74 (0.62-0.86), modulus 0.82, CSA 0.22; high-strain loading best. Same limits.
- 2026 MA, PMID 42924672 [V]: 57 reports/50 cohorts; stiffness +24.1% vs strength +16.1% (within-group, not control-adjusted). Tendon stiffness changed at similar or larger proportion than strength: cuts against "tendon is far slower".
- Bone: not found in my search (PubMed pull returned 0 for bone stress/time course). No bone numbers [U]; I did not verify any bone remodelling timeline.
- Time to injury in novices:
  * Nielsen 2013, Orthop J Sports Med, PMID 26535228 [V]: prospective cohort, n=930 novice runners, 1 yr, GPS, 254 injured over 155,318 km; time scale was cumulative km, not weeks. Predictors: BMI>30, age 45-65, previous non-running injury; previous running injury and experience not significant. No "week 4-6" peak reported in abstract.
  * Nielsen 2013, J Strength Cond Res, PMID 22990565 [V]: n=60 novices, 10 wk, 13 injured; injured runners' volume rose 86% (CI 12.9-159.9) in the week before onset vs other weeks. Injury follows a volume spike; no calendar-week clustering stated.
  * Nielsen 2014, JOSPT, PMID 25155475 [V]: n=874 novices, 202 injured; weekly distance progression >30% vs <10%: HR 1.59 (0.96-2.66) for distance-related injuries; overall no significant difference across 3 groups.
  * Buist 2010, Am J Sports Med, PMID 19966104 [V]: n=532 novices, 13-wk program, 21% injured; men: BMI HR 1.15/unit (1.05-1.26), prior injury HR 2.7 (1.36-5.55). No week-of-onset pattern in abstract.
  * Nielsen 2014, PLoS One, PMID 24923269 [V]: 254 injured of 933; commonest MTSS 15%, PFP 10%, Achilles tendinopathy 7%, plantar fasciitis 5%; median recovery 71 days. Not onset timing.
  * Full-text onset-by-week data exist in these cohorts possibly, but I did not read full texts [U]. So "weeks 4-6" is unverified; I found no paper stating it.
DISTORTION: Presents a precise ratio (3-6x) and a week window (4-6) as established; neither is in any paper I found. Cardio "2-4 weeks" vs tissue "2-3 to 6+ months" is a plausible lay summary but the tendon MAs show measurable stiffness gains by 8-12 wk of heavy loading. · flags: abstract-free numbers / selling something (running-club seller), marker→outcome (stiffness is a marker, not injury).
WIDER EVIDENCE (strongest first):
1. Ramskov 2018 (Run Clever), JOSPT, PMID 29895234 [V]: RCT, 447 recreational runners, 24 wk; schedule progressing high-intensity running (>=88% VO2max) vs schedule progressing volume. Intensity-specific injuries (Achilles, gastrocnemius, plantar fascia) risk difference -0.8% (-5.0 to 3.4) at 2 wk, -2.0% (-9.2 to 5.2) at 8 wk, -5.1% (-16.5 to 6.3) at 16 wk; volume injuries similarly null. Conclusion: no difference. 80/447 injured. Caveat: recreational runners (not strict novices), wide CIs, so not proof of no effect.
2. Nielsen 2012 SR, IJSPT, PMID 22389869 [V]: 31 studies incl. 3 RCTs; quality low (44%); conflicting results for volume, duration, intensity, frequency; cannot say which training errors cause injury.
3. Nielsen 2014 PMID 25155475 and 2013 PMID 22990565 above: weekly-progression signal weak (HR 1.59, CI includes 1) and exploratory.
4. Tendon MAs above (PMIDs 27747846, 35657492, 42924672): adaptation measurable by 8-12+ wk; magnitude depends on loading intensity.
No study found comparing interval vs easy running injury rates in true novices. Search did not cover this beyond Ramskov [U for others].
FIT TO DARREN: Plausible direction applies: BMI ~28 (Nielsen 2013: risk rises >30; Buist 2010 BMI HR 1.15/unit in men), never ran, 88 kg. Strength-trained (muscle/tendon loaded in gym, but not running's repetitive impact; Buist found prior non-axial-load sport raised risk, HR 2.05, 1.03-4.11) and no squat/deadlift. Practical: conservative ramp of 4x4 is reasonable, watch Achilles/calf/shin/knee at weeks 3-8. The 10% ramp already covered elsewhere. Run Clever suggests high-intensity progression is not clearly worse than volume progression, but that trial isn't a 90-95% HRmax novice trial. No liver/entecavir issue specific to this question.
