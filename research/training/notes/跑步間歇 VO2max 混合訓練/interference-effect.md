# Interference effect: how much does adding running / HIIT blunt muscle and strength?

Research worker note, 2026-11-22. English working note (not a 繁中 report).
Evidence tags: [V] = I read the PubMed abstract this session (via NCBI E-utilities); full texts were NOT read, so subgroup details beyond the abstract are unverified. [U] = not read. Everything below is [V] unless marked.
SMD = standardised mean difference (negative = concurrent training (CT) worse than resistance training (RT) alone).

## 1. Meta-analyses (effect sizes)

| Study | Design / n | Key numbers |
|---|---|---|
| Wilson 2012, J Strength Cond Res, PMID 22002517 | MA, 21 studies, 422 effect sizes (ES) | Hypertrophy ES: RT 1.23, CT 0.85, endurance 0.27. Strength ES: RT 1.76, CT 1.44. Power ES: RT 0.91, CT 0.55 (all three groups differ). Running (not cycling) + RT gave significant losses in hypertrophy AND strength. Negative correlation with endurance frequency (r -0.26 to -0.35) and duration (r -0.29 to -0.75). Old (mostly pre-2011 studies), ES are uncontrolled-mix, so biased; VO2max not reported in abstract. |
| Murlasits 2018, J Sports Sci, PMID 28783467 | MA, same-session order, strength-then-endurance (SE) vs endurance-then-strength (ES) | Lower-body 1RM +3.96 kg (95% CI 0.81-7.10) favouring strength first. VO2max difference 0.39 ml/kg/min (CI -1.03 to 1.81), no order effect. |
| Eddens 2018, Sports Med, PMID 28917030 | MA, 10 studies, >=5 wk | Resistance-first: lower-body dynamic strength +6.91% (CI 1.96-11.87, p=0.006) vs endurance-first. No order effect on lower-body hypertrophy (+1.15%, CI -1.56 to 3.87), static strength, VO2max, body fat. |
| Sabag 2018, J Sports Sci, PMID 29658408 | MA, HIIT+RT vs RT | Hypertrophy and upper-body strength: no difference. Lower-body strength ES -0.248 (p=0.049). Cycling HIIT ES -0.377 (p=0.074), running HIIT ES -0.176 (p=0.261); both not significant alone. Longer inter-modal rest looked better. |
| Petre 2021, Sports Med, PMID 33751469 | MA, 27 studies, lower-body 1RM (leg press / squat) | Trained: ES -0.35 (p<0.01); moderately trained: -0.20 (p=0.08); untrained: +0.03 (ns). In trained, same session ES -0.66 (p<0.01) vs different sessions -0.10 (p=0.55). |
| Schumann 2022, Sports Med, PMID 34757594 | MA, 43 studies, vs identical RT without aerobic | Max strength SMD -0.06 (CI -0.20 to 0.09, ns). Explosive strength -0.28 (CI -0.48 to -0.08, p=0.007). Whole-muscle hypertrophy -0.01 (CI -0.16 to 0.18, ns). Explosive loss larger same-session (p=0.043) than when separated >=3 h (ns). No moderator effect for running vs cycling, frequency (<5 vs >5/wk), training status, age. |
| Lundberg 2022, Sports Med, PMID 35476184 | MA, 15 studies, muscle FIBRE size | Overall fibre hypertrophy SMD -0.23 (CI -0.46 to -0.00, p=0.050). Type I -0.34 (ns), type II -0.13 (ns). Type I with running -0.81 (CI -1.26 to -0.36), cycling not. Small subgroup, preliminary. |
| Monserda-Vilaro 2023, J Strength Cond Res, PMID 36508686 | MA, 25 studies | Whole-muscle: no difference for any CT type (SMD <0.03). Fibre size: RT > CT when HIIT included (SMD >0.33 HIIT alone, >0.27 HIIT+continuous), not with continuous only (<0.16). |
| Huiberts 2024, Sports Med, PMID 37847373 | MA, 59 studies, n=1346, ages 18-50 | Lower-body strength: males SMD -0.43 (CI -0.64 to -0.22), females +0.08 (group difference p=0.03). No sex difference for upper-body strength, power, VO2max. Hypertrophy data insufficient. Untrained (not trained / athletes) had lower VO2max gains with CT (p=0.04). |
| Chen 2024, J Exerc Sci Fit, PMID 38187085 | Network MA, 40 studies, n=841 | Lower-limb max strength: all CT types below RT alone, but HIIT running smallest gap (SMD -0.08, CI -0.25 to 0.08). Muscle cross-section: HIIT running +0.15 (CI -0.46 to 0.76), moderate continuous cycling +0.07 (all CIs span 0). Wide CIs; network MA, low precision. |
| Ferraro-Farro 2026, Int J Sports Med, PMID 41734815 | MA of RCTs, sprint-interval CT, 9 studies, n=177 | vs RT alone: lower-body strength SMD 0.01, upper-body -0.06, jump 0.11 (all ns). VO2max SMD +0.78 (p=0.001) in favour of CT. SIT (all-out sprints) not the same as 4 min intervals. Small. |
| Held 2026, Sports Med, PMID 41762427 | Umbrella review, 17 MAs, 144 studies, n=1492 | CT vs RT: strength, power, hypertrophy comparable; CT vs RT aerobic capacity SMD +0.77 (p=0.02). CT vs endurance alone: aerobic comparable. Sequence: no significant effect, trend that RT-first helps strength (SMD 1.69) and hypertrophy (0.83, p=0.36). Overlap of primary studies between MAs is a limitation. |

Reading across: whole-muscle hypertrophy is essentially unaffected (Schumann -0.01; Monserda <0.03; Held comparable). Max strength is unaffected on average (-0.06) but lower body in trained men is the weak spot (Petre -0.35; Huiberts male -0.43; Sabag -0.25). Explosive strength takes the clearest hit (-0.28). VO2max: CT keeps VO2max gains vs endurance alone (Held), and adding SIT to RT raises VO2max a lot vs RT only (+0.78).
Gap: I found no meta-analysis giving VO2max effect sizes for "RT lifters who add running intervals" in absolute ml/kg/min; Huiberts and Held give SMD only.

## 2. Moderators

- **Running vs cycling**: Wilson 2012 (running worse) and Lundberg 2022 (type I fibres, running -0.81) point to running; Schumann 2022 found NO modality difference; Sabag found cycling HIIT nominally worse than running HIIT (-0.377 vs -0.176, both ns). Conflicting, so no reliable conclusion. Rowing: no data found.
- **HIIT vs moderate continuous**: Chen 2024 and Sabag suggest HIIT running is not worse; Monserda 2023 says fibre hypertrophy lower when HIIT is included but whole muscle unaffected. Conflicting; effect sizes small either way.
- **Frequency/volume**: Wilson 2012 correlations negative (more frequent/longer = worse); Schumann 2022 found no difference >5 vs <5 sessions/wk. Wilson is older and correlational.
- **Training status**: Only Petre 2021 supports "trained is hit harder" for lower-body 1RM; Schumann 2022 and Huiberts 2024 found no status moderator for strength. Darren (late novice / early intermediate) sits in the "moderately trained" bin: ES -0.20, p=0.08, borderline.
- **Lower vs upper body**: Sabag: only lower-body strength affected, upper-body and hypertrophy fine. Huiberts: upper-body strength unaffected. Running mostly loads the legs, so interference is local.
- **Sex**: Huiberts 2024 found lower-body strength blunted in males, not females (n females small).

## 3. Sequencing and timing

- **Same-session order**: Strength first is better for lower-body dynamic strength (Eddens +6.9%; Murlasits +3.96 kg) with no effect on hypertrophy or VO2max. Held 2026 umbrella: trend only.
- **Separation**: Schumann 2022: explosive strength loss only when same session; >=3 h gap ns. Petre 2021: trained men, same session -0.66 vs different sessions -0.10. Robineau 2016 (J Strength Cond Res, PMID 25546450, RCT, n=58 amateur rugby players, 7 wk, 2 strength + 2 aerobic sessions/wk, strength always first, magnitude-based inference, small groups): 0 h gap worst for strength gains and VO2peak, 6 h worse than 24 h on some measures, 24 h best (VO2peak change higher in 24 h than 0 h and 6 h). Weak: small groups, athletes, magnitude-based inference.
- **Next-day residual fatigue**: I found NO study of "intervals the day before a leg session" and its effect on leg-session performance or training adaptations. Indirect evidence only: Andrade 2023 (Res Q Exerc Sport, PMID 35302926, n=16 young men, crossover): 8 x 40 s at 100% vVO2max run immediately BEFORE 4 sets to failure at 80% 1RM cut half-squat reps more than bench reps (p=0.018 between exercises), i.e. same-session. Lloria-Varella 2023 (Eur J Appl Physiol, PMID 37285051, n=25, 8 x 15 s all-out cycle sprints): isometric MVC, force and power unchanged at 24 h and 48 h (full recovery by 24 h). That is cycling sprints, not 4-min running intervals, which are longer and have eccentric load. Inference only: fatigue appears gone by next day for sprint work; unproven for 4 x 4 running.

## 4. Molecular argument (brief)

AMPK activation by endurance exercise was proposed to inhibit mTORC1. Human data do not support this: Apro 2015 (Am J Physiol Endocrinol Metab, PMID 25605643, n=8 trained men, interval cycling then resistance exercise): AMPK up ~90%, S6K1 activity and muscle protein synthesis response not inhibited; MuRF1 (breakdown marker) increased. Lundberg 2014 (J Appl Physiol, PMID 24408998, n=10 men, unilateral, 5 wk): quadriceps size grew MORE in the cycling + RT leg (6% vs 3%), but concentric torque gain was smaller. Fyfe 2014 review (PMID 24728927) and Murach 2016 review (PMID 26932769) conclude molecular interference is not demonstrated in humans. In practice: signalling does not predict chronic outcome; the more plausible mechanisms are fatigue, glycogen depletion and reduced lifting quality, which fits the strength/power-specific losses above. Small acute studies, not outcomes.

## 5. Energy deficit / maintenance

- Maintenance: no study found testing interference specifically at maintenance kcal. The meta-analyses mostly include participants in unspecified or surplus energy balance. Extrapolation: no reason in the data for it to be different, but untested.
- Deficit: Longland 2016 (Am J Clin Nutr, PMID 26817506, RCT, n=40 young men, 4 wk, ~40% deficit, RT + HIIT 6 d/wk): lean mass +1.2 kg with 2.4 g/kg protein vs +0.1 kg with 1.2 g/kg; fat -4.8 vs -3.5 kg. No RT-only control arm, so it says nothing about interference; it shows RT + HIIT can coexist with lean-mass gain in a deficit when protein is high. 4 weeks, young men, 4-compartment measured.
- Body fat meta-analysis (Lafontant 2025, J Int Soc Sports Nutr, PMID 40405489, 36 studies, n=1564): CT lost 1.09 kg more fat than RT alone in >=10-wk studies; workload-matched, no difference. Not about interference or deficit directly.
- Conclusion: no direct evidence for concurrent-training interference under a deficit. Say so in the 2027 cut plan.

## 6. Practical placement (evidence only)

His week: Tue legs, Thu shoulders/arms, Sat back, Sun chest, treadmill walking most days.
- Evidence favours separating run from lifting sessions of the same limbs (Petre; Schumann; Robineau). Same-session, lift first (Eddens, Murlasits).
- The interference is lower-body-specific (Sabag; Huiberts), and the run loads the legs. So intervals are best on a non-leg-lifting day. Thu (shoulders/arms), Sat (back) or Sun (chest) do not interfere with upper-body lifting per the lower/upper split in Sabag and Huiberts.
- The day before Tue legs is Mon; a Mon interval session has no direct evidence either way. Lloria-Varella suggests sprint-type work recovers in 24 h. Safest: do the run on Sun or Thu with a gap of >=24 h before Tue legs; Sat/Sun for the second session. Do not run immediately before leg day, and avoid running intervals on the same day as legs; if forced, lifting first and >=3-6 h gap (Schumann; Robineau).
- If intervals and a lift share a day, put them on an upper-body day, run AFTER lifting (shoulders/arms, 50-min weekday cap makes this tight).
- Frequency: 1-2 interval sessions/wk. Wilson's dose-response is correlational; no study defines a safe threshold.
- Sprint intervals preserved strength in Ferraro-Farro 2026 (small, n=177), but not directly applicable to 4-min intervals.

## Bottom line for Darren

1. Average cost of adding running to lifting: whole-muscle growth about zero (SMD -0.01), max strength about zero (-0.06), explosive strength small loss (-0.28). Held 2026 umbrella agrees. CT also lifts VO2max (SMD +0.77 vs RT alone).
2. Where it does hurt: lower-body 1RM in trained/moderately trained men (-0.20 to -0.43) and mostly when run and lifts share a session. You are the profile at risk (male, moderately trained), but your leg work is machine/leg-press based, which is exactly the lift measured in Petre.
3. Place intervals on a non-leg day, not Tue and ideally not Mon; Thu, Sat or Sun fit; keep a gap of >=24 h from leg day (>=3-6 h if same day, lifts first).
4. Molecular "AMPK blocks mTOR" fear is not borne out in humans; ignore it.
5. No data exist for: next-day leg performance after 4-min running intervals, interference at maintenance kcal, interference in a deficit, rowing. Treat the plan as an experiment: track leg-press 1RM/e1RM on Tue; if it falls or leg-day volume drops for 2+ weeks, cut interval frequency to 1/wk.
6. Weak evidence flags: Wilson 2012 (old, correlational), Robineau 2016 (n=58 across 5 groups, magnitude-based inference), Lundberg 2014 and Apro 2015 (n=10, n=8), Andrade 2023 (n=16), Lundberg 2022 running subgroup (few studies). All abstract-level reads.
