# Minimal lifting dose, mobility and "useful" strength (for adding running intervals)

Compiled 2026-11-22. [V] = I read the PubMed abstract this session (full text NOT read for any paper; numbers come from abstracts). [U] = not read. Not found = searched, nothing located.

## A. Minimum effective dose to maintain / still build

| Source | Design, n | Finding (number) | Caveat |
|---|---|---|---|
| Spiering 2021, J Strength Cond Res, PMID 33629972 [V] | Narrative review (not systematic) | Strength and muscle size (young adults) maintained up to 32 wk with as little as 1 session/wk and 1 set/exercise IF relative load stays high. Older adults need 2 sessions/wk and 2-3 sets/exercise. "Intensity is the key variable." Endurance maintained 15 wk with 2 sessions/wk or 33-66% less volume if intensity kept. | Narrative; mostly untrained/short studies. Military authors. |
| Bickel 2011, Med Sci Sports Exerc, PMID 21131862 [V] | RCT, n=70 (young 20-35, old 60-75); 16 wk RT 3x/wk, then 32 wk at 1/3, 1/9 dose or detraining | Young: both 1/3 and 1/9 dose preserved hypertrophy; 1/3 dose gave extra fibre hypertrophy. Strength largely retained even when fully detrained (slight drop only at end). Old: neither dose preserved fibre size. | Previously untrained at start, so "retaining" is easier than for trained lifters. The 1/9 dose = about 1 session per 3 wk-equivalent volume (exact protocol not in abstract). |
| Tavares 2017, Eur J Sport Sci, PMID 28316261 [V] | RCT, n=33 untrained men; 8 wk ST then 8 wk reduced | Volume cut 50-57%, frequency 1 or 2x/wk: 1RM and quad CSA unchanged. Full cessation: 1RM -22.6%, CSA -5.4%. Frequency made no difference when volume-load equal. | Untrained, lower-body only, short. |
| Schoenfeld 2019, MSSE, PMID 30153194 [V] | RCT, n=34 resistance-trained men, 8 wk, 3x/wk, 1 vs 3 vs 5 sets/exercise/session | Strength (squat, bench 1RM) rose in all groups, no difference (so ~1 set x 3 sessions = about 13 min/session was enough to gain strength). Muscle size: dose-response, more sets gave more growth (elbow flexors, thigh). | 8 wk; "trained" = about 4 yr but only ~1RM-based; men only. |
| Androulakis-Korakakis 2021, Front Sports Act Living, PMID 34527944 [V] | Mixed methods; 2 small training studies, n=25 powerlifters, plus surveys/interviews | Suggests ~3-6 working sets of 1-5 reps/wk per lift, 1-3 sessions/wk, >80% 1RM, RPE 7.5-9.5, to gain strength for 6-12 wk. | WEAK: expert/survey-derived, tiny RCTs, powerlifters, bench/squat/deadlift only. Strength only, not muscle. |
| Iversen 2021, Sports Med, PMID 34125411 [V] | Narrative review | Min. about 4 sets/muscle/wk at 6-15RM (15-40 reps if to failure); volume matters more than frequency; one leg press, one pull, one push exercise as the core. Stretching only if flexibility is the goal. | Narrative; "minimum to gain" in time-poor people, not trained lifters. |
| Ralston 2017, Sports Med, PMID 28755103 [V] | Meta-analysis, 9 studies, 61 groups | Strength: low weekly sets ES 0.82 vs high 1.01 (diff 0.18, 95% CI 0.06-0.30). Modest dose-response. | Only 9 studies; defines low/med/high per-exercise; mostly untrained/novice. |
| Schoenfeld 2017, J Sports Sci, PMID 27433992 [V] | Meta-regression, 15 studies, 34 groups | Each extra weekly set = +0.37% muscle gain; higher vs lower volume +3.9% gain. Categories <5, 5-9, 10+ sets/muscle: trend only (p=0.074). | Few studies, mixed training status. |
| Schoenfeld 2019, J Sports Sci, PMID 30558493 [V] | Meta-analysis, 25 studies | Frequency does not matter for hypertrophy when volume is equated. | Spreading volume is a convenience choice. |
| Pelland 2026, Sports Med, PMID 41343037 [V] | Bayesian meta-regression, 67 studies, n=2058 (79% male, mean age 25) | More volume gives more hypertrophy and strength, diminishing returns; strength saturates much sooner. Frequency: negligible effect on hypertrophy, but positive (diminishing) effect on strength. | Abstract gave no set thresholds, so I cannot quote numbers. Full text not read. Mean age 25. |
| Swinton 2024, Sports Med, PMID 38652410 [V] | Bayesian meta-analysis, 295 studies, n=6710 | Strength gains depend mostly on load intensity (heaviest loads best for max strength) and outcome domain; vertical jump best near 30% 1RM, power 40-70% 1RM. | Mostly short studies, many untrained. |
| ACSM Position Stand, Currier 2026, MSSE, PMID 41843416 [V] | Overview of 137 systematic reviews, over 30,000 participants | Strength: loads >=80% 1RM, full ROM, 2-3 sets, at start of session, >=2 sessions/wk. Hypertrophy: >=10 sets/wk and eccentric overload. Power: 30-70% 1RM, <=24 reps/set, Olympic lifts or fast-concentric training. Training to failure, equipment, periodisation: no consistent effect. | Review of reviews; mostly healthy, young to middle-aged, short trials. |

Detraining data: only Bickel, Tavares and Spiering above. No long-term data found on 2-yr trained lifters (a gap).

### What I can conclude for maintenance (my inference from the above)
- Maintaining strength: about 1 session/muscle-or-lift/wk, 1-3 hard sets per exercise (about 3-6 hard sets/wk per movement pattern), loads >=~80% 1RM or hard sets near failure. Supported by Spiering, Bickel (young), Tavares, Schoenfeld 2019.
- Maintaining muscle size: about 1/3 of previous volume looked sufficient in young adults (Bickel; Tavares used about 50%). Evidence is in untrained-then-trained novices; for Darren (about 2 yr) probably OK but uncertain.
- Growing slowly: needs more, roughly >=10 hard sets/muscle/wk (ACSM; Schoenfeld 2017 trend). Cutting to a maintenance dose should be expected to stall growth, not erase it.
- Intensity must stay high (heavy-ish, hard sets). Cutting sets is safe; cutting effort/load is what the reviews warn against.

### Practical (inference, not an RCT result)
- Frequency does not matter if weekly volume is equal (Schoenfeld 2019; Tavares). So 2 full-body sessions/wk can replace the 4-day split if each muscle still gets about 3-6 hard sets (maintain) or 8-10 (slow growth).
- Darren's present 10-16 sets/muscle/wk could be reduced by roughly a third (about 6-10) and still be within the "maintain, slow gain" band by these sources. Dropping from 4 to 2-3 lifting days is compatible with the evidence; no study tested this exact swap alongside running.
- Concurrent running: whole-muscle hypertrophy is generally not hurt, but Lundberg 2022 (Sports Med, PMID 35476184 [V], meta of 15 studies) found small fibre-hypertrophy interference, SMD -0.23 (CI -0.46 to 0.00), larger for running than cycling (SMD -0.81 for type I fibres). Fibre-level outcome, small studies.

## B. Stiffness, mobility, "useful" strength

### Resistance training and range of motion (ROM)
- Alizadeh 2023, Sports Med, PMID 36622555 [V]: meta-analysis, 55 studies. RT increases ROM (ES 0.73). RT vs stretching: no difference (ES 0.08). RT + stretching vs stretching alone: no difference (ES -0.001). Body-mass-only RT did not significantly improve ROM. Larger gains in sedentary people (ES 1.04) than active (0.43). Conclusion: stretching may not be needed when training with external loads. Used full-ROM exercises.
- Afonso 2021, Healthcare (Basel; NOT Sports Med), PMID 33917036 [V]: 11 RCTs, n=452. RT vs stretching on ROM: ES -0.22 (CI -0.55 to 0.12), no difference. Highly heterogeneous.
- Morton 2011, J Strength Cond Res, PMID 21969080 [V]: RCT, n=25 (+12 non-randomised controls), untrained, 5 wk. Full-ROM RT = static stretching for hamstring/hip flexibility. WEAK: small, preliminary, convenience control.
- Implication: if his lifts are done through full ROM, they are not making him less mobile. Truncated reps or only machines with short ROM are not tested here.

### Stretching / mobility work
- Konrad 2024, Sports Med, PMID 38760635 [V]: 85 studies, 204 ES. Chronic static stretching raises ROM (ES about 1.0 vs control); foam rolling training also (ES 0.73); similar, but static stretching better when intervention is <=4 wk.
- Konrad 2025, Sports Med Open, PMID 41269471 [V]: 13 studies, n=556. ROM gains from stretching are large (ES 0.93) and partly lost after 2-6 wk of detraining (ES -0.41); confidence in maintenance low; more weekly frequency helped retention.
- Behm 2016, Appl Physiol Nutr Metab, PMID 26642915 [V]: systematic review. Acute static stretching: -3.7% performance (-4.6% if >=60 s per muscle, -1.1% if <60 s); dynamic stretching +1.3%. ROM improvements last <30 min. Stretching within a warm-up with dynamic activity afterwards recommended. No clear effect on injury from static/PNF.
- Lauersen 2014, Br J Sports Med, PMID 24100287 [V]: 25 RCTs, n=26,610. Injury RR: stretching 0.96 (0.85-1.10), no effect; strength training 0.32 (0.21-0.48), multi-component 0.66. Flag: sports injuries in athletes/military, heterogeneous; RR for strength training seems implausibly large.
- Warneke 2024, Sports Med Open, PMID 38637473 [V]: 42 studies, n=1318. Chronic static stretching: strength d=0.30, hypertrophy d=0.20; larger with longer duration/frequency. Flag: small effects; many studies are calf stretching with devices, up to 1 h/day, which is not realistic as a routine. Warneke 2022 Front Physiol PMID 36685189 [V]: n=55 RCT-type study, 6 wk of 1 h/day calf stretch: MSt +6-15%, ROM +6-21%, thickness +4-14%.
- Wiewelhove 2019, Front Physiol, PMID 31024339 [V]: 21 studies. Pre-exercise foam rolling: flexibility +4.0% (g=0.34), sprint +0.7%, strength/jump negligible. Post-rolling: muscle pain perception -6.0% (g=0.47), strength recovery g=0.21. "Effects rather minor." (Acute effects only.)
- Iversen 2021 (above): prioritise stretching only if flexibility is the goal; use exercise-specific warm-up.

### Does strength transfer to athletic / "functional" performance?
- Seitz 2014, Sports Med, PMID 25059334 [V]: meta, 15 studies, n=510, back-squat strength gain vs sprint change r=-0.77. Sprint improved 3.1% on average. Mostly athletes, squat only. Correlation of study-level effects, not individual causation.
- Llanos-Lagos 2024, Sports Med, PMID 38627351 [V]: meta in runners (n about 894). High-load strength training improved running performance (ES -0.47), combined methods ES -1.04; plyometric alone not significant. No improvement in VO2max, vVO2max, sprint. GRADE very low to moderate.
- ACSM 2026 (above): power training and Olympic-style lifting improved physical function; loaded 30-70% 1RM with fast concentric.
- Swinton 2024 (above): jump best at light loads, max strength best with heavy loads, so power needs separate work.
- Hyrox: Brandt 2025, Front Physiol, PMID 40230601 [V]: n=11 recreational athletes, simulated race. Runs were 51 of 86.5 min. Faster time correlated with higher VO2max (p=0.01), more endurance training volume (p=0.04), lower body fat (p=0.03); no strength variable listed as a determinant; authors say low-to-moderate maximal strength and mobility demands. Tiny n, correlational, Spearman.
- Loaded carries: not found. Only strongman biomechanics papers (McGill 2009, PMID 19528856 [U]; Hindle 2019, PMID 31820223 [U]); no trial of carry training effects.
- "Functional training" vs traditional RT in healthy adults: search returned no relevant meta-analysis or RCT. Not found; no claim made.

### What "feeling stiff" might reflect
- No study located that answers this for a healthy lifter. Candidate explanations are inference only: DOMS from high weekly volume/eccentrics, low general activity, prolonged sitting, short-ROM lifting, or low stretch tolerance (Behm 2016 attributes acute ROM changes to reduced stiffness or improved stretch tolerance, but that is about stretching sessions). A DOMS review exists (Di Lorenzo 2025, PMID 41283559 [U], not read). Ask him whether stiffness is morning vs post-session, and measure ROM (e.g. sit-and-reach, hip flexion) rather than relying on feel.

## Bottom line for Darren
1. Cutting volume is supported if effort and load stay high. Evidence (Spiering, Bickel, Tavares, Schoenfeld 2019) says 1/3 to 1/2 of current volume, 1-2 sessions per muscle/wk, heavy hard sets, maintains strength and muscle in young adults. Muscle growth, though, scales with volume (Schoenfeld 2017, ACSM >=10 sets/wk), so expect plateau, not loss.
2. A reasonable cut given running (my inference): 3 lifting days (or 2 full-body + 1 upper/accessory), about 6-10 hard sets/muscle/wk, reps 5-12 close to failure, keep leg press/rows/presses heavy. 2 days works for maintenance only (about 3-6 sets/muscle/wk).
3. Frequency matters little; volume and intensity do. Spreading sets is a scheduling choice.
4. Running will not erase muscle overall, but a small fibre-level interference (Lundberg 2022) means placing hard intervals away from leg day is sensible (this separation advice is my inference, not tested).
5. Stiffness: full-ROM lifting improves ROM as well as stretching does (Alizadeh 2023; Afonso 2021). A separate stretching routine adds little; short post-session stretches or foam rolling are optional and the benefits are small (Wiewelhove: flexibility +4%, pain -6%). Static stretching does not reduce injury (Lauersen RR 0.96); strength training does (RR 0.32, caveats above). Do not do long static stretches right before heavy sets (Behm: -4.6% when >=60 s).
6. Making strength "useful": strength transfers to sprinting (Seitz r=-0.77) and to running performance in runners (Llanos-Lagos), but not VO2max. Power work (jumps, fast light lifts; ACSM 30-70% 1RM; Swinton) is a separate stimulus. For Hyrox-style goals, the one small study says aerobic capacity predicts finish time (Brandt 2025), so the added running is the right priority. No evidence found for loaded carries or "functional training" beating regular RT.
7. Gaps: no trial on trained lifters adding intervals while reducing volume; Pelland 2026 thresholds not read; Androulakis-Korakakis is weak; stiffness cause unsourced.
