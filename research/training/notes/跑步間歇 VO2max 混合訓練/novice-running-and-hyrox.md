# Novice running for a heavier lifter, and HYROX: evidence notes (2026-11-22)

Tags: [V] = I read the PubMed abstract this session (not full text). [U] = not read / not found. No full texts were read.
All numbers below are from abstracts. Quality flags are my own judgement from the abstract.

## A. Starting to run as a heavier novice

### A1. Injury incidence
- Videbaek 2015, Sports Med, PMID 25951917 [V]. Systematic review + meta-analysis, 13 prospective cohorts/RCTs. Running-related injury (RRI) per 1000 h: novice runners 17.8 (95% CI 16.7-19.1), recreational runners 7.7 (6.9-8.7); range 2.5 to 33.0. Caveat: injury definitions differ across studies. Takeaway: novices are about 2x as injury-prone per hour as experienced runners.
- Nielsen 2013, Orthop J Sports Med, PMID 26535228 [V]. Prospective cohort, n=930 Danish novices, 1 year, GPS-measured km, neutral shoe; 254 injured (injury = complaint restricting running >=1 week). Predictors at 500 km: Type B behaviour cIRD 11.9% (p=.04); age 45-65 +14.7% (ns); previous non-running injury +11.1% (borderline). BMI: only "borderline significant" across 4 BMI groups (abstract truncated). Weak-ish: observational, wide CIs.
- Nielsen 2014, Br J Sports Med, PMID 23766439 [V]. Same cohort (n=927): foot pronation does not predict injury in a neutral shoe. Relevant only as "do not worry about arches".
- Johnston 2018, J Sci Med Sport, PMID 29559317 [V]. Systematic review, 12 endurance studies. Higher injury/pain risk: high weekly/monthly distance (medium effect), <2 sessions/week (medium), previous injury (medium), age >45 (small), older shoes (small). No study used ACWR. Low-moderate quality (the abstract notes only CASP appraisal).

### A2. Body mass, and how much to start with (most relevant to Darren)
- Nielsen 2014, Int J Sports Phys Ther, PMID 24944852 [V]. Prospective cohort, n=749 novices, 3 weeks, 56 injured. Among BMI>30, running 3-6 km in week 1 had cumulative risk difference +14.3% (95% CI 3.3-25.3) and >6 km +16.2% (4.4-28.0) vs low BMI/low distance. Authors suggest BMI>30 start below 3 km in week 1. Exploratory, short, volunteers. NOTE: Darren is BMI ~28 (overweight, not >30), so the threshold is a conservative guide, not a measured cut-off for him.
- Bertelsen 2018, JOSPT, PMID 29932876 [V]. Cohort, n=914 novices (405 normal, 341 overweight, 168 obese), GPS, week 1. Overweight/obese runners chose the SAME distance/duration as normal-weight but ran slower (overweight -0.5 km/h, obese -1.7 km/h). Authors: heavier runners take a higher load per stride, and self-chosen dose does not compensate.
- Bertelsen 2018, Int J Sports Phys Ther, PMID 30534460 [V]. RCT, n=56 obese (BMI 30-35) novices, 4 weeks: 3 km/week (3x1 km) vs 6 km/week (3x2 km), both +10%/week. Intention-to-treat risk difference -16.3% (95% CI -43.8 to 11.3, p=.25: NOT significant). Per-protocol -31.2% (-57.0 to -5.2, p=.02) but 5 over-ran and 15 uploaded nothing. Small, underpowered; treat as suggestive only. Population BMI 30-35, not Darren's.

### A3. The "10 % rule" and spikes
- Buist 2008, Am J Sports Med, PMID 17940147 [V]. RCT GRONORUN, n=532 novices for a 4-mile race: graded 13-week plan based on the 10% rule vs standard 8-week plan. RRI 20.8% vs 20.3% (p=.90). Conclusion: the 10%-rule plan did NOT reduce injuries. (Design paper: Buist 2007, PMID 17331264.)
- Schuster Brandt Frandsen (Nielsen group) 2025, Br J Sports Med, PMID 40623829 [V]. This is the Garmin-RUNSAFE result (protocol: Nielsen 2019, BMJ Open, PMID 31494626 [V]). Cohort n=5205 adult runners (mean age 45.8, 22% female), 18 months, 588,071 sessions, 35% injured (overuse, self-reported). Exposure: single-session distance relative to longest run in previous 30 days. Hazard rate ratios vs <=10% increase: small spike (>10-30%) 1.64 (1.31-2.05); moderate (>30-100%) 1.52 (1.16-2.00); large (>100%) 2.28 (1.50-3.48). Weekly ACWR showed an inverse relationship; week-to-week ratio no relationship. Conclusion: a single run longer than 10% over your longest run in the last 30 days raised injury rate. Quality: large, objective exposure, but observational, experienced runners (not novices), self-reported injury, 22% women. Note the effect did not rise in a clean gradient (small spike 1.64 vs moderate 1.52).
- So: weekly-total "10% rule" has no RCT support (Buist); a SINGLE-SESSION rule (do not exceed the longest recent run by >10%) has some cohort support. Fredette 2022 (ACWR review): I could not locate it by PubMed search [U]; do not cite. Damsted 2018: not found as a separate paper [U] (search returned only the 2019 protocol).

### A4. Treadmill, walk-run, Couch-to-5K
- Keast 2022, Sports Med, PMID 35708887 [V]. Systematic review + meta-analysis, 36 studies on tibial load. Treadmill vs overground: tibial loading lower, SMD -0.83 (-1.53 to -0.12). Higher speed raises tibial load (SMD 1.03). This is acute biomechanical loading, not injury incidence. Evidence on treadmill vs outdoor injury rates: none found.
- Walk-run / Couch-to-5K: I found no peer-reviewed injury RCT by PubMed search [U]. No claim made. (GRONORUN 2 preconditioning RCT, Bredeweg 2010, PMID 20809930, is protocol only in the abstract I read; result [U].)

### A5. Strength training
- Lauersen 2014, Br J Sports Med, PMID 24100287 [V]. Meta-analysis of 25 RCTs, n=26,610, 3464 injuries, general sports (NOT running-specific). Strength training RR 0.315 (0.207-0.480); overuse injuries RR 0.527 (0.373-0.746); stretching RR 0.963 (ns). Heterogeneous; many trials were team sports; effect size is implausibly large, so I would discount it. No running-novice trial of strength-for-injury was found.
- Balsalobre-Fernandez 2016, J Strength Cond Res, PMID 26694507 [V]. Meta-analysis, 5 controlled trials, n=93 highly trained runners (VO2max >60). Strength training 8-12 wk, 2-3x/wk improved running economy, SMD -1.42 (-2.23 to -0.60). Population is elite; small; applicability to a novice is unknown.
- Blagrove 2018, Sports Med, PMID 29249083 [V]. Systematic review, 24 studies, PEDro 4-6 (modest quality), runners with >=6 months experience. Running economy improved ~2-8% vs control in most studies; 1.5-10 km time-trial tended to improve; VO2max generally not changed (abstract: listed among other parameters, partly truncated).
- Implication for Darren: his lifting is likely not harmful for running economy; the evidence is in trained runners, not novices.

## B. HYROX

Total: the literature is new (2025-2026), small, mostly descriptive. PubMed "hyrox[tiab]" returned only about 10 records.

- Brandt 2025, Front Physiol, PMID 40230601 [V]. Simulated Individual Open race, n=11 recreational athletes (27% women), median 18 months experience. Finish 86.5 (IQR 14.5) min; runs 51.2 min vs stations 32.8 min (p=.003), i.e. ~60% of time is running. HR 79.5% of max "very hard" zone. Max lactate stations 8.5 vs runs 7.7 mmol/L; peak HR/lactate/RPE at the last station (wall balls). Heaviest stations were fastest (sled push 128 s, sled pull 155 s). Faster finish correlated (Spearman) with higher VO2max (p=.01), more endurance training volume (p=.04), lower body fat (p=.03). Authors say HYROX has "moderate to low" maximal-strength demand. n=11, correlational, simulated race: low quality.
- Rappelt 2026, Front Physiol, PMID 42524284 [V]. Public results of PRO and ELITE races, females n=11,842, males n=27,854, 7 seasons. Running ~50% of total race time; the running improvement was the main driver of the elite times improving. Strength-determined stations showed larger time penalties among slower athletes. Selected-population (PRO/ELITE), not recreational.
- Gutierrez-Hellin 2026, Int J Sports Physiol Perform, PMID 42697540 [V]. n=24 male PRO athletes, finish 69:58; lab VO2max 51.6 mL/kg/min, VT2 16.4 km/h. Race running pace fell from 16.1 km/h (run 1) to 13.6 km/h (run 8), mean ~76% of vVO2max. Higher sustained relative running intensity linked to shorter total running time. Good design for its size, but elite men only.
- Fernandez-Navarrete 2026, J Sports Med Phys Fitness, PMID 42189569 [V]: percentile benchmarks from 186,411 race results (descriptive; useful for target-setting; mean/percentiles not read). Fernandez-Navarrete 2026, Res Q Exerc Sport, PMID 41380137 [V]: participation trends, 278,063 athletes. Both descriptive.
- Chittenden 2026, Front Sports Act Living, PMID 42780891 [V]. Prospective cohort, n=89 HYROX athletes (56 female), 12 weeks, OSTRC-H2 weekly, response 62%. Injury incidence 14.4/1000 h (11.1-18.4), time-loss 6.4/1000 h; 73% overuse; knee 22% of episodes; shoulder injuries near-absent; previous injury rate ratio 2.60 (1.00-6.75, CI includes no effect). Small, low response rate, exploratory. This is the only injury data and it suggests running-related lower limb, not shoulder, is the main problem.
- Villarroel Lopez 2025, J Funct Morphol Kinesiol, PMID 41133555 [V]: scoping review of HIFT (39 studies, general CrossFit-type), not HYROX-specific.
- Rios 2025 (PMID 41295764) is a monitoring-concept paper; Biazzo 2026 (PMID 42625862) gut microbiota, n=38; Buoite Stella 2026 (PMID 41762273) sleep in 8 athletes: not relevant to training decisions.

### What a lifter needs
- Only sourced statement: faster HYROX finishing times correlate with VO2max and endurance volume (Brandt n=11), and running is ~50-60% of race time (Rappelt, Brandt). The sources do not give a prescription (km/week vs station work) for a non-runner; none exists in what I found. Any plan is extrapolation.

### Station risks for his constraints
- Wall balls, sandbag lunges, burpee broad jumps: NO studies found on injury risk per station [U]. The only data: shoulder injuries near-absent in Chittenden's n=89 (few cases), and wall balls produced the peak HR/lactate/RPE in Brandt (n=11). Nothing speaks to a flaring right shoulder. A lighter wall-ball weight (HYROX divisions differ) is a rules fact, not sourced here.

## Bottom line for Darren
1. Your main injury risk is the running, not the stations: novices have ~17.8 RRI/1000 h (Videbaek), HYROX athletes 14.4/1000 h with the knee/lower leg leading (Chittenden). Heavier body mass means higher load per stride and heavier novices do not self-reduce their dose (Bertelsen 2018).
2. Start small. Evidence for obese novices (BMI>30) points to under ~3 km in week 1 (Nielsen 2014, exploratory). You are BMI ~28, so treat 3 km/week as a prudent start, not a proven cut-off.
3. Do not rely on a weekly "+10%" rule: the RCT showed no benefit (Buist 2008). The better-supported rule: never make a single run longer than ~10% beyond your longest run in the last 30 days (Brandt Frandsen 2025, HRR 1.5-2.3 above that). Count the 5-week Taiwan trip as a possible training gap and restart conservatively afterwards (my inference).
4. Treadmill gives lower tibial load than overground (SMD -0.83, Keast), and you already walk on one. No injury-incidence data either way.
5. Keep lifting: strength training improves running economy in trained runners (Balsalobre-Fernandez, Blagrove) and shows injury-prevention signal in general sports (Lauersen, probably overestimated). Not tested in novices.
6. HYROX: runs are ~50-60% of race time, and VO2max/endurance volume track finishing time in the single small study. This supports building running base first. There is no evidence for station-specific risk or a plan for non-runners.
7. Not found / unverified: Fredette 2022, Damsted 2018 progression paper, any Couch-to-5K trial, any station-level injury study.
