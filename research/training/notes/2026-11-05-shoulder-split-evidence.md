## Q1 Fatigue late in session and humeral head position
- Chen 1999, J Shoulder Elbow Surg, PMID 10077797 [V]. Design: pre/post, no control. n=12 healthy men, age 27. AP radiographs at 0/45/90/135 deg scaption before and right after deltoid + rotator cuff fatiguing exercise. Pre: <=0.3 mm total humeral head excursion. Post: 2.5 mm; at 0 deg 1.2 mm more inferior; superior migration at all abduction angles. Tiny n. Moderate-weak (mechanism, healthy, immediate, protocol not a gym session).
- Chopp 2010, JSES, PMID 20598916 [V]. Pre/post radiographs at 0/45/90/135 deg scaption after a simulated job task meant to exhaust the whole cuff. Post-fatigue: cuff could not centralise head, increasing superior translation at higher elevations (n not stated in abstract). Weak-moderate; occupational overhead task.
- Chopp & Dickerson 2012, Hum Mov Sci, PMID 22230714 [V]. 3-D geometric simulation (36 sims) from empirical fatigue data: superior migration narrows subacromial space, scapular reorientation widens it; migration dominant. Model only. Weak.
- Motlagh 2026, J Sports Sci, PMID 41804856 [V]. n=24 powerlifters (12M/12F), ultrasound AHD and supraspinatus occupation ratio, bench press posture, pre-warmup/post-warmup/post-exercise (3x8 at 73.9% 1RM). Abstract reports sex difference (F smaller AHD, higher occupation ratio, p<=0.001); fatigue effect NOT stated in abstract. Not usable for fatigue claim.
- Teyhen 2008: search found nothing; [M] unverified, do not cite.
- Verdict: fatigue -> superior head migration is shown radiographically in n=12 healthy men after exhaustive cuff/deltoid work (immediately after). Plausible, not proven, mechanism for symptoms after several shoulder exercises. No study in lifters measuring symptoms across a session; no trial of exercise order/fatigue vs pain.

## Q2 Posterior shoulder / external rotator / lower trap and pain in weight trainers
- Kolber 2017, J Strength Cond Res, PMID 27390859 [V]. Case-control. n=55 recreational male lifters (>=2 d/wk), 24 with SIS (clinical cluster, +LR 5.0) vs 31 without. SIS group: less IR/ER range (p<=0.016), lower bodyweight-adjusted external rotator and lower trapezius strength (p<=0.02), higher select agonist:antagonist ratios (p<=0.004). Cross-sectional, no causation. Moderate-weak.
- Kolber 2009, JSCR, PMID 19077737 [V]. n=90 men (60 lifters, 30 controls). Lifters: reduced mobility, higher strength ratios (imbalance), p<=0.001. Cross-sectional. Weak-moderate.
- Kolber 2011, JSCR, PMID 21157394 [V]. n=88 women (57 lifters, 31 controls): reduced IR, more posterior tightness, anterior laxity; NO strength-ratio differences. Women, not applicable directly.
- Kolber 2010 review, JSCR, PMID 20508476 [V]. Narrative review: up to 36% of RT injuries at shoulder; mostly retrospective surveys; "paucity" of prospective data. Weak.
- Kolber 2014, JSCR, PMID 24077379 [V]: see Q3 (external rotator strengthening inversely associated with SIS signs).
- Gap: no prospective trial that adding rear-delt / posterior work prevents or reduces shoulder pain in lifters. Studies name external rotators and lower trapezius, not posterior deltoid specifically; rear-delt work is not shown to be the same thing. Escamilla 2009 (Sports Med, PMID 19769415 [V], review) says scapular muscles/cuff fatigue or weakness disrupt scapulohumeral motion; EMG/biomechanics review, mechanism only.
- Verdict: associations only; supports external rotator / lower trap strength more than "rear delt".

## Q3 Lateral raise position and impingement
- Kolber 2014, JSCR, PMID 24077379 [V]. Cross-sectional. n=77 men (154 shoulders): 46 lifters (>=2 d/wk), 31 controls. SIS cluster (painful arc + Hawkins-Kennedy). Lifters more often positive (p<0.001). SIS signs significantly associated (p<=0.004) with lateral deltoid raises and upright rows performed above 90 deg; inversely with external rotator strengthening. Questionnaire exercise exposure, not prospective. Moderate-weak; the only direct lifter study found on raise height.
- Escamilla 2009 (above) [V]: infraspinatus/subscapularis force in scaption 2-3x supraspinatus; middle deltoid force estimate 434 N in abduction. Review; no raise-position imaging verdict.
- Subacromial-space imaging exists (Graichen 1999 AJR 10587151; 1999 Surg Radiol Anat 10370995; Graichen 2001 JSES 11307075; Graichen 2003 MSSE 14652502) [M: titles only, abstracts NOT read]; not read for IR vs ER/empty can conclusions.
- NOT FOUND: any study comparing thumbs-up/ER vs IR ("empty can") lateral raise on subacromial space or symptoms in lifters; no cable vs dumbbell raise pain study. Say no evidence.

## Q4 Exercising with pain
- Smith 2017, Br J Sports Med, PMID 28596288 [V]. SR+MA of RCTs, 9 papers/7 trials, n=385, chronic MSK pain (not shoulder-only, not lifters). Painful vs non-painful exercise: short-term pain SMD -0.27 (-0.54 to -0.05) favouring painful exercise; no difference medium/long-term pain or function/disability at any time. Moderate-quality evidence. Says pain during exercise need not be a barrier in therapy settings; says nothing about acute flare mid-session or heavy overhead pressing.
- Littlewood 2016 Clin Rehabil PMID 26160149 (self-managed loaded exercise for rotator cuff tendinopathy RCT) [M: title only]. No "3/10" threshold source verified; the repo's "<=3/10" is unsupported by anything found here. Silbernagel pain-monitoring is Achilles, not verified.
- Verdict: pain-allowed rehab loading is not harmful long-term in chronic MSK pain trials; no shoulder-specific numeric threshold verified.

## Q5 Time-efficient training
- Iversen 2021, Sports Med, PMID 34125411 [V]. Narrative review. Supersets, drop sets, rest-pause "roughly halve training time vs traditional while maintaining volume"; probably better for hypertrophy than strength; >=4 weekly sets per muscle at 6-15RM; weekly volume matters more than frequency. Weak-moderate (narrative).
- Robbins 2010, JSCR, PMID 20847705 [V]. Acute crossover. n=16 trained men, 3 sets bench pull + bench press, 4RM load, paired (alternating, ~4 min between same exercise) vs traditional (2 min). Volume load per set and session total higher in paired (p<0.05); paired more efficient (VL/time). Upper-body antagonist pairing (pull+press). Tiny n (16 counts as <20).
- Weakley 2017, Eur J Appl Physiol, PMID 28698987 [V]. Randomised crossover, n=14 men (tiny n), traditional vs superset vs tri-set. SS/TRI: greater efficiency, higher lactate, higher CK at 24 h, CMJ still reduced at 24 h; authors: may need extra recovery. Acute only, not hypertrophy.
- Krzysztofik 2019, IJERPH, PMID 31817252 [V]. SR of 30 studies: agonist-antagonist/upper-lower supersets may offer time-efficient advantage; evidence insufficient for volume guidelines.
- Schoenfeld 2016, JSCR, PMID 26605807 [V]. RCT, n=21 resistance-trained men, 8 wk, 1 min vs 3 min rest, 7 exercises x 3 sets, 3x/wk. 1RM squat, bench greater with 3 min; thigh thickness greater with 3 min; triceps trend (p=0.06); no differences in upper-body endurance. Tiny-ish n (21), whole-body; 1-min rest group lost volume.
- Singer 2024, Front Sports Act Living, PMID 39205815 [V]. Bayesian MA, 9 randomised studies, 19 measurements. Small hypertrophic benefit for rest >60 s (arm longer-vs-short 0.13, CrI -0.27 to 0.51; thigh 0.17, -0.13 to 0.43; whole body -0.08); no appreciable difference >90 s. Moderate.
- No supersets-vs-straight-sets long-term hypertrophy RCT found/verified in this pull; shoulder-specific superset safety: no evidence.

## Q6 Splitting weekly volume across 2 sessions
- Schoenfeld 2019, J Sports Sci, PMID 30558493 [V] confirmed. 25 studies; volume-equated no significant difference between frequencies (incl. resistance-trained, upper body); non-volume-equated favours higher frequency modestly. "Strong evidence" frequency doesn't matter when volume equated. Strong.
- Iversen 2021 [V]: weekly volume more important than frequency.
- Per-session per-muscle volume cap: NO verified source found (searches returned nothing relevant). Repo's "<=~11 hard sets per session" has no citation here. Say no evidence.
- Shoulder-specific caveat: no data on whether splitting lowers shoulder symptoms; side-delt twice/week is a preference-level choice per Schoenfeld 2019. Pelland 2025 (PMID 41343037) already in memory [V].
