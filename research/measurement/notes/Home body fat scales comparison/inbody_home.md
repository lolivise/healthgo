## 2. Specs: size, electrodes, frequencies, battery, connectivity, users, metrics

### Takeaway
All three Dial models are **8-point hand-and-foot (handle) scales** using InBody's DSM-BIA. The **H20N is dual-frequency (20 and 100 kHz)**, Bluetooth-only, and shows weight, PBF, SMM, BFM, BMR, BMI and InBody Score. The **H30 adds a third frequency, Wi-Fi sync, 20-user auto-recognition, visceral fat level, waist-hip ratio and a backlit splash-proof LCD**. Both units are about 31-32 × 36 × 6 cm and weigh about 2.5-2.7 kg, with a 10-150 kg capacity. None of the home units shows segmental outputs in the app; segmental graphics need a pro device on LookinBody Web.

## 3. Data: export, Apple Health / Health Connect, APIs, BLE, Garmin

### Takeaway
There is **no official consumer export or API**. The InBody app saves results only as an **image**, and I found no CSV or Excel export for home users. **Apple Health sync of body composition is officially supported**, with Lean Body Mass and body-composition writes confirmed in the App Store notes. AU and US shops also claim Samsung Health and Google Health / Health Connect. The **practical Python route** is either (a) Apple Health → your own pipeline, or (b) the **unofficial, reverse-engineered InBody mobile REST API** (`inbody-api-mcp`, MIT, PyPI). Route (b) returns full history, including PBF, SMM, body water, visceral fat and impedance fields. **openScale has no InBody support**, Home Assistant only has a cloud-polling custom integration, and **there is no route into Garmin Connect**.

### Cited Findings
- **InBody app (iOS):** developer InBody Co., Ltd., free with no subscription mentioned, 4.0★ from ~1.9K ratings, v2.9.38 updated within a day of the fetch. Users "can sync steps / body composition data to the Apple Health app". Release notes mention **Lean Body Mass support for body composition data (US and Canada)** and Apple Health for manually entered data. A developer reply says results can be saved **as an image file, not as a PDF**. No data-export feature is described. **[VENDOR]/[UGC]** — [InBody app, App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id884923678)
- The Oceania shop H20 page says results sync with **Apple Health, Google Health and Samsung Health** through the InBody App. **[VENDOR]** — [au.inbodyshop.com H20](https://au.inbodyshop.com/products/inbodydial-h20n)
- InBody USA says the H30 works with Apple Health, Google Fit and Samsung Health, and the app supports Apple Watch and Galaxy Watch. **[VENDOR]** — [InBody USA H30](https://shop.inbodyusa.com/collections/at-home-body-composition-scale/products/dialh30)

### Inferences
- For his Python pipeline (`uv run healthgo …`, which already uses the reverse-engineered `python-garminconnect`), the **InBody cloud API via `inbody-api-mcp`** is the same pattern and the richest source: full fields plus history. It shares the same fragility: unofficial, may break, and needs the registration phone number plus password in 1Password. It is a reasonable fit, but expect occasional breakage.
- Apple Health is the official fallback. It is only useful if he has an iPhone, and getting the data out of Apple Health into Python needs an export app or Shortcut. Whether InBody writes **body fat %** (not just weight and lean mass) to HealthKit is implied by "body composition data" but not explicitly confirmed. Lean Body Mass is called out as US/Canada only, and AU behaviour is unknown.
- To get InBody readings into Garmin Connect, he would have to write them himself: read from the InBody API, then use `python-garminconnect`'s add-body-composition call. No source tested this end to end.
- The H20N's "start the test from the app" requirement is ongoing friction: you open the phone app on every weigh-in. The H30's Wi-Fi sync removes this and is the one practical reason to prefer the H30 for unattended daily use.

### Gaps
- Whether the InBody app's HealthKit / Health Connect write includes **body fat %** in Australia, and which fields exactly.
- Whether `inbody-api-mcp` has been tested with H20N/H30 home-scale scans, as opposed to gym InBody 570/770 scans, and whether the AU region host works.
- No BLE protocol documentation or open-source direct-BLE reader for H20N/H30 was found.
- developers.inbody.com could not be fetched (TLS certificate mismatch), so its eligibility terms are unread.

## 4. Independent validation beyond Siedler 2022

### Takeaway
Siedler et al. remains the anchor. The **H20N had the best consumer accuracy vs a 4C model** (bias −0.1 %BF, TE 3.3 %BF) and was **equivalent for tracking change** (TE of change ~2.1 %BF). One **2026 independent study in 55 adults with obesity** found the H20N agreed excellently with DXA for **%BF (ICC 0.93)** and was good but imprecise for SMM (ICC 0.78, 95 % CI −0.15 to 0.94). I found **no peer-reviewed data on the H30 or H40**, and **no home test-retest precision (CV / MDC)** study for any InBody home model.

### Cited Findings
- **Siedler et al., Br J Nutr (Tinsley lab):** H20N bias −0.1 %BF (95 % CI −0.9 to 0.7), total error **3.3 %BF**, the only consumer device comparable to research-grade BIA. For change over 12-16 weeks (n = 37), H20N bias −0.4 %BF, TE ≈ 2.1 %BF, equivalent within ±1 %. The study was independently funded with devices bought by Texas Tech. The senior author discloses in-kind equipment support from Biospace (InBody), among others. **[INDEP]** — [PMC10404482](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10404482/) (numbers as extracted in the companion note `accuracy_validation_certs.md`)
- **2026, "Assessing body composition for resource-limited settings: agreement of a scalable BIA device compared to DXA and clinical BIA in adults with obesity"**, J Prim Care Community Health 17, online 11 June 2026, DOI 10.1177/21501319261459756. n = 55, age 62.6 ± 7.6 y, 67.3 % female, BMI 37.6 ± 5.7, in a behavioural weight-loss programme. **H20N vs DXA: %BF ICC 0.93 (0.79-0.97)**, SMM ICC 0.78 (−0.15 to 0.94). H20N vs InBody S10: SMM ICC 0.93, %BF ICC 0.81. Authors' conclusion: the H20N "provides clinically useful estimates". The authors also warn that agreement can degrade under uncontrolled hydration, meals or exercise. Only the abstract was seen; bias and limits of agreement were not retrieved. **[INDEP]** — [metatoc listing](https://metatoc.com/papers/189236-assessing-body-composition-for-resource-limited-settings-agreement-of-a-scalable-bia-device-compared-to-dxa-and-clinical-bia-in-adults-with-obesity)
- InBody's "over 98 % agreement with DEXA" and "up to 99 % agreement with professional-grade devices" claims carry no cited study. **[VENDOR]** — [InBody USA H20](https://shop.inbodyusa.com/products/h20)
- InBody pro analyzers (520, S10, 770) systematically **underestimate %BF vs DXA**. For example, a 2016 thesis (n = 50) found DXA %BF significantly higher than the InBody 520 (p < .001) and the S10 (p = .0006). That evidence covers lab models, not the H20N. **[INDEP]** — [NIU thesis](https://huskiecommons.lib.niu.edu/allgraduate-thesesdissertations/6624)

### Inferences
- For a ~90 kg man tracking a cut toward 15-18 % BF, the evidence supports the H20N as the best-validated home device for **trend**, not for exact %BF. A single reading has a ±3 %BF error band and real change error of ~2 %BF, so only multi-week averages under fixed conditions are meaningful. The 2026 obesity-cohort result (older, mostly female, BMI ~38) does not transfer cleanly to him.
- The H30 cannot borrow the H20N's validation outright: the frequency count and probably the algorithm differ.

### Gaps
- No H30 / H40 validation.
- No day-to-day reliability (CV, TEM, MDC) for any InBody home unit.
- No study in lean or athletic men cutting weight.

## 6. Regulatory listings (FDA, TGA, CE)

### Takeaway
The **H20 / H20(B) is FDA 510(k)-cleared** as a Class II body composition analyzer under the shared clearance **K123228** (13 March 2013, with the InBody 770/570/S10). InBody's own pages describe the H20N with the "H20B" FDA wording, and whether the H20N is covered by that clearance is unconfirmed. **No TGA ARTG entry** was found for any InBody home scale. **CE medical status** for the home models is not established.

### Cited Findings
- **FDA K123228:** "INBODY 770, INBODY 570, INBODY S10, INBODY H20/INBODY H20(B)", Biospace Co. Ltd, decision SESE on 2013-03-08, regulation 870.2770 (Analyzer, Body Composition). **[INDEP database mirror]** — [regdatalab K123228](https://www.regdatalab.com/510k/K123228)
- InBody India's H20N page says verbatim: "The H20B has an FDA 510k clearance and is registered as a Class II Medical Device." **[VENDOR]** — [InBody India H20N](https://shop.inbody.in/products/inbody-dial-h20n)
- TGA ARTG: no entry found for H20N, H20B, H30 or H40 in search results. **[searched]**

### Inferences
- In Australia, consumer body-fat scales are generally sold as non-medical goods, so the lack of an ARTG listing is normal and not a red flag.

### Gaps
- Direct confirmation on accessdata.fda.gov that the H20N falls under K123228 (only mirrors were seen).
- No ARTG direct search was run, because the TGA site is not reachable via search snippets.
- No CE / EU MDR status was found for the home models.
