## 2. Prevent list for Darren (derived = inference, ranked by evidence strength x relevance with Hep B)
1. **Do not overshoot the surplus (keep to roughly +5%, gain well under ~0.5% body weight per week).** Every outcome (liver fat, insulin sensitivity, fat mass) scales with surplus size: Sevastianova, Luukkonen, Johannsen, Bray, Garthe, Helms. In trained people extra energy beyond a small surplus bought fat, not muscle (Garthe; Helms). Tinsley's ~0.55%/wk is a ceiling from a supplement-fed preliminary study, not a target. Strength: moderate (several RCTs, all small).
2. **Cap saturated fat; prefer unsaturated fat as the surplus source.** SFA surplus raised liver fat +50-55% vs PUFA +15% or none (Luukkonen 2018, LIPOGAIN-2 2019, both RCT). This is the best-supported macronutrient lever for liver fat. Strength: moderate; overweight participants, 3-8 wk, palm oil vs sunflower oil is an extreme contrast (inference: his actual diet differences will be smaller).
3. **Avoid sugary liquids and sugar in the surplus.** Carbohydrate overfeeding raised liver fat +27-33% via DNL (Sevastianova, Luukkonen). But hypercaloric fructose = glucose for liver fat (23872500) and the sugar effect is "confounded by excess energy" (25099546), so the lever is energy plus liquid form, not sugar per se. His ~35 g/day is fine. Adults gain +0.83 kg when SSBs added (36789935). Strength: moderate for liquid sugar and weight.
4. **Keep training volume and daily walking during the bulk.** The only exercise-protection RCT (Walhin 2013, n=26, aerobic, 7 d) fully blocked the insulin response of overfeeding; the same surplus plus inactivity impaired sensitivity within 3 days (Knudsen 2012). Whether lifting does the same is not shown. Do not let steps collapse (inference). Strength: weak-moderate.
5. **Avoid easy, fast-eaten, energy-dense processed food as the surplus vehicle; slower-eaten whole foods.** Hall 2019 +508 kcal/d, eating-rate trial -369 kcal/d for slow texture. Strength: moderate for intake effect, low for any harm beyond energy. In a controlled bulk the point is control over the size of the surplus.
6. **Avoid heavy drinking in the recovery window; no safe-dose data.** One binge dose cut post-exercise MPS 24-37% (Parr, n=8). Beyond that, alcohol and a treated HBV liver are an independent concern (inference; no paper checked this session on HBV + alcohol). Strength: weak for muscle, rationale for liver is non-bulk-specific.
7. **Grazing all day on surplus calories (inference, weak).** Koopman 2014 (n=36) found extra eating events raised IHTG and larger meals did not. Treat as a hypothesis; one small trial in lean young men.
8. **Do not stretch the bulk long or start with a large surplus then cut.** Direct evidence: Garthe (fat +15% vs +3%). Cost of the later cut is inference.

## 3. Monitoring signals supported by evidence (no waist, no photos)
- **Weekly average body weight** (Garmin weigh-ins, already collected); keep gain rate below the plan ceiling. Supported because fat gain tracks surplus size (Garthe, Bray, Helms). Rate rising above ~0.5%/wk for 2+ weeks means cut intake a step (inference).
- **Bloodwork twice a year: ALT/AST/GGT, fasting glucose (+ insulin/HbA1c if available), lipids (LDL-C, HDL, TG, ideally apoB).** Supported by: liver enzymes and atherogenic lipids rose with SFA surplus (LIPOGAIN-2 2019, LIPOGAIN 2014); GGT and baseline liver fat predicted who accumulates liver fat (33344496); fructose/sucrose raised LDL (Aeberli 2013). Continue entecavir monitoring as his doctor sets.
- **Lift progress** (e1RM from Garmin sets) vs weight gain: if weight rises but lifts do not, the surplus is buying fat (inference from Garthe, where lean gain did not rise with larger surplus).
- Nothing in the evidence supports daily scale-chasing or a specific liver-fat home test; a fatty-liver ultrasound/elastography is a doctor decision (inference).

## 4. Unresolved / no good evidence
- Whether a modest (+5-10%) surplus in a trained, lean Asian man raises liver fat measurably. All liver-fat RCTs used +750-1000 kcal/d in untrained, often overweight people for 3-8 wk. Helms-size surplus effects on liver fat are unmeasured.
- Whether lifting (not aerobic) protects against overfeeding harms.
- HBV plus steatosis outcomes: conflicting (no difference conventionally; "protective" in matched IPD; biopsy-diagnosed FL had far higher 10-y HCC). Cannot say whether adding liver fat on top of treated HBV changes his risk.
- Alcohol dose below binge level and hypertrophy; alcohol with HBV.
- Liquid non-sugar calories, high protein displacing fibre, sodium, large single meals: no usable evidence found.
- Blood lipid trajectory during bulking in lifters (athlete data): none found.
- Ethnic differences (Asian body fat, PNPLA3) in response to overfeeding: Cuthbertson 2017 names ethnicity as a modulator but no specific data read; PNPLA3 genotype did not predict liver fat in 33344496.
- Not retrieved: Samocha-Bonet 2010, Ribeiro 2019, Lê/Tappy, Ma 2015, Chiu 2014 (these were named in the brief but not verified; do not cite from this file).
