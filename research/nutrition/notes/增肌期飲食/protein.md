## 2. Numbers for Darren (derived; inference labelled)

Body weight 80-86 kg during the bulk, FFM ~68-70 kg.

**Total protein (inference from Morton 28698222 breakpoint 1.62 g/kg/d; Tagawa 36057893 plateau 1.5 g/kg/d for strength; ISSN 1.4-2.0; Schoenfeld 2.2 g/kg upper):**
- Floor: 1.6 g/kg = 128-138 g/day.
- Sensible target: 1.8-2.2 g/kg = 144-189 g/day; centre about 1.9-2.0 g/kg = 155-170 g/day.
- His current ~167 g is already 1.94-2.09 g/kg at 80-86 kg. No evidence in this pull that more helps lean mass; Antonio 2015 (3.4 g/kg) showed no extra FFM versus 2.3 g/kg. So he does not need to raise it as the bulk starts; keep it and let carbs and fat carry the added energy.
- Per FFM (inference, 68 kg): 167 g = 2.5 g/kg FFM. No primary study here is expressed per FFM except Helms (deficit only).

**Per meal (inference from Schoenfeld 29497353 review, Areta 23459753 acute, Trommelen 38118410):** about 0.4 g/kg = 32-34 g per meal; anything from 30 to 50 g is within what the data allow. 4 meals of ~40 g = 160 g; 5 meals of ~33 g = 165 g. Three to five feedings; 3 vs 5 made no difference in 40673785 (tiny n).

**Pre-sleep:** 27.5-40 g casein, milk or Greek yogurt before bed is plausible (Snijders 25926415, SR 32811763), but the benefit is confounded with extra total protein; counts toward the daily total, not on top.

**Post-workout:** protein within a few hours either side of training; the NMA (38039960) favoured post-exercise intake, but the effect is small (+0.54 kg FFM, CI 0.10-0.99) and total daily intake matters more (inference).

## 3. Things to avoid / myths

- **Myth: more and more protein builds more muscle.** Evidence: breakpoint ~1.6 (Morton), 1.5 for strength (Tagawa); 3.4 g/kg gave no extra FFM over 2.3 (Antonio 2015, 26500462). Beyond roughly 2.2 g/kg, no benefit seen; costs are kcal and appetite displacement (inference).
- **Myth: extra protein calories stay lean / are not fattening.** Bray 2012: fat gain was the same on 5/15/25% protein in a +40% surplus; calories alone set fat gain. Protein only raised lean mass and REE (22215165). Antonio's "no fat gain" trials had no controlled surplus (self-reported intake, trained, unchanged training) and should not be read as proof that protein cannot be stored as fat.
- **Myth: the body can use only 20-25 g per meal.** Trommelen 2023 (100 g > 25 g, >12 h) and Schoenfeld review argue against a hard ceiling; but the long-term gain from large boluses is unproven, and Areta's 4x20 g beat 2x40 g in MPS (acute, n=8/arm). Neither supports skipping meals. (Mixed; flag as unresolved.)
- **Myth: you must drink a shake within 30 minutes.** Not supported as an effect on mass beyond total intake (Schoenfeld review; NMA effect small). Protein before or after training is fine.
- **Myth: plant protein does not work.** Soy vs animal: no LBM difference (29722584). Overall MA: animal slightly better in under-50s (+0.41 kg, 0.08-0.74; 33670701). Whole-food chicken/eggs/yogurt are fine; whey is not required, just convenient.
- **Kidney scare:** In healthy adults, high protein did not change GFR (30383278: SMD 0.11, -0.05 to 0.27). 1-year crossover in trained men at ~3.3 g/kg showed no kidney marker change (27807480, n=14).

**Darren-specific caution on kidney labs (Hep B, entecavir dosed by eGFR):** (a) a cooked meat meal of ~54 g protein transiently raised serum creatinine and lowered calculated eGFR, gone after 12 h fast (24062331); (b) creatine raises creatinine without changing cystatin C (19083405, patients); (c) high-protein trials mostly show eGFR going up, driven by creatinine-based estimates (42289790). Inference: if a lab is drawn for dosing, draw it fasted, ideally after a few days off chicken-breast-heavy dinners/creatine, and ask the doctor to add cystatin C. This is not a reason to cut protein; it is a reason not to read a single post-meal creatinine as renal decline. Check with the prescribing doctor before any dose decision.

## 4. Unresolved / no good evidence

- No RCT found in this pull that tests protein dose (e.g., 1.2 vs 1.6 vs 2.2 g/kg) inside a controlled +5-10% energy surplus in resistance-trained people. The "need falls in a surplus" idea is plausible (Bray: normal and high protein gave similar lean gain, 2.87 vs 3.18 kg) but is inferred from untrained, non-lifting, short data. Treat 1.6-2.2 g/kg as the best available guess.
- Nunes 2022 dose-response MA and Tagawa's mass analog: Nunes not found/read; unverified.
- Morton's breakpoint CI not shown in the abstract; upper CI could extend toward 2.2 g/kg [U].
- Leucine threshold (ISSN 700-3000 mg) is an expert position, not a tested rule; no chronic RCT checked.
- Whole food vs powder: no head-to-head chronic trial read.
- Long-term (>1 y) kidney outcomes in people with a drug dose tied to eGFR: none found.
- Per-meal dose: acute tracer studies disagree with each other; chronic evidence on distribution is tiny n and mostly non-significant (32321161 P=0.06; 40673785 NS).
- Two items in the table (Nunes) are [U]; first-author names for PMIDs 31794597, 32321161, 30383278 and 33670701 were not displayed in the sources I read.
