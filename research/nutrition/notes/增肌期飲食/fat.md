## Numbers for Darren (derived, inference labelled)

Body weight 82-88 kg. All figures are my arithmetic on the evidence above; the evidence itself gives no tested optimum.

| kcal | 20% fat | 25% fat | 30% fat | 35% fat |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2,700 | 60 g (0.7 g/kg at 86 kg) | 75 g (0.87 g/kg) | 90 g (1.05 g/kg) | 105 g (1.2 g/kg) |
| 3,000 | 67 g (0.78 g/kg) | 83 g (0.97 g/kg) | 100 g (1.16 g/kg) | 117 g (1.36 g/kg) |

- Floor: 0.7 g/kg (about 60 g) stays a judgement. Direct evidence: going from roughly 40% to 18-25% fat lowered T by about 12-15% in men (Hämäläinen, Dorgan, Wang, Whittaker MA SMD -0.38), but the 2025 MA of 11 RCTs found no significant difference. So hormone benefit appears when fat is not cut hard.
- Inference: a target of about 25-30% kcal (0.9-1.1 g/kg, roughly 75-100 g/day) sits above the range where T dropped in the trials and well below what produced harm in overfeeding trials. There is no trial that tests 25 vs 35% in a lean bulk, so this is a compromise: leaving room for carbohydrate (the training fuel) and protein.
- Upper limit for body composition: no evidence found. Practical ceiling is energy: fat is 9 kcal/g, so each +10 g shaves 90 kcal from carbs/protein.
- Type split (inference): SFA at or below 10% kcal (270-300 kcal, 30-33 g at 2,700-3,000 kcal; the 10% figure is a guideline cut-off, not derived from the trials here). Rest as MUFA plus PUFA, roughly equal, with PUFA at about 10% kcal (matches the PUFA level of the favourable arm in PMID 40885400). Your current eggs (2/day) and chicken breast fit; an egg has about 1.5-2 g SFA [U, not verified this session].
- Sources that fit: fatty fish 2-3 times a week as food (omega-3, no supplement need), olive oil and canola/rapeseed oil as main added oils (canola lowers LDL more, PMID 35866510), nuts, avocado, chia. Cap: butter, cream, fatty red meat, cheese, palm oil/coconut oil, lard, processed foods with palm oil.
- Fish oil: already 1.2 g EPA+DHA. Meta-analyses show no meaningful muscle-mass benefit (SMD 0.07, 0.26) and a very small strength effect (SMD 0.12), so more is not supported; reducing soreness after unaccustomed eccentric work is the only signal (g=-0.75). Do not raise the dose for muscle.

## Things to avoid / myths

- "High saturated fat bulk builds muscle/testosterone": the T data come from comparing high-fat vs low-fat total fat, not SFA. The only SFA-specific signal, Volek 1997 (n=12 correlation, r=0.77), is observational. Overfeeding SFA raised liver fat 50-55% relative (PMIDs 31369090, 29844096) and ceramides; it did NOT increase lean mass more (PMID 38636844). For a Hep B patient, a liver-fat rise is the main reason to keep SFA low during the surplus.
- "PUFA overfeeding grows more lean tissue": LIPOGAIN 2014 (n=39) said so; the larger 2024 replication (n=61) found a difference of 0.07 L (-0.21 to 0.35). Do not use it.
- Unsaturated fat is not free: UNSAT overfeeding still raised liver fat 15% and simple sugar 33% (PMID 29844096). The lesson is a small surplus, not "any fat is fine".
- Trans fat: industrial trans linked with CHD RR 1.42 (1.05-1.92) in cohorts only (PMID 26268692). Avoid fried/processed baked goods.
- "SFA is harmless for the heart" (de Souza 2015, all-cause RR 0.99) vs "SFA cuts must help" (Cochrane RR 0.79): cohorts and trials disagree; LDL effect itself is well supported (-15.7 mg/dL when SFA differs >=2% kcal). Cheapest consistent position: keep SFA modest.
- Fish oil as an anabolic supplement: not supported in healthy adults (PMIDs 36811583, 42735611, 27009278).

## Unresolved / no good evidence

- Optimal fat % for a lean-bulking trained man: no trial found. Testosterone data are small, short, surrogate, and mostly men not lifting; none measured lean-mass outcomes.
- Whether the liver-fat effects (3-8 wk, +1000 kcal/day or muffin-based overfeeding) scale to a +200-300 kcal/day surplus: unknown. The pooled LIPOGAIN data show very large individual variation and no link with weight gained; baseline GGT and visceral fat predicted it, so a baseline liver check (ALT/GGT, ultrasound) is worth discussing with his doctor [inference].
- Mensink 2016 WHO meta-analysis: not found/verified. Fantus 2020 NHANES diet-fat study: no such paper found (his 2020 NHANES paper is about exercise). Dorgan/Hämäläinen/Wang are 1984-2005 and use older assays.
- Dose-response of omega-3 for DOMS; effect of >1.2 g EPA+DHA in already-trained lifters: no data.
- Hep B-specific diet-fat trials: not searched in this pull; no evidence relating SFA to HBV outcomes was verified here.
